**Spells** are magical effects that are created by creatures via **spellcasting**. Spells are frequently cast to deal [damage](/wiki/Damage_mechanics "Damage mechanics") and provide [healing](/wiki/Healing "Healing"), or to inflict [conditions](/wiki/Condition "Condition") on their targets.

All [classes](/wiki/Classes "Classes") are capable of casting spells with the help of [scrolls](/wiki/Scrolls "Scrolls") and [magic items](/wiki/Equipment "Equipment"), and classes with the Spellcasting or Pact Magic features – called *spellcasters* – have spell slots they can expend to cast spells they know, without the need for scrolls.

## Overview

Spells are created from the Weave, the magical energies of the multiverse. They are conjured through experience, knowledge, or innate ability, or via the use of scrolls and magical items.

### Spell levels

All spells have a level – a measure of their power and difficulty to cast. To cast a spell, the caster must usually expend a [spell slot](#Spell_slots) of sufficient level. Spells cast from scrolls consume the scroll instead and do *not* consume spell slots when cast, nor do most spells granted by magic items.

Level 0 spells are called *cantrips*, and can usually be cast at will, without expending a spell slot.

### Spellcasting ability

Most classes – except base [fighter](/wiki/Fighter "Fighter") and [rogue](/wiki/Rogue "Rogue") or their subclasses not listed below – have a designated [ability](/wiki/Ability "Ability") known as their *spellcasting ability*, which represents their capacity to cast spells.

When casting a spell, the spellcasting ability used depends on whether the spell was learned (first three points below) or cast from an item.

- Spells learned from taking levels in a class use the spellcasting ability modifier of that class.
- Spells learned by [transcribing scrolls](/wiki/Transcribing_scrolls "Transcribing scrolls") into a wizard's Spellbook count as wizard spells (because learning from scrolls is a wizard class feature), and thus use Intelligence.
- Spells granted from racial features use a spellcasting ability determined by race.

#### Item spellcasting ability

Spells cast from items (including scrolls) and other non-class or race sources, such as [illithid powers](/wiki/Illithid_powers "Illithid powers") and free recasts of [recastable spells](/wiki/Category:Recastable_spells "Category:Recastable spells") (regardless of source), use the spellcasting ability of the class a character most recently took a *first* level in. However, when multiclassing into fighter or rogue (except the Eldritch Knight or Arcane Trickster subclasses), a character's spellcasting ability does not change, as these classes do not have their own.

For example, a character who starts with 5 levels in [sorcerer](/wiki/Sorcerer "Sorcerer") will use Charisma for all of their sorcerer spells, as well as spells granted by equipment (e.g., the [![Fire Bolt](/w/images/thumb/2/23/Fire_Bolt_Icon.webp/20px-Fire_Bolt_Icon.webp)](/wiki/Fire_Bolt "Fire Bolt") [Fire Bolt](/wiki/Fire_Bolt "Fire Bolt") cantrip granted by the [![](/w/images/thumb/6/68/Quarterstaff_PlusOne_Unfaded_Icon.png/20px-Quarterstaff_PlusOne_Unfaded_Icon.png.webp)](/wiki/Gold_Wyrmling_Staff "Gold Wyrmling Staff") [Gold Wyrmling Staff](/wiki/Gold_Wyrmling_Staff "Gold Wyrmling Staff")) and spell scrolls. If this character at level 6 takes their first level in [wizard](/wiki/Wizard "Wizard"), then their wizard spells (including scrolls transcribed into their Spellbook) will use Intelligence, their sorcerer spells will continue to use Charisma, but spells granted from equipment and spell scrolls cast directly will now use Intelligence. If the character at level 7 takes their sixth level in sorcerer, then nothing changes, because sorcerer is not a "new" class on this character. However, if the character at level 8 now takes their first level in [druid](/wiki/Druid "Druid"), then their druid spells use Wisdom, their sorcerer spells continue to use Charisma, their wizard spells continue to use Intelligence, and spells granted by equipment and scrolls cast directly now use Wisdom since druid is the newest class on this character. If the character at level 9 takes their first level in fighter, their spellcasting ability does not change at all.

Although Eldritch Knights and Arcane Tricksters only receive a spellcasting ability at level 3, it still only matters when the first level was taken in fighter or rogue. For example, if a character takes their first level in fighter, and then a level in druid, their spellcasting ability will be Wisdom. Even if they then take two more levels in fighter and choose the Eldritch Knight subclass, their spellcasting ability will remain Wisdom, since they took their first level in druid more recently than in fighter.

## Spell properties

Each spell belongs to a school, which defines its general purpose, and all spells have a range at which they can be cast and a specified target or [area of effect](/wiki/Areas "Areas"). A spell may have an instantaneous effect when cast (for example, a projectile attack), or a spell may have an effect which persists for a specified *duration* (measured in *turns*).

Most spells can be interrupted by [![Counterspell](/w/images/thumb/5/50/Counterspell_Icon.webp/20px-Counterspell_Icon.webp)](/wiki/Counterspell "Counterspell") [Counterspell](/wiki/Counterspell "Counterspell") before they are cast, and most spells cannot be cast by [![Silenced](/w/images/thumb/b/b6/Silenced_Condition_Icon.webp/20px-Silenced_Condition_Icon.webp)](/wiki/Silenced_(Condition) "Silenced (Condition)") [Silenced](/wiki/Silenced_(Condition) "Silenced (Condition)") creatures.

### Schools of magic

Not to be confused with [the wizard School subclasses](/wiki/Wizard "Wizard")

Every spell belongs to one of eight schools of magic: [abjuration](/wiki/Abjuration "Abjuration"), [conjuration](/wiki/Conjuration "Conjuration"), [divination](/wiki/Divination "Divination"), [enchantment](/wiki/Enchantment_(school) "Enchantment (school)"), [evocation](/wiki/Evocation "Evocation"), [illusion](/wiki/Illusion "Illusion"), [necromancy](/wiki/Necromancy "Necromancy"), or [transmutation](/wiki/Transmutation "Transmutation"). Some class features only benefit spells of a specific school.

### Casting time

All spells have a casting time that determines whether it requires the caster to take an ![](/w/images/thumb/f/f2/Action_Icon.png/23px-Action_Icon.png.webp) [Action](/wiki/Actions#Resources "Actions"), a ![](/w/images/thumb/c/c9/Bonus_Action_Icon.png/23px-Bonus_Action_Icon.png.webp) [Bonus Action](/wiki/Actions#Resources "Actions"), or a ![](/w/images/thumb/c/c1/Reaction_Icon.png/23px-Reaction_Icon.png.webp) [Reaction](/wiki/Actions#Reactions "Actions"). Most spells require the caster to take an action.

### Spell saves

Harmful spells which *do not* require an attack roll and target an area or a specific point in space – or affect a target directly – allow targeted creatures to attempt a [saving throw](/wiki/Saving_throw "Saving throw") (save) against the spell, potentially reducing or ignoring the spell's effects.

The [Difficulty Class](/wiki/Difficulty_Class "Difficulty Class") (DC) of these saves – called the *[spell save DC](/wiki/Spell_save_DC "Spell save DC")* – is generally based on the caster's spellcasting DC:

8 + proficiency bonus + spellcasting ability modifier

### Spell attack rolls

Some harmful spells – typically those cast in the form of rays or projectiles – require the caster to make an [attack roll](/wiki/Attack_roll "Attack roll") against the target's [Armour Class](/wiki/Armour_Class "Armour Class") (AC) in order to determine if their spell attack hits or misses.

The caster adds their spellcasting ability modifier, as well as their proficiency bonus, to the results of the attack roll:

### Concentration

Main article: [Concentration](/wiki/Concentration "Concentration")

Most spells with a duration require [![](/w/images/thumb/8/83/Concentration_Icon.png/20px-Concentration_Icon.png.webp)](/wiki/File:Concentration_Icon.png) concentration to maintain them. A caster may only maintain the effect of one concentration spell at a time. If concentration on a spell is broken, the spell and its effects end immediately.

#### Ending concentration

[![](/w/images/thumb/b/b1/Concentration_Example.webp/230px-Concentration_Example.webp)](/wiki/File:Concentration_Example.webp)

Concentration icon in the hotbar. Clicking "X" will end concentration.

Although concentration typically lasts for a spell's full duration, or until another concentration spell is cast, there are multiple ways for concentration to break:

Manually
:   Concentration can be ended by the player at any time via the "X" on the current concentration spell's icon, displayed next to the caster's main portrait.

Taking damage
:   Any time a creature concentrating on a spell takes damage, they make a Constitution saving throw against a DC equal to half the damage taken, or 10, whichever is higher. If the save fails, concentration ends.

Conditions
:   Some conditions – such as [![Downed](/w/images/thumb/6/66/Downed_Condition_Icon.webp/20px-Downed_Condition_Icon.webp)](/wiki/Downed_(Condition) "Downed (Condition)") [Downed](/wiki/Downed_(Condition) "Downed (Condition)") or [![Sleeping](/w/images/thumb/3/35/Sleeping_Condition_Icon.webp/20px-Sleeping_Condition_Icon.webp)](/wiki/Sleeping_(Condition) "Sleeping (Condition)") [Sleeping](/wiki/Sleeping_(Condition) "Sleeping (Condition)") – also end concentration, without allowing a save.

Long rests
:   Taking a [long rest](/wiki/Long_rest "Long rest") ends concentration.

Dismissing companions
:   Sending a companion to camp or otherwise removing them from the active party ends their concentration.

## Spellcasters

Spellcasters can be *full casters* – meaning they can learn up to 6th level spells, *half casters* – meaning they can learn up to 3rd level spells, or *one-third casters* – meaning they can learn up to 2nd level spells. Non-spellcasters do not gain or add spell slots.

### Prepared spells

![Prepared Spells Icon.webp](/w/images/thumb/8/81/Prepared_Spells_Icon.webp/24px-Prepared_Spells_Icon.webp) Some casters – known as *prepared spellcasters* – must *prepare* a smaller selection of their class's spells to cast them. Spells can be prepared at any time except during combat.

Prepared spellcasters include:

- [![Cleric](/w/images/thumb/6/64/Class_Cleric_Hotbar_Icon.png/25px-Class_Cleric_Hotbar_Icon.png.webp)](/wiki/Cleric "Cleric") [Cleric](/wiki/Cleric "Cleric")
- [![Druid](/w/images/thumb/a/ad/Class_Druid_Hotbar_Icon.png/25px-Class_Druid_Hotbar_Icon.png.webp)](/wiki/Druid "Druid") [Druid](/wiki/Druid "Druid")
- [![Paladin](/w/images/thumb/2/2e/Class_Paladin_Hotbar_Icon.png/25px-Class_Paladin_Hotbar_Icon.png.webp)](/wiki/Paladin "Paladin") [Paladin](/wiki/Paladin "Paladin")
- [![Wizard](/w/images/thumb/2/23/Class_Wizard_Hotbar_Icon.png/25px-Class_Wizard_Hotbar_Icon.png.webp)](/wiki/Wizard "Wizard") [Wizard](/wiki/Wizard "Wizard")

The number of prepared spells each class can have at a time is equal to spellcasting ability modifier + class level (minimum of 1).

Additionally, spells granted by [racial features](/wiki/Race "Race") or by certain class features (such as a cleric's [Domain Spells](/wiki/Cleric#Select_a_subclass_(Domain) "Cleric")) are always prepared and do not count towards a class's maximum number of prepared spells.

Some spells with ongoing effects that do not require concentration, such as [![Speak with Animals](/w/images/thumb/2/24/Speak_with_Animals_Icon.webp/20px-Speak_with_Animals_Icon.webp)](/wiki/Speak_with_Animals "Speak with Animals") [Speak with Animals](/wiki/Speak_with_Animals "Speak with Animals") and [![Aid](/w/images/thumb/5/50/Aid_Icon.webp/20px-Aid_Icon.webp)](/wiki/Aid "Aid") [Aid](/wiki/Aid "Aid"), must remain in the caster's prepared spells list after being cast, or their effects will end.

### Spell slots

All spellcasters have spell slots, which they expend to cast their known spells. ![Cantrips Icon.png](/w/images/thumb/f/f9/Cantrips_Icon.png/24px-Cantrips_Icon.png.webp) Cantrips do not expend spell slots and most can be cast at will. Depleted spell slots are generally replenished by taking a [long rest](/wiki/Long_rest "Long rest") ([short rest](/wiki/Short_rest "Short rest") for warlocks), though there are methods of [creating additional slots or replenishing expended ones](/wiki/List_of_sources_of_additional_resources#Spell_slots "List of sources of additional resources").

A spell of a given level requires a spell slot of the same level or higher to be expended to cast it. Many spells will be ![Upcast Icon.png](/w/images/2/2f/Upcast_Icon.png) [upcast](/wiki/Upcasting "Upcasting") when cast with a higher-level spell slot, making them more powerful. Even if it gains no other benefit, a spell always counts as the level of the slot used to cast it (for effects such as [![Counterspell](/w/images/thumb/5/50/Counterspell_Icon.webp/20px-Counterspell_Icon.webp)](/wiki/Counterspell "Counterspell") [Counterspell](/wiki/Counterspell "Counterspell")).

A caster's available Spellcasting spell slots are determined by the caster's *effective spellcaster level* (ESL). A full caster's ESL is exactly their class level, whereas the ESL of half-casters and one-third casters is equal to half or one-third their level, rounded up.

When multiple classes with the Spellcasting feature are chosen for a character, the creature's total ESL is instead the summed fractional spellcaster level of every individual spellcasting class they have taken a level in, rounded down. For example, a character who has taken five levels in [![Eldritch Knight](/w/images/thumb/7/7e/Class_Fighter_Eldritch_Knight_Hotbar_Icon.png/25px-Class_Fighter_Eldritch_Knight_Hotbar_Icon.png.webp)](/wiki/Eldritch_Knight "Eldritch Knight") [Eldritch Knight](/wiki/Eldritch_Knight "Eldritch Knight") and five levels in [![Arcane Trickster](/w/images/thumb/1/12/Class_Rogue_Arcane_Trickster_Hotbar_Icon.png/25px-Class_Rogue_Arcane_Trickster_Hotbar_Icon.png.webp)](/wiki/Arcane_Trickster "Arcane Trickster") [Arcane Trickster](/wiki/Arcane_Trickster "Arcane Trickster") would have an ESL of 3 (53+53, rounded down).

Arcane Tricksters have unique Spell Slots per Spell Level if multiclassing after level 10, with ![](/w/images/thumb/e/ef/Spell_Slot_Icon.png/20px-Spell_Slot_Icon.png.webp) 6 [Level 1 Spell Slots](#Spell_slots) and ![](/w/images/thumb/e/ef/Spell_Slot_Icon.png/20px-Spell_Slot_Icon.png.webp) 2 [Level 2 Spell Slots](#Spell_slots) at ESL of 4 and 5.

#### Pact Magic

Warlocks do not cast spells through the Spellcasting feature, but through their unique Pact Magic feature. Spell slots granted by Pact Magic are always of the highest level the warlock can cast, and are replenished on a short rest. Spells cast using Pact Magic are always upcast at the highest possible level.

To distinguish them from Spellcasting spell slots, Pact Magic spell slots have a unique colour: ![Warlock Spell Slot](/w/images/thumb/6/6c/Warlock_Spell_Slot_Icon.webp/20px-Warlock_Spell_Slot_Icon.webp)

Spell slots gained from Pact Magic are calculated differently from those gained from Spellcasting. As such, levels taken in warlock are ignored when calculating ESL.

#### Shadow Spell Slot

Playing as [Gale](/wiki/Gale "Gale") origin can grant him a ![Level 3 Shadow Spell Slot](/w/images/thumb/9/98/Shadow_Spell_Slot_Icon.png/20px-Shadow_Spell_Slot_Icon.png.webp) [Level 3 Shadow Spell Slot](/wiki/Consumed_Shadow_Weave "Consumed Shadow Weave"), which functions as an additional level three spell slot, separate from regular spell slots. It recharges per long rest.
See [Consumed Shadow Weave](/wiki/Consumed_Shadow_Weave "Consumed Shadow Weave") for more information.

### Scrolls

Spells can be cast from scrolls that are bought, found or looted. Spells cast from scrolls cannot be [upcast](/wiki/Upcasting "Upcasting") (cast with a higher-level spell slot for additional benefits), but they can benefit from [metamagic](/wiki/Metamagic "Metamagic"). Unlike in D&D 5e, scrolls cannot be created. Wizards can learn spells from scrolls (transcribe the spell from a scroll into their spellbook), consuming the scroll in the process. See [transcribing scrolls](/wiki/Transcribing_scrolls "Transcribing scrolls").
