# Gameplay Mechanics

**Baldur's Gate 3** is a turn-based adventure with a wide variety of **gameplay mechanics**. Understanding the core mechanics can help the players navigate the challenging world of Baldurs Gate 3.

This page will give a general overview over the most important aspects, to understand certain nuances, reading linked articles might be required.
Every section has a dedicated wiki page with further information.

## Combat mechanics

The combat mechanics in Baldurs Gate 3 evolve around each character and enemy taking *turns*, based on their initiative roll at the start of combat, reminiscent of Dungeons & Dragons 5th Edition.

### Turn-based System

A *turn* is the most basic unit of time in *Baldur's Gate 3* gameplay. During combat, each creature takes a turn, usually in sequential order. When all creatures have taken their turns, a *round* has passed.

When two or more allied creatures are in close proximity to each other and would have otherwise taken their turn immediately after each other, they can usually take their turns at the same time.

While turns are primarily featured in combat, a turn-based mode is also available outside of combat, and automatically turns on in certain scenarios, such as when certain traps are activated.

Main article: [Turn-based mode](/wiki/Turn-based_mode "Turn-based mode").

#### Turn order

The order in which creatures take their turn (the *turn order*) is decided by rolling for [initiative](/wiki/Initiative "Initiative"), which is a 1d4 + Dex mod roll.

Main article: [Initiative](/wiki/Initiative "Initiative").

Adjacent creatures in the turn order share their turn order position and can act interchangeably. For this matter, creatures who are summoned by the player but not controlled by the player, such as the ghouls from the [Necromancy of Thay](/wiki/Necromancy_of_Thay "Necromancy of Thay"), are not considered to be on the player's side.

:   The maximum group size for a shared turn order position is 10 creatures.

If a creature that is attacked is unaware of their attackers at the start of combat, the first round of combat is declared a *surprise round*, during which only the attackers may take actions.

Main article: [Surprised](/wiki/Surprised_(Condition) "Surprised (Condition)").

#### Action economy

Most creatures are able to take one ![Action Icon.png](/w/images/thumb/f/f2/Action_Icon.png/25px-Action_Icon.png.webp) Action, one ![Bonus Action Icon.png](/w/images/thumb/c/c9/Bonus_Action_Icon.png/25px-Bonus_Action_Icon.png.webp) Bonus Action and one ![Reaction Icon.png](/w/images/thumb/c/c1/Reaction_Icon.png/25px-Reaction_Icon.png.webp) Reaction per turn, but some equipment and class features give additional or free actions.

Main article: [Actions](/wiki/Actions "Actions").

See also: [Attacks](/wiki/Attacks "Attacks").

Actions, bonus actions and reactions are also represented as [resources](/wiki/Resources "Resources") that are replenished at the start of a creature's turn. For example, when a creature takes an action, their action resource is used.

### Dice Rolls

Dice rolls are a central game mechanic to determine the outcome of a variety of situations, such as whether a character will succeed at using a particular skill, or if an attack will land and how much damage it will do.
The base dice are as follows:

- ![](/w/images/thumb/8/86/D4_Force.png/31px-D4_Force.png.webp)  d4
- ![](/w/images/thumb/a/a5/D6_Radiant.png/27px-D6_Radiant.png.webp)  d6
- ![](/w/images/thumb/9/90/D8_Cold.png/26px-D8_Cold.png.webp)  d8
- ![](/w/images/thumb/4/45/D10_Poison.png/29px-D10_Poison.png.webp)  d10
- ![](/w/images/thumb/a/a8/D12_Psychic.png/29px-D12_Psychic.png.webp)  d12
- ![D20.png](/w/images/thumb/1/13/D20.png/27px-D20.png.webp) d20

Additional modifiers can be applied, which are either bonuses which add to the result, or penalties which are subtracted from it.

Main article: [Dice rolls](/wiki/Dice_rolls "Dice rolls").

#### Advantage

[![Advantage](/w/images/thumb/f/fc/Advantage_Icon.png/28px-Advantage_Icon.png.webp)](/wiki/Advantage "Advantage") [Advantage](/wiki/Advantage "Advantage") and [![Disadvantage](/w/images/thumb/b/b9/Disadvantage_Icon.png/27px-Disadvantage_Icon.png.webp)](/wiki/Disadvantage "Disadvantage") [Disadvantage](/wiki/Disadvantage "Disadvantage") only apply to ![D20.png](/w/images/thumb/1/13/D20.png/27px-D20.png.webp) d20 rolls for [Attack Rolls](/wiki/Attack_Roll "Attack Roll"), [Saving Throws](/wiki/Saving_Throw "Saving Throw"), and [Ability Check](/wiki/Ability_Check "Ability Check"), but never to [Damage Roll](/wiki/Damage_Roll "Damage Roll"), greatly affect the outcome of dice rolls.

Main article: [Advantage](/wiki/Advantage "Advantage").

### Attacks

Attacks are attempts by a [creature](/wiki/Creatures "Creatures") at hitting a target, usually with a [weapon](/wiki/Weapon "Weapon") or a [spell](/wiki/Spells "Spells"). The outcome of an attempted attack is determined by making an *attack roll* against the target's [AC](/wiki/Armour_Class "Armour Class"). If successful, the attack may deal [damage](/wiki/Damage "Damage") and / or inflict a [condition](/wiki/Conditions "Conditions").

**Result = ![D20.png](/w/images/thumb/1/13/D20.png/27px-D20.png.webp) d20 + Ability score modifier + Proficiency bonus**

Main article: [Attacks](/wiki/Attacks "Attacks").

#### Critical Hit

Critical hits occur when a creature [rolls a natural 20](/wiki/Dice_roll "Dice roll") on an [attack roll](/wiki/Attack_roll "Attack roll"). While initially a mechanic subject purely to the whims of luck, a number of features, abilities, and items allow the player to manipulate the odds of scoring a critical hit.

Main article: [Critical hit](/wiki/Critical_hit "Critical hit").

## NPCs

Non-player characters are characters not controlled by the player. They have pre-determined behaviours and defined [ability scores](/wiki/Ability_score "Ability score"), [class actions](/wiki/Class_action "Class action"), and inventories.
NPCs can be divided into a number of archetypes that are not necessarily mutually exclusive, such as Traders and Bosses.

Main article: [Non-player characters](/wiki/Non-player_characters "Non-player characters").

## Player Character

When starting the game, the player is prompted to create their own character. The character can be fully customized or be choosen from the [Origin](/wiki/Origin "Origin") characters.

Main article: [Character creation](/wiki/Character_creation "Character creation").

### Spellcasting

While all classes are capable of casting spells, some classes are proficient in spellcasting, and automatically learn spells. These classes may cast these learned spells using *spell slots*.

Every class – including those without the Spellcasting feature – has a designated [ability](/wiki/Ability_scores "Ability scores") known as their *spellcasting ability*

Spells are created from the Weave, the magical energies of the multiverse. They are conjured through experience, knowledge, or innate ability, or via the use of scrolls and magical items.

### Stats

Every creature has a set of basic statistics:

[![](/w/images/thumb/2/25/HP_Icon.png/40px-HP_Icon.png.webp)](/wiki/File:HP_Icon.png)

Hit points (or HP), given in numbers, represent a creature's life force, or an object's structural integrity. They indicate how much damage you or a party member can take before being [Downed](/wiki/Downed_(Condition) "Downed (Condition)"), and how much damage creatures and items can take before being killed or destroyed.

Main article: [Hit points](/wiki/Hit_points "Hit points").

[![](/w/images/thumb/a/a3/16_Armor_Class_Icon.png/40px-16_Armor_Class_Icon.png.webp)](/wiki/File:16_Armor_Class_Icon.png)

Armour Class (AC) is a measurement of how difficult a creature is to be hit by an attack. AC can be increased by equipping [armour](/wiki/Armour "Armour") and [shields](/wiki/Shields "Shields"), by selecting certain [feats](/wiki/Feats "Feats") when leveling up, or utilizing certain [spells](/wiki/Spells "Spells").

Main article: [Armour Class](/wiki/Armour_Class "Armour Class").

[![](/w/images/thumb/4/4b/Creature_Size_Icon.png/40px-Creature_Size_Icon.png.webp)](/wiki/File:Creature_Size_Icon.png)

A [creature's](/wiki/Creatures "Creatures") size indicates how large it is, roughly communicating other parameters like the model's dimensions/how much space it physically occupies. The weight of a creature affects what can [Throw](/wiki/Throw "Throw") or [Shove](/wiki/Shove "Shove") it.

Main article: [Creature size](/wiki/Creature_size "Creature size").

[![](/w/images/thumb/8/81/Fast_Movement_Icon.png/40px-Fast_Movement_Icon.png.webp)](/wiki/File:Fast_Movement_Icon.png)

Movement speed determines how far a creature can move on its turn, on a 1:1 ratio. A creature's [race](/wiki/Race "Race") determines whether their default movement speed is *slow*, *normal* or *fast*:

- Slow: ![7.5 m (25 ft)](/w/images/thumb/0/08/Range_Icon.png/25px-Range_Icon.png.webp)⁠7.5 m (25 ft)
- Normal: ![9 m (30 ft)](/w/images/thumb/0/08/Range_Icon.png/25px-Range_Icon.png.webp)⁠9 m (30 ft)
- Fast: ![10.5 m (35 ft)](/w/images/thumb/0/08/Range_Icon.png/25px-Range_Icon.png.webp)⁠10.5 m (35 ft)

Main article: [Movement Speed](/wiki/Resources#Movement_speed "Resources").

### Conditions

Conditions are changes in a character's status, both beneficial and harmful. They can be inflicted by certain [Spells](/wiki/Spells "Spells"), class features, [surfaces](/wiki/Surface "Surface") and other environmental hazards. Many conditions are temporary, and some can also be removed with the right spell or potion.

Main article: [Conditions](/wiki/Conditions "Conditions").
