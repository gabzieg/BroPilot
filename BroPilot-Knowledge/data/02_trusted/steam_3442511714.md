Do you like valuable loot? If you want more of it, this is a must, because it's everywhere if you know how to look for it.  
  
It's simple: hold the Alt key to reveal nice big labels on almost all the more interesting stuff out there. Yes, almost.  
  
This could be you! Read all about it right here.

## Difficult Terrain: Do The Math Yourself

Don't trust the line.  
  
In TB Mode and combat or while holding shift, a line will follow your mouse. This line represents your movement speed. In TB Mode and combat, the line eventually turns red, representing the point at which your movement runs out for your turn. We'll call this line your **projected move**, and the portion before the red your **projected movement**.  
  
Sometimes, though, a portion of your projected move might be yellow. This means you'll be crossing difficult terrain. My recommendation is to avoid the stuff at all costs. If you see a yellow or red bit in your projected move during combat before where it would normally turn red, consider a change of course ASAP!  
  
The worst deception inherent in the projected move is that if you start your turn on normal terrain, your projected movement will be its full value regardless of difficult terrain along the way. This makes crossing difficult terrain seem much less bad than it is. But alas, you will be fooled and a Mud Mephit will explode on you!  
[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143167567/BFD11F8C6388D4908727765ECA140340027F8965/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143167567/BFD11F8C6388D4908727765ECA140340027F8965/)Note: the dotted portion of the projected move has no bearing on where the character stopped, but instead indicates light level for use in hiding.  
  
In more detail, the shortcoming of the projected move line is that the game only generates projected movement based on the current status of the character, and does not update the projection until after you make a move. If you are just outside of difficult terrain, it'll project your full movement. If during your turn you take a little step into the difficult terrain, it'll then project your halved movement remaining. This is no less deceptive because it will show halved movement even if you were to return to normal terrain over the course of a move. Instead of running out of movement, you'll have more than you thought you'd have, which for a careful planner can be just as annoying as running out sooner than you thought, as the game is still lying to you about what will be possible.  
[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143251986/366675D29FD91DFE792EAC672C19F27FA96E013E/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143251986/366675D29FD91DFE792EAC672C19F27FA96E013E/)  
If you can't avoid a difficult terrain, jump your way into it. Jumping from inside difficult terrain will cost double movement.  
  
Additionally, if you find yourself standing on a surface that will entangle you, harm you, or cause you to fall prone, you should definitely jump out of it to avoid any such effects. You'll see AI in the game do this frequently. If you jump or fly, landing in difficult terrain will cause you to roll saving throws against any additional negative effects immediately. It's still worth it to jump your way into it if you can't avoid it—you're just making the best of a bad situation at that point.  
  
So yes, do the math in your head. Movement works as it should in the end, even if the projection is a lie.

## You Have Telekinesis

During combat and turn-based mode, time, and as a consequence how much you're allowed to move, are at a premium, and trying to pick up that grenade that just got lobbed at you before it's too late to disarm it spends far more of them than you can spare.  
  
At times like these, you must open your mind to your latent psychic powers (no, not from a brain worm) to bring those items to you without moving so much as a finger. I'm talking about **clicking and dragging objects!**  
[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143030099/E00054ABA901B6F5AA078FDA0941E77310C63367/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143030099/E00054ABA901B6F5AA078FDA0941E77310C63367/)  
The range of your telekinesis is about 4m or 12ft, but the distance you can move something depends on the weight of the object and the Strength score of the character you are controlling. Where you place the object does not matter, as long as there's space for it and the trajectory is not obstructed. This means that if you can move an object 3m, and it is 4m away, you could place it 7m away from you. Additionally, as long as you are able to move an item at all, you can move it as many times as you need. The real limit having low Strength imposes on moving items is being able to place an object higher up. Without a slope or steps to work with, you simply won't be able to, even if it would be a very small horizontal distance to move.  
  
Important notes inspired by various infuriating learning experiences:  

* If you hold Alt to reveal name tags for objects, you can click and drag an item or pick it up by its name tag.
* You should release the Alt key after you start dragging something because it may switch targets to the next name tag you mouse over.
* The range at which you can actually pick up an object is represented by a white circle around the feet of your character only shown while holding Shift, during turn-based mode, and in combat.  
  [![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143109755/676244B1D3D07248B9C811383D18A83FE0404801/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143109755/676244B1D3D07248B9C811383D18A83FE0404801/)
* For easy results, first move all the objects you can reach into a pile on the floor. Then, move the character into the middle of the pile (this is easier said than done—normally clicking a pile of objects would make the character move only as far as it needs to to pick up one of the objects. To force movement instead of talking to another character or otherwise interacting with the world, use Shift+Click). Then pick up all the items without expending any further movement.
* If you're in TB Mode or combat and end up holding down click on the floor instead of an object you wanted to move, release click over your hotbar to prevent any unwanted movement.
* You *can* try to move items straight into the white circle, but the game prevents you from dragging items on top of your character, leaving only the very edge of your ring unobstructed. That's why it's easier to move your character into the midst of a pile than to drag items straight to your character.

## Loot Good, Guards Bad

Pictured: Me when my tempers are rising because it's clear not everything I find is at my disposal  
[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143004049/20C58F1DFD59D30391C2CF5063197600C543B915/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143004049/20C58F1DFD59D30391C2CF5063197600C543B915/)  
When it comes to stealing items, Baldur's Gate 3's stealth system is incomprehensible and unusable.  
[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1794014874421872/0EA4E2A2624570F02D9C0B6B2DFE7CF1A5F6DE52/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1794014874421872/0EA4E2A2624570F02D9C0B6B2DFE7CF1A5F6DE52/)  
If you steal or pickpocket someone, NPCs will immediately become aware of it and immediately interrogate your characters about it, no matter how many other people are around. Your characters are the only ones anyone suspects, and even if the one who did the thieving talks their way out of it, the NPCs will interrogate each of your nearby party members in turn and if one of them fails to talk their way out of it, a fight breaks out.  
  
Pictured here is a hiding character getting spotted by an NPC facing completely away from him with no sightlines. In two seconds, guards materialize and detain the character.  
  
It's simply not possible to avoid detection when committing theft, so don't stick around; always run. To steal items, hide first, then enter Turn-Based Mode, and take what you're going to take. Finally, once you've done your dirty work, a good way to escape is by zooming out on your minimap and finding a waypoint some ways away. Leave Turn-Based Mode and quickly click on the waypoint to teleport to it.  
  
[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1794014874446111/E6A3B6010B2AAAAB918E7305F701769ACA41BC31/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1794014874446111/E6A3B6010B2AAAAB918E7305F701769ACA41BC31/)If you're still not convinced stealth and crime is poorly implemented, pictured here is that same character not being spotted while in the sightlines of a guard having Darkvision, then having to roll stealth against the other guard whose sightlines do not show as covering him. This is due to the fact that when you commit a crime, nearby people may or may not turn to look, and their sightlines may or may not accurately represent that.  
  
Normally, in Turn-Based Mode, you are able to move in turn, then the environment, including all NPCs, move for one turn's length of real time before you move again. However, when you commit a crime in Turn-Based Mode, the normally frozen-in-place NPCs are still able to turn around to look right at you, even if you were hiding as well. They'll be on alert for a few turns' worth of time, as well, so don't come back too quickly.  
  
In short, after you steal, don't try to hide. You need to disappear completely from the area.

## Camera and Trajectory Difficulties

Sometimes, you just can't do the thing.  
  

The Disappearing Act

Trying to be helpful, Baldur's Gate 3 is prepared to make the environment disappear so you can see what you're doing. Unfortunately, this comes at the risk of some unintended frustrating circumstances.  
  
Case in point: This giant willow tree will disappear down to the stump if it is between your camera and the characters. However, this will cause the protruding branch that will block your projectiles to be completely invisible as well, causing confusion and delay that one struggles to tolerate.  
[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143310706/42A5D16D532CC7A3A69CF09ED66226D0E9A67C19/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143310706/42A5D16D532CC7A3A69CF09ED66226D0E9A67C19/)  
The advice to take to heart here is thus: scrutinize before you trust your eyes. You'll need to work around the difficulty the camera creates by moving the camera and turning it around to reveal whatever the problem might be.  
[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143310705/94C93C886EBE4E2C5ABCE171630DEF287BD64CB3/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143310705/94C93C886EBE4E2C5ABCE171630DEF287BD64CB3/)  
Additionally, you will want to make ample use of the Home key (yes, it's a real button on your standard keyboard; no, I am not tech savvy) to return the camera to following your character if it ever decides to take flight and refuse to return to the earth, which it can also do sometimes.  
  

Take The Shot (Psych!)

If you thought that was the extent of the difficulties, think again. This drapery can block your ranged attacks even when it says there is no obstruction.

Can't Fly Through Doors

[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143368339/2766AFC866982A43798C519E795078556F0F40A0/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143368339/2766AFC866982A43798C519E795078556F0F40A0/)One especially common issue you can encounter while using any kind of flight is where you're trying to make your way into a room but no matter where you're aiming it says "Cannot Find Path." My understanding of this issue is that flight is so good that they are limiting it from flying too far past a door. To get through a door with flight, first fly up right in front of the door, fly again just through the threshold, then fly one more time to reach wherever it is past that that you needed to go. Fortunately, most variations of flight allow it to be used as freely as walking, with notable exceptions being Omeluum's Levitate and Storm Sorcerers' Tempestuous Flight.  
  
The reason I think they limit it like this is that in flight, movement used is measured in a straight line from your start to end point no matter what the actual trajectory you take looks like. Your character might fly all the way around a wall to get to the other side of it, and you will only spend movement equal to the width of the wall because your endpoint was the closest point on the other side.  
  

This List of Grievances Could Be So Long

There are many camera- and environment-based quirks throughout the game, all of them unique. You might think you can make a jump to a particular spot and fall to your death instead (Hag's Lair act 3, cage elevator in Cazador's Dungeon). You might throw one of your weapons to try and break something and find it is now irretrievable (I'm looking at you, Wyrm's Rock). There is, unfortunately, no single way to avoid this frustration, so you must eyeball it, engage in trial and error, and maybe not play Honor Mode for a while (unless you welcome frustration, of course). Quicksave (F5) often and don't be ashamed to quick load (F8) when the game screws you over. The only good thing I can say about it is that they were kind enough not to put the quicksave and quick load hotkeys too close to each other for the sake of avoiding confusion.

## How to Not Explode

Exploding is a common occurrence in Faerûn. It can sometimes be avoided!  
  

Traps

When you're outdoors, you're usually able to pan your camera as far as it goes and click wherever you want to go next and not worry about exploding.  
  
This kind of freedom is enjoyable, but don't get too comfy. If you hear the sound of a die rolling, right click anywhere not on the HUD immediately to cancel most actions in progress, and press G to ungroup from your party members; you might have found a trap and be at serious risk of exploding! Sometimes the Perception checks happen very, very close to the traps, so be on your guard, always. Always!  
  
Whatever you do, though, *don't* panic and run back the other direction, unless you are traveling entirely on your own. If you turn around and start moving in another direction, your party members who are grouped with you will automatically take their places behind you to stay out of your way. The problem with that, of course, is directly behind you was a trap, an explosion! You exploded!  
  
Pictured: You, exploding  
[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143664942/48AE23ED05694A802A21765C2987BFBA7772B433/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143664942/48AE23ED05694A802A21765C2987BFBA7772B433/)  
**Don't let this be you!** Right click to stop moving! Don't run away yet! Ungroup (G) first!  
  
Pictured: You, not exploding (good job!)  
[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143883166/C68CD8020CD88AD41DA1992C93000DD1376D3484/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143883166/C68CD8020CD88AD41DA1992C93000DD1376D3484/)  
Explanation:  

* If you failed the Perception check, have any party members with you move in one at a time to roll for it too.
* What kind of check is it? If it's Survival, you're safe. If it's Perception, watch out.
* If everyone fails a Perception check, go back the way you came, but carefully! Stay ungrouped until you're a safe distance back.
* Be aware of your surroundings. Are there objects in plain sight that look like they could explode? Maybe steal those if you can without getting too close to where the trap might be. Are there large, conspicuously round boulders perched high above? Just food for thought.
* From a safe distance, try and Throw (X) some junk in the general direction of the check to see if you can't set it off by chance. Prefer large and heavy objects such as heavy chests to set off or destroy mines or trigger pressure plates.
* If that fails, try to find another route. There are usually many traps in sequence, so trying to blindly jump past a trap is ill-advised. You could end up caught between two unseen tripwires with no sure way forward or back except the exact way you came.  
  [![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143902749/4D475BBFC2ACF5C9BAD0B198350D1AC6735F321A/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143902749/4D475BBFC2ACF5C9BAD0B198350D1AC6735F321A/)
* Sometimes, even if your character doesn't spot it, you can see a trap with your own eyes. Tripwires, for example, are sometimes exposed without need for a Perception check.

  
(This tripwire has been spotted by the characters, but throwing something at it works all the same if you spotted it yourself.)  
  

Bombs and Barrels

Most volatile barrels (save for smokepowder, mercifully,) default to your melee or ranged attack on click, corresponding to your currently held weapon.  
[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143959953/0D530137A5120EB83E38DC2FEC263C9E55D95F6B/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143959953/0D530137A5120EB83E38DC2FEC263C9E55D95F6B/)To avoid something like this happening to you, be sure to always right-click explosive barrels to pick them up instead. **Most explosives cannot explode in your inventory.** But even if you do accidentally destroy a barrel, you could get lucky by not having a flaming weapon that will cause them to explode, and instead merely spill the liquid everywhere. Just be careful you don't then set the more flammable liquids on fire under yourself. Same goes for gas clouds. Some of those can explode too.  
  
[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143974181/68CA69E4F884EE91B455497844710D8237A1FB3C/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1793686143974181/68CA69E4F884EE91B455497844710D8237A1FB3C/)

AI combatants in BG3 will use the environment to their advantage too, including picking up and throwing grenades and setting off explosives with ranged attacks to deal damage to you. This is all the more reason to eliminate them from the equation before the enemy gets the chance to make you explode.

## "What If I'll Need It Later?"

It's a fantasy RPG. You're gonna get a lot of potions.  
  

Potions and Elixirs

[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1794014874259524/598BFAD95BE05C88C5825EFB592715D7A0AADFD7/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1794014874259524/598BFAD95BE05C88C5825EFB592715D7A0AADFD7/)Generally speaking, the items called Elixirs give buffs that last all day long. You may think, when you're starting out, that you don't have that many and might need to conserve them for when you need them, but do not do this.  
  
Consider instead which elixirs you have are most beneficial to which of your characters and use them. Use them after every long rest! You'll either be better off for it or won't live to regret it. You do not want your backpack looking like this when you end up getting killed. Use those things!  
  
I promise you a hundred times too, you won't need to hold onto your healing potions. Use them in battle if you have a bonus action to spare. Throw (X) them at party members to heal them at range, and at closely packed groups of party members to heal them all simultaneously—it's full efficacy for each!  
  
I beg you, implore you as a long-time player of this and other RPGs, use all sorts of potions whenever you get an excuse. At least use potions of speed for every fight that looks even sort of serious. You'll feel like a real fool when you're three rounds deep and you're debating whether to use your bonus action to clutch it with a speed potion or drink a healing potion to maybe survive another hit. Yes, you get lethargic for a turn afterward, but if you can kill several enemies before then, and while hastened you probably will, you'll find yourself coming out of fights way less worse for wear than you would otherwise.  
  
What you don't want is to finish the game nearly encumbered by your consumables, effectively having wasted the effort to collect them. No regrets!  
  

Food

[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1794014874286270/F4E8E26764FF148A36B0C0233F2B4C88015DBE12/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1794014874286270/F4E8E26764FF148A36B0C0233F2B4C88015DBE12/)  
If you are doing any looting at all, food will be one of the things highlighted by holding the Alt key. Yes, it costs 40 or 80 camp supplies to do a full rest, but you know, just maybe, if the scroll bar for your camp supply selection menu is getting to be similarly sized to or smaller than the game's cursor, you can spare yourself a long rest.  
  
You might tell yourself, "my cleric is out of spell slots but my bard hasn't used any of theirs, we can keep going before a long rest!" or "My warlock is still good to go after this short rest even if my wizard is completely tapped."  
  
But nay, I say, nay! What are you saving all the food for? The vast majority of it isn't worth very much at all to sell. Food is for eating, my friend, and having 1000 camp supplies won't help you when you could really use a Spirit Guardians or Lightning Bolt but can't because you thought you could swing it, and you end up paying the price in Scrolls of Revivify and 200 gold payments to Withers.  
  
Hit points are cheap. Spell slots save lives.  
  

Just Use The Stuff

Whether it's spell scrolls, abilities that recharge every short rest, thieves tools and trap disarm kits, weapon coatings (those things last ten turns—combat is almost never that long, so use them preemptively!), or specialty arrows, you should put from your mind whether you will need to save it for when you really need it and instead think about whether it will be useful at all right this moment, and just use it! The game gives you way more than you need, so to enjoy it to the fullest, be a little "wasteful."

## The BG3 Action Economy

There's no wrong way to approach combat in BG3, but there's kind of a right way.  
  

Take The Alert Feat

In Baldur's Gate 3, every character that joins combat must roll a four-sided die (plus Dex mod) to determine the order in which each combatant acts. If multiple allied combatants are adjacent to one another in the turn order, they may act freely between one another, allowing for more synergy in actions, such as a spellcaster weakening an enemy before a melee attacker moves in to deal damage.  
  
The Alert feat adds five to initiative, which is the equivalent of having a +5 Dex mod, which would normally require a whopping 20 Dexterity, the maximum reachable by Ability Score Improvement. Suffice it to say, the Alert feat puts your character into a higher tax bracket when it comes to initiative. And in a turn-based combat system, initiative is everything.  
  
Let's simplify combat to the extreme to highlight the importance of initiative. Imagine that all the combatants have 3 hit points, and attacks always hit and always deal one damage, and no one has a Dex mod. It's an even fight, four against four. If your whole party has the Alert feat, one of the enemies is dead before they can do anything at all. This in principle is why the Alert feat is overpowered. Nine times out of ten, you will be at a major advantage, because nine times out of ten your enemy doesn't have 20 Dex or the Alert feat.  
  

Strike Before You're Seen

If you thought Alert was busted, it's only worse when you fish for surprise rounds whenever possible. Without going into too much detail, you can get a surprise round by approaching undetected, usually by having your whole group Hide (Shift+C), and then making the first attack before the enemy detects you. This will, usually, initiate a Surprise round in which all of the enemies cannot move or act for a whole round, thus giving you an additional round of combat to act as you please. Note that you can Dash and Jump while remaining hidden, but also that taking any damage, most relevant being fall damage, will break your hiding.  
  
Returning to our extremely simplified hypothetical combat, now two of the enemies are dead before any of them can act, and one of them is one hit away from death. They attack twice, cannot do enough damage to kill one of you, and as combat round 3 starts, you finish off the enemy with no casualties (for anyone following the math, an attack made to initiate combat counts as that character's attack on the surprise round of combat, though in lower difficulties and with Assassin Rogues the character(s) can act again).  
  
Important note learned through numerous painful experiences: you want to be certain that combat DOES immediately start after you surprise the enemy. If combat DOES NOT start, time will continue passing as normal, and after six real-time seconds, the Surprised condition will disappear and they cannot be Surprised again. So come out swinging, and don't snipe from too far. For best results, use turn based mode to sneak all the way into the melee before initiating combat.  
  

Everyone Jumps Good but Strong Characters Jump Better

Don't skip this section yet, average-strength characters. Jump (Z) is useful for everyone with a Bonus Action to spare: Jump costs 3m of movement and goes 5m. It goes even further than walking if the elevation decreases on the way (up to a point, of course—eventually you're just falling straight down, but by then you'd take so much fall damage you'd have to have a death wish to have started such a jump).  
  
[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1794014873640766/8A45AD27BC2FDB1B25A30B84596F3B058967695A/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1794014873640766/8A45AD27BC2FDB1B25A30B84596F3B058967695A/)[![](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1794014873640762/8E90BA0E927FD0D95778B041048421A5703997A5/)](https://images.steamusercontent.com/ugc/1794014873640762/8E90BA0E927FD0D95778B041048421A5703997A5/)  
But of course, for stronger characters, it only gets better. For every 2 points above 10 your Str is, you go another meter, so at an exceptional 20 Str, base jump distance would be 10m. In a tactical game like BG3, it goes without saying that being able to move far more than most is a great advantage. Also, same as with dragging items, having greater Str allows you to jump higher, which can't be caught up with in the same way as horizontal movement, and opens up some small portions of the game you'd otherwise need a spell or potion or wild shape (certain creatures have enhanced jumping abilities) to reach.

## I Like BG3

No, really.  
  
After writing this whole guide, part of me feels like I should just stop playing the game if I have so much criticism to provide about it, but it really is my favorite game of all time; I didn't talk about any of the things I love about the game; that's not the purpose of the guide.  
  
I wanted to put this guide out here as a boon to prospective and beginner players, to help the former decide whether they'd enjoy the game and to help the latter enjoy all the good things about the game more fully, knowing a little better to handle what difficulties await them.  
  
I've tried to get a few others into the game, and results are mixed. But that's okay. There's no game everyone will enjoy, so it's for the better if I can help people realize whether or not this game is for them.  
  
If you're a fan of the game reading this, hey cool. I hope you learned something.  
  
If you have your own advice for new players about things that they might not grasp getting into it, let me know and I might add it.  
  
If you haven't bought the game, I hope I could help you a little bit in choosing whether or not to buy it. Also, kudos to you for looking in guides before buying the game. Maybe this should have been a forum post, I don't know. I just wanted to put a bunch of GIFs of explosions in BG3 in a guide.  
  
Have a good day!
