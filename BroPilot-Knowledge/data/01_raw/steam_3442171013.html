Solo Honour Mode: General Rules
In this section, we will list as many general rules about being a Solo Honour Mode Anti-Lactose Machine. We'll cover individual build strategies where needed and then we'll get to the real big walkthrough of the whole game with maps and videos. Basic Rules: Survival 101
Rule 1: Lines of Defence
You'll notice we're stacking as much AC, Saving Throw buffs and Advantage as we possibly can. This is because these numbers are what stop you from taking damage but these numbers are not your only line of defence against dying. In fact, we view them as the last line of defence. Allow us to explain:
  • Your first line of defence is simply not being put in danger in the first place. You never, ever, ever want to find yourself surrounded, for example, whether you have a Misty Step available or not. You have tools that can keep you out of harms reach so use them. Don't put yourself in a position that can cause you to retreat, figuring out how to heal and bail at the same time. Don't make this harder for yourself. Play smart, play strategic. You can't get Held by Auntie Ethel in Act 1 if you're out of range. This goes double for Boss fights and quadruple for Boss fights you can't run from.

  • Your second line of defence is your ability to remove yourself from danger, should you be put into it, or mitigate it if this isn't possible. These are your contingency plans: Invisibility Potions, Hide, Misty Step, Fog Cloud, Darkness Arrows, Hold Person, Arrows of Transposition, using Scratch to bait an Opportunity Attack and other more specfic, situational tools. If you find yourself in danger, immediately get out of it. Have multiple methods of getting out of danger instead of relying on one, if possible. If you can't get out of danger fully, make sure your position is one that mitigates the danger as much as possible e.g. going to roofs to cause Disadvantage on shots against you and forcing Dash out of melee enemies. Remember, killing something is also a method of removing a threat but weigh up the risk if you miss!

  • Your last line of defence is your AC, your Saving Throws and your buffs like Blade Ward & Shield of Thralls. You might well have 26AC, Blade Ward, Resistance, Advantage on Spell Saving Throws and whatever else, but you can still die if you take too many hits. High AC & Saving Throw bonuses do not mean you are invincible or that you should play differently or substantially riskier as standard. We would rather force a Dash from an enemy than risk rolling the dice, but if you have to eat an attack, we've done our best to help you mitigate the damage it does to you.

Rule 2: Know Thy Enemy
We don't expect players to have Bachelors Degrees in Baldur's Gate and know every enemy inside out. We do, however, expect you to use the tools the game gives you.
Whatever your poison (controller or PC) make sure you Inspect whatever it is you're fighting and therefore know how to fight it.

We also cannot stress enough the importance of checking the wiki before an important fight. Too many times we've gone into a fight and forgotten about a mechanic that we then have to deal with on-the-fly. Sometimes we recover, others... not so much. Information is key and knowledge is power; if you are uncertain about an encounter you should read about it before you engage.

If you've played Honour Mode before you will know that enemies have Legendary Actions; inspecting them reveals what they do. If you aren't familiar with an enemy or a Boss Legendary Action, inspect it and form your strategy around it's weaknesses & resistances. Knowledge is Power!

Rule 3: Threat Priority
There's a lot of stuff that can kill you in this game fairly quickly. You need to prioritise your threats in the order of how fast they can ruin your day. As a general rule of thumb:
  • Things that can cast spells need to die ASAP.
  • Things that can hurt you from a distance need to die after the casters.
  • Things that have no ranged capability can be kited while you kill the things that can hurt you.
Stuff that can cast Hold Person, Paralyse you or Stun you need to die as soon as possible. Getting hit with either of those three effects will usually mean your run is over in 95% of cases and this isn't hyperbole. You can genuinely go from dominating a situation to the Nautiloid because of these three effects. If you cannot remove them from the fight within 2 turns you either need to stay out of range so they can't do anything to you or use something that stops them from being a threat. Your own Hold Person, Paralysis, Stun or even sometimes Sleep is enough to allow you to take them out risk-free.

Rule 4: Form a Plan
All of the encounters in this game are static encounters, which means they don't change locations or enemy types. This allows you to formulate a strategy before you even get into combat. The group of Gnolls that the Hyena runs to for back-up, for example, always wander between the bodies they're chewing on and will eventually group very close together. This looks like a perfect opportunity for Special Arrows that have AoE, Smokepowder Bombs or even a Spike Growth if you chose to take it. As for the Newborn Gnolls, all of that blood can be converted to Ice Surface, which causes them to go a'slippin' and a'slidin' rather than bombing towards you intent on giving you a toothy smooch. Formulate a plan, execute it. Simple. Isn't it...?

Rule 5: Adapt
The 5th Rule of Survival is understanding that rules 3 & 4 are, as a certain pirate film suggests, more guidelines than rules. We stand by "Don't put yourself in danger in the first place" & "Know your Enemy" and we will die on those hills, but when it comes to Threat Priority & Planning... a famous dude once said "No plan survives contact with the enemy" and there's a reason that dude's famous because he was 100% correct.
Your assessment of threats must be dynamic and you should re-assess every turn. Yes, casters can be scary, but you really don't want to try drop a caster when you have things in melee range. You must deal with the most pressing threat that turn in a way that puts you in the least amount of danger.
Sometimes your plans go perfectly thus you have shockingly clean and controlled fights. Other times it goes west instantly. We suggest having an over-arching plan for the whole fight and then planning your moves at a turn-based level, sometimes even on an action-based level. Usually you should shoot something then, once you see the results of that shot, decide whether to press the attack or start kiting. You must be adaptable in order to survive. Think on your feet and use all the tools you have at your disposal. Judge us not on the mistakes we make, but on how we get ourselves out of them.

Rule 6: Keep Your Head on a Swivel
The environment in Baldur's Gate is just as deadly as the enemies you encounter. Take a look at your surroundings and be suspicious of a failed Perception check randomly appearing: that's an ambush. Traps and Mimics are a thing: in Act 3 especially there are a few traps that can do catastrophic damage whether they're saved or not, as well as some that simply kill you outright.

Additionally, be mindful of your positioning in combat. If you're near a cliff, river or chasm don't end your turn within shoving distance of them. When traversing the high ground in the Shattered Sanctum, be mindful of Priestess Gut with her bootleg Thorn Whip and the gobbos with Void Bulbs; these will pull you rather than push. If you engage the Duergar from the moving platform in the Grymforge, be careful not to get clipped with an Arrow of Roaring Thunder. Feather Fall can help you somewhat but if you land in lava... game over.
Solo Honour Mode: General Tips and Tricks
So you've heard us talking about staying out of danger and learning to play the game. Well, what do we mean by that?

Kiting
The first thing you can try is using Enhanced Leap before a combat. You can jump far, especially if you have high Strength/are using Strength Elixirs. Jump over enemies onto terrain or jump away and force a Dash. Use a Featherfall to lead enemies up cliffs then jump back to the bottom and keep forcing those Dashes.
This is Kiting, and if you are unfamiliar with the term it means leading enemies on a goose-chase in order to stop them from hitting you. It is exceptionally effective. Please watch our run-throughs to see how we use this. If it looks like we run away a lot, it's because you kind of have to. Fight on your terms.

Breaking Lines of Sight
Breaking Lines of Sight (Los) is another incredibly effective tool, especially to stop those scary casters and ranged attackers. They cannot hurt what they cannot see. Try your best to always remove LoS for enemies. Force that Dash! An enemy that has used an Action to Dash will, especially in the early portions of the game, be unable to do anything nasty to you that turn, and thus you have effectively rendered them useless. Some enemies will buff themselves or their homies if they can't see you, so be aware of this. Goblin Devouts are Clerics; they'll try to Bless or Heal if they can't get to you. All this means is they've painted a target on their own backs in the subsequent turns because we don't want things being Blessed unless it's us. Not Us the Intellect Devourer, us as in... nevermind...

Use the Terrain
Use roofs and cliffs to break LoS and gain Advantage on your shots. Funnel enemies into corridors/doorways to bunch them up for AoE Arrows or Smokepowder Bombs. Make sure you're fighting things one at a time or are at least able to consistently kill things each turn so only one thing eventually makes it to you. Use Doors to filter things into the room at a manageable pace if you can lock them, or at least use them to break LoS, too.

Buffs and Abilities
Longstrider will give you more movement distance, which is very helpful. Haste Helm gives you Momentum for 3 turns in combat, Boots of Speed give you Click Heels which is basically Cunning Action: Dash; all of these things will help you kite and break LoS. It's why we recommend them so much. Constantly having Bless improves your To Hit chance, Poisons add more damage or debilitating effects to your attacks, Shield of Thralls gives you Temp HP & a melee Stun, the list goes on. Do not forget to apply buffs where appropriate and consider each encounter carefully to decide what you want to use.

Items
You can't afford to be using Mirror Image scrolls every fight, but equally if they're sitting in your inventory you might as well use them. Do not forget about Scrolls, Bombs, water bottles, Lump's Warhorn, the Spectator Jar, Special Arrows; the list is long and inclusive. As we say, do not be scared to use your items but don't waste them; do not apply poisons if you're wearing Broodmother's Revenge, for example.

They're Everywhere!
This is kinda obvious but it needs repeating. Don't end your turn surrounded as this can be a death sentence. If you can't escape you are now on a timer, as probability states that something will get through your defences sooner or later.

Start Strong, Finish First
Make sure you cause as much carnage as you possibly can to enemies in the first and second rounds. You do not want to be in extended combats therefore make sure you start strong. Apply Concentration Spells far enough away so as to not aggro whatever you're about to fight, but not far enough away that you waste turns. Apply Poisons to the correct weapon if you need them, pop your small Health Potions to proc Bless, then unleash pain.

Retail Therapy
You will notice in our streams that one of us likes to strip companions of all their clothes. This is not because we're skeeves, it's because those clothes are worth money. If you loot a lot and sell what you don't need, you will have more than enough gold.
Vendors restock after Level Ups, so visit vendors after levelling up and respec'cing. You can abuse respec'cing to buy loads of supplies but remember to leave some back for equipment, and don't do this before you transition into another act. You will meet vendors fairly quickly; some of them will have direct upgrades to your equipment and some of them are spenny.
Having said that, hoarding money is not a good idea. You can't spend it if you're dead and it won't help you once combat starts. Buy combat supplies. You want to prioritise Healing Potions, Elixirs, Special Arrows and useful Scrolls; Hold Person, Feather Fall, Invisibility, Dimension Door, Misty Step, Protection from Good and Evil, the list goes on. You shouldn't need to buy camp supplies on a solo if you're looting correctly, even as a caster.
We usually have a spending spree at the start of the acts to buy our equipment and some supplies, then explore a little and gather more coin before stocking up on potions and such. Hoarding gold being detrimental is only true if you don't have any supplies, so find the balance.

Five Finger Discount
A strategy we found to be effective was to steal supplies and save money for the hard-to-steal stuff and equipment. This isn't foolproof and you must be careful where and how you do this. We recommend the Gloves of Thievery[bg3.wiki] and the Smuggler's Ring[bg3.wiki] as equipment to buff your sticky fingies but remember that if you get caught you're not wearing your full combat set.

Patience Really is a Virtue
Too many times, we have watched people move or use actions when there was no need to. If you have a good position and have killed more enemies than you thought, there's no harm in skipping turns waiting for things to come to you. Don't give up an advantage because you feel the need to do something.
In the same vein, waiting for the right time to start combat can mean the difference between winning and visiting the Nautiloid. Watch some enemies patrolling: figure out when they're grouped together, hopefully near a cliff, for a spicy AoE. Wait for one to walk away on its own and snipe it; that's one less body on the field when the combat starts or you started combat with one enemy dead, the rest are 30m+ away from you and need to Dash at least twice before they reach you. 3 turns of damage real estate, all because you were smart about your timing and have patience.

Divide and Conquer
Like we said just there, waiting for enemies to be alone is a good chance to steal from them and/or kill them without bringing their friends down on your head. You can also separate large areas out into "separate" encounters but take care not to over-extend and aggro too much at once. Learn where it's possible to have fights without bringing in new combatants. If this does happen, and you bite off more than you can chew, assess the situation. If you aren't confident, run. You must fight on your own terms at all times to minimise risks!

Sneaky Sneaky
If you can use Stealth, you should. It allows you to get into optimum positions, steal stuff and most importantly, gives you bonuses to your To Hit chance. If you can't sneak, Invisibility Potions and Scrolls are plentiful... eventually. Use these sparingly and only when you have to if you have limited supplies, as they are a "Get out of the Nautiloid Free" card when things go west. Stealth is a little broken in combat, as some enemies just freeze up and hurl threats at you. We are mindful that we promote playing the game and staying in combat as much as possible but sometimes this is unavoidable; it is just how the AI works. We don't abuse this, but we definitely use it.
Yes, there's a difference.

Comparison is the Thief of Joy, But Right Now it's Necessary
Our final big tip is for when you decide to break free from the shackles of cheese and go out into the game with your own ideas & builds in mind. When deciding what race to pick, builds to use and equipment to take, consider what you lose from not having your previous selection as well as what you'll gain by switching to the new selection. We do this all the way through this guide and talk about certain comparisons in great detail, but we encourage you to think like this yourselves when making decisions.
You'll also (hopefully) notice that our equipment recommendations all seem to steadily increase your survival effectiveness while trying to retain as many of the sick bonuses you already have as possible. This is intentional and it all stems from this thought pattern.
The clearest example we can give is the Boots of Speed: they are amazing but instantly redundant once you hit lvl 7 if you follow our Gloom Stalker guide. Removing them before lvl 7, you have to consider whether whatever you're switching them out for is as good as having Click Heels. After lvl 7? No-brainer, get them Evasive Shoes; everything Click Heels gave you is more or less covered by Cunning Action: Dash.
Form your own opinions and think critically about your choices. It will improve your chances dramatically.
Solo Honour Mode: Specific Tips and Tricks
This is a compendium of specific, situational knowledge that we want to get out into the world. Some of this might be known, some of it might be the product of our runs; either way, we want you to know it too. You're welcome!
  • Whether you use controller or PC, you can find and set abilities, powers, items and spells to your bars for quick access. For example, when we say "turn off Sharpshooter", we mean toggle it off using the ability that you get once you take the Feat Sharpshooter. You can do the same for Great Weapon Master so you remove your To Hit penalty when you know a regular hit will kill and/or Markoheshkirs' free spell ability & the Freecast Illithid Power, so it's not always the first spell you cast that's free.

  • You can initiate turn-based mode and Dash before opening the final Nautiloid Door to get extra movement speed, as your actions are refreshed after the cut-scene with Zhalk and the Mindflayer.

  • If you are able to enter stealth using a Bonus Action, consider using it at the end of your turn on a Surprise round so you have a meatier chance to hit/crit on your first shot at the beginning of the next turn.

  • Similar to the above, in any combat you can move out of your enemy's vision cone, pop a Bonus Action Stealth and your next attack against them has a better chance to hit/crit. As long as you're not in the red, this will work and you're hidden. We did say Stealth is kinda broken... but equally, don't do this unless you definitely don't need your Bonus Action to heal or kite.

  • Diluted Oil of Sharpness does not bypass physical resistance, it simply makes your weapon deal magical damage instead. What this means is Sharpness Oil will allow you to deal full physical damage to Malus Thorm, but not Myrkul or the Shadow-Cursed Shambling Mound.

  • Killing a Vendor means they drop the 3 most expensive items in their inventory. If you're planning on killing a vendor, organise their inventory by Price to figure out what you want to buy and what you can save money on. You can also sell the Runepowder Barrel to them for 1k gold then kill them to get it back, if they don't have 3 items that cost above 1k gold.

  • There are 16 (we think) pieces of Dwarf Meat in the Goblin Camp Exterior. They are a free heal for Durge characters and that heal is fairly nice when paired with certain items. You can use these in a pinch to restore health and keep your actions or you can use them in certain fights to keep your Bless up (if you're using The Whispering Promise, which you should be; more on that later) and also keep your actions. These are really good, don't waste them. We genuinely recommend saving them until Myrkul onwards.

  • You can find Berry Bushes in Act 1 that have Raspberries on them. They are Camp Supplies but they can also be consumed to restore health... or proc Bless. They don't cost an action to use either. Dwarf Meat for non-Durge characters! Please be mindful though, the game loves to use these as supplies when you auto-select so to be safe, you probably want to manually assign your supplies until you've used them up. There are bushes near Scratch and the path leading to the second encounter with Ethel (with Mayrina's brothers), as well as on the Rosymorn Monastery trail. You probably didn't spot them because they don't show up when you highlight objects, which is kind of trash but we'll allow it. Check out our Gloom Stalker playthrough to find out where these bushes live, as the wiki has surprisingly let us all down on this one.

  • If you talk to Sazza after "rescuing" her, tell her you'll free her. Open her cage but don't talk to her, and kill her instead. You'll get the max XP possible without having to lead her to the Goblin Camp.

  • You can cast Shield of Thralls on Summons. Scratch is now a walking Stun grenade. You're welcome.

  • Hand in the quest to Kill the Duergar for Sovereign Spaw just before you leave for the Gith Creche. Your prize includes the Bliss Spores buff which adds 1d6 to everything. That improves your To Hit by 15% and adds 1d6 to your Zathi'isk check. It also stacks with Bless. You can keep this buff active from the Gith Creche all the way up to the end of the Shar Temple in Act 2, if you are smart with resources, as it disappears on a Long Rest.

How to Approach Encounters
We decided to include this section to give those of you who don't watch the YouTube/Streams some idea about how we approach the game and the order of encounters. We've already given you the spiel about cheese and hopefully from that, you deduced that we try to play the game in the order it is meant to be played without skipping any content.

What this means is we will kill everything that has a Legendary Action in our runs and we are not above slaughtering The Grove, The Myconids and The Creche to hit levels and get our abilities quicker.

What this doesn't mean is we fight absolutely everything or believe fighting absolutely everything is necessary. Drow Disguise to skip the goblin ambush in Moonhaven, then kill gobbos afterwards on your own terms? We recommend it. Completely bypass the fight where you rescue Barcus by also being a Drow? By all means! Can you pass dialogue with Voss before the Mountain Pass? Go right ahead. Persuade the Thorm Siblings to off themselves? Do it!

Our reasoning here is you have to take the risk of going up to the person/thing you may start combat with, standing in front of them with no cover or strategic terrain and potentially fighting them in that situation. That's so risky we honestly applaud the attempt and if you succeed, fair enough, well done you. A few of the more memorable encounters we've had ourselves have been off the back of a failed speech check, not that it's any of our business how you play your game of course as you don't need our permission to dialogue check!

We also highly recommend utilising the BG3 Wiki[bg3.wiki] for specific encounter advice; it doesn't seem smart to write individual encounter guides when this exists but if anybody is struggling with anything specific, let us know in the comments or hit us up on YouTube/Twitch and we'll see if we can un-brick your run.

OK so what level should I be when I hit the next area?
Here's a rough idea of what level you wanna be when you're about to do something. If you aren't at these levels and you follow this guide, you're not completing all the encounters and that is objective fact.

As a rough guide, you should be:
Lvl 3 after leaving The Grove. Make sure to do everything you can, including the big fight with Wyll and Zevlor. We know some of you skip that... wild...
Lvl 5 before attempting to fight Ethel, Spider Matriarch and the three Goblin Leaders; if you want to do the Grove Fight with Minthara, and we recommend you do, you should be Lvl 5 for that too. Leave Voss and his Githyanki.
Lvl 6 after completing everything in the Wilderness map minus the Voss encounter, the Underdark and some of the Grymforge.
Lvl 7 around about the time you reach the Gith Creche.
Lvl 8 some ways into Act 2, usually we ping when we deal with the Cursed Raven encounter.
Lvl 10 any time between the Shar Temple and the Oubliette (Illithid Colony for you casuals), before Myrkul.

These milestones should help you gauge where you're at with regards to the content you need to complete. After Lvl 10, you can approach Act 3 however you like.

Are these milestones up for debate?
If you kill every enemy and pay attention to most quests, you will hit Lvl 10 before Myrkul, and that is all that matters.

The difference, in our experience, between killing Zhalk + Mindflayer on the Nautiloid and bailing immediately is the difference between pinging Lvl 3 right after the first Grove fight and pinging Lvl 3 right after you leave the Grove having spoken to everyone after the fight.
The difference between murder-hobo-ing the Grove (after you kill the Gobbo leaders and the Tiefs bail + Halsin joins you is the best time) and not doing so is hitting Lvl 7 in the Monastery against the Gremishkas or hitting it in the Creche while you maul the Gith.
The difference between slaughtering the Myconid Colony and leaving them alive is pinging Lvl 8 almost immediately after entering Act 2 or hitting up Last Light and cleaning up some less scary encounters first.

You can hit Lvl 11 before Myrkul if you min/max your XP but you don't need to stress yourself out doing that, just play the game with a focus on killing things + doing quests and you will be fine, promise.

Illithid Powers Breakdown
Should we go Illithid?
Yes. Yes you should. It's that simple.

You will want to collect every Mindflayer Parasite you find, and you want to unlock all of the powers as soon as you can. We're not going to list every single one here, only the ones you want to prioritise; due to the fact you are solo, you WILL have enough to unlock every one with parasites left over. In fact, you get so many parasites that you have enough to unlock the Second Tier in Act 3 instantly if you collect all of them in acts 1 & 2.

This will affect your ending of course but you aren't here for the endings, you're here to solo this game like a badass.

Speaking of endings, should you turn into a full Mindflayer in Act 3? Absolutely not as a Martial class, otherwise it's up to you if you're a spellcaster & not a Bladesinger using Shadowblade. We will point out though, Orpheus/Emperor are not full Party Members so you're not breaking any arbitrary rules if you're going for a "true" solo, plus having another body and therefore another turn in the endgame is very helpful but you can make your own choice.

In no particular order:
  • Cull The Weak - Get this ASAP. This kills things that have less HP than the amount of Illithid Powers you have. So if you have 5 Illithid Powers unlocked, anything you hit that survives with 4 or less HP is instantly killed. This gets silly the more powers you get, as it is essentially extra damage in situations where you know you'll bring the enemy under the threshold. We cannot stress enough how important this power is. Once you have unlocked all of them in Tier 1, anything with 15HP or less is dead. After unlocking all of Tier 2, anything with 25HP or less is killed. That's BIG.

  • Shield of Thralls - Gives you 10 Temp HP and a forcefield that, when broken, Stuns all enemies in melee range of you. Really useful for the Temp Health alone, but the Stun can be used both defensively and offensively if you're smart.

  • Favourable Beginnings - Add your proficiency bonus to the first Attack Roll or Ability Check against a target. This is helpful in and out of combat and a nice power to have in general.

  • Luck of the Far Realms - Guaranteed Crit per Long Rest. Please don't waste this if you have Assassin, we totally have never done that before...

  • Charm - Stop something from attacking you as a Reaction. It's nice to stop nasty things hitting you with multi-attacks but your reaction is sometimes better left to Psionic Backlash.

  • Psionic Backlash - Use a Reaction to inflict 1d4 Psychic per spell level on something casting a spell within 30ft. You can use this intelligently to finish off casters without wasting an Action, as if the damage pushes them into CtW thresholds, it will proc and kill them. Does more damage the higher their spell level which is nice. Pay attention to your Reactions and what you want to use them for, don't Charm or Backlash the first thing that hits you!

  • Ability Drain - This is slept on. When you attack an enemy it gets -1 in the skill associated with the attack. So a melee hit is -1 Strength, a ranged hit is -1 Dex and a spell/cantrip is the modifier associated with it (so for Rangers it would be Wisdom, for example). Ability Drain also stacks. The real reason you want this is because the -1 is a Condition. There are certain items and loadouts that require you to inflict a Condition and this basically guarantees you do so with every basic attack. Sweet.

  • Fly - You can Fly without a Scroll, Spell or Potion. This is too good. It's criminal that you only get this in Act 3.

  • Black Hole - Disgustingly good power. Pulls enemies into it like a Void Bulb but also Slows them, which robs them of their Actions. You can also cast another 5 for free before you need to recharge it via Short Rest. You can totally couple this with AoE spells like Hunger of Hadar or Cloudkill to make yourself a tidy little kill zone. This combo is way more effective if you get the Awakened passive from the Zaith'isk. More on that in a moment.

  • Displacer Beast Shape - Cool little Wildshape that can teleport, but mainly you can use it to get a free temp healthbar if you know you're about to take some unavoidable attacks. It's nice but not mandatory, however it has saved us in a pinch a few times.

  • Mind Sanctuary - AoE Haste that lasts for 3 turns and does not make you lethargic. This is buggy so we recommend only popping this when you know you're going to set up shop for the full 3 turns, as walking out of it can rip you off and steal your Action. Be extra careful about what you're doing while you use this, case in point - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=THIxTxazxCc&t=13s 7:10 onwards. Let this be a warning.

  • Freecast - Cast a Spell without it costing a Spell Slot. Way more useful for Not Gloom Stalker, Paladin and Bladesinger builds, but still handy. It's an extra Misty Step at the very least.

  • Psionic Dominance - Free Counterspell (!!) thats guaranteed against lvl 4 Spells and under, otherwise you have to pass a Check based on the level of the spell that was cast. This is really nice and has come in clutch a few times for us.

  • Absorb Intellect - Slept on. This ability drains a targets Intelligence score by -1 per turn and if its INT score drops to 2 it dies. Yeah, it dies. A notably good place to use this is on the Steel Watcher Titan in the Foundry in Act 3, because you can kill it without breaking a sweat using this ability. We would never advocate for such a cheap tactic in one of our challenge runs. You, however, made no such promises... although, if you kill the Titan this way, it won't drop Gontr Mael. Up to you.

  • Mind Blast - This is why Mind Flayers are scary. This is a conical attack that can Stun everything caught in it while dealing Psychic damage. If this hits you in a solo run, the Mind Flayer will usually try to eat your brains directly afterwards and that does silly damage. Run-Ending damage, to be exact. But now you can do it back to them, minus the brain-eating.

  • Illithid Expertise - Expertise in Speech skills. It's worth respeccing and picking different Proficiencies and Expertise once you get this. Proficiency is like the little brother of Expertise, you usually can't have Expertise without Proficiency, but you do not need Proficiency in a Speech stat after getting this, as it makes you Proficient automatically.

About the Zaith'isk
The Zaith'isk can give you the Awakened passive which allows you to cast Illithid Powers using a Bonus Action rather than an Action. This dramatically increases the usefulness and frequency of use for your powers, so you can cast that naughty Black Hole/Cloudkill combo in one turn if you so desire. Please remember your Bonus Action is important for manoeuvring, hiding and healing, so don't get too trigger-happy.
You need to pass an Int check, a Wisdom check and then a check based on your class in the Zaith'isk to get Awakened. Remember, these are Saving Throws so Bless won't help you here, but Resistance will. We would never respec to Druid or Cleric to get the best chance we possibly have to get Awakened. Nope, not us...
Note that you should talk to Stornugoss and enter the Zaith'isk or not talk to her at all and get in it of your own accord. If you talk to her, kill her and then try to use it, it won't let you. We normally execute her and then jump in ourselves.
Gloom Stalker: Origin
Here it is. The infamous Gloom Stalker. We've seen a few guides out there that tell you what to pick, but rarely do they fully cover why you make those decisions and even more rare are the people that tell you how to fully utilise the class and its loadouts. That changes today!
We've listed the Origins in the order of usefulness, in our opinion. The next section will cover Tav races separately, so skip to that if you don't wanna play with an Origin.
Obviously, Origin Characters will require a run to Withers asap or a Respec Mod in order to get them to be Rangers to follow the class guide, unless you go Durge. Speaking of...

The Dark Urge
The Dark Urge compliments this build and play style with the Deathstalker Mantle. We don't believe the Mantle is endgame or Best in Slot (BiS), it just makes fights before lvl 5 easier/less risky. We will dive deeper into the Mantle in the equipment section.
Medicine Proficiency is whatever, Intimidation can come in handy.
Slayer Form is ok but in our opinion should be better, considering what you sacrifice to get it. In case it's not obvious we don't want to lose Last Light, as Dammon has two of our recommended endgame equipment pieces.
Another reason to pick Durge is to have a 1v1 vs Orin in Act 3, making that fight substantially easier. Top tip, Orin's Slayer Form isn't a Wildshape, so when it gets to 0 health she dies; another reason to save Last Light as, if you have the Slayer Form, she doesn't.
Finally, and this is the most important reason to pick Durge: walking around kicking squirrels and carving up Bards is just good fun.

Astarion
Astarion, as a Rogue, can utilise bows better than the others and as such will be equipped to play the way you want to play from the get-go, although his stats will still suck and need a respec.
The main reason you take Astarion is his "Happy" condition after a bite, which gives you +1 to Attack Rolls, Saving Throws and some Ability Checks. He needs to bite things in order to get that sweet +1 though and his bite is a melee attack, so comes with the risks of getting into melee with biteable things. It's worth mentioning that Astarion gets a +1d10 (sweet) Necrotic bonus added to his Weapon and Unarmed Attacks if he Ascends, but you will only get to enjoy this in Act 3. He is also an Elf, so he'll get Perception Proficiency, Darkvision & the Charmed Advantage & Magic Sleep Immunity.

Gale
Gale is Human, but you don't play him for that. He gets a free lvl 3 Spellslot in Act 2 after wrecking the Thorm siblings, gets the Ring of Evasion (use your Reaction to pass a Dex Saving Throw if you fail it) and can get Anointed in Splendour for free if he chooses to kiss and make up with Mystra. Not bad, but genuinely not that great either. The lvl 3 Spellslot will come in handier for certain builds though. More on that later.

Karlach
Karlach, as a Barbarian, is the polar opposite of what we want and her Soul Coins only really suit her when she stays as a Barbarian. We do NOT want to walk around at under 25% HP for the sake of 1d4 Fire Damage. She is a Zariel Tiefling though, so she'll get a Ranged Branding Smite and Thaumaturgy to play with.
Note: It has come to our attention that Karlach's 1d4 Fire Damage is bugged and doesn't require you to be at <25% HP to get, so you do you. Still, playing as Karlach? No thanks.

Lae'zel
Lae'zel is a Githyanki and comes with all of the bonuses they get. She's also starting out as a Fighter. Realistically, she's the same as Shart: unless you want story flavour, just pick a Tav Githyanki.

Shadowheart
Shadowheart is a High Half-Elf (which, fun fact, is why her Firebolt cantrip sucks: it scales off her INT and not her WIS) so you can have some story flavour if you want to pick Half-Elf, but that is genuinely about it.

Wyll
Wyll gives you access to some nice Warlock gear but other than that, also a Human. Snore. You'd probably be better off picking a Tav and grabbing him as a companion (not in your party, just at camp... and not casting buffs on you) to get the gear anyway, if you really want it. Unless you really need Rapier Proficiency for some reason. We don't know what that reason is though...
Gloom Stalker: Durge/Tav Race
For all Durge and Tav lovers, we'll list all of the races in the order we feel they suit the playstyle best. For Tav specifically, we recommend choosing Guild Artisan as your background, but it's up to you.

Elf
Your optimum selection is Wood Elf, purely based on the Stealth Proficiency, Perception Proficiency and the extra movement distance (1.5m). You also have Darkvision as standard, as well as Advantage on Charmed & you can't be magically put to sleep. Perception Proficiency alongside your Wisdom (run ahead to the Stats section for more on this) means you'll have a good chance of finding all those buttons and detecting those Ambushes. You could go High Elf and take Minor Illusion for an easy Grym fight and grouping gribblies up for AoEs, but we don't believe this is worth it over the Wood Elf bonuses.

Drow
Basically a Wood Elf without the Stealth Proficiency and Movement Speed. They are also objectively cooler. Weapon Proficiencies don't matter as we'll be covering all of that with our classes, and a free Faerie Fire (lvl 3) & Darkness (lvl 5) cast can come in handy but it's never going to be game-changing. You also don't have to Shapeshift in Act 1 at all, which is convenient but again, not game-changing.

Half-Elf
Half-Elf is the same as Elf except it doesn't get the Perception Proficiency and instead gets Shield Proficiency. As above, Weapon Proficiencies don't matter as you'll always have a class that covers them for you in your build. We prefer Elf and Drow over Half-Elf but Half-Elf still gets really good bonuses that compliment the build and playstyle.

Githyanki
Next best would be Githyanki due to their Astral Knowledge, Mage Hand and free Enhanced Leap & Misty Step per Long Rest. The Mage Hand in particular can be used as a source of Wet, Haste Spores or just another target on the field that can throw stuff for you, which is pretty valuable when you only have one character. They don't get that extra movement distance or Proficiencies, though. Not having Darkvision doesn't matter as much for Gloom Stalker as you get it as a class feature. Additionally, while there is a lot of equipment that have special effects that only proc when you're a Gith, you can use the Mask of the Shapeshifter[bg3.wiki] or a Disguise Spell/Ability to proc them anyway. Make sure to equip the item after you transform or it won't register your bonus effects properly.

Half-Orc
Half-Orc? With a primarily Ranged class? "Are you mad?" we hear you cry. And to that we say: "Probably". Hear us out.
Half-Orc is a melee race, no doubt about it, but it comes with a sweet ability called Relentless Endurance that improves your survivability drastically. When you die as a Half-Orc, once per Long Rest, you are insta-rezzed with 1 HP. Yeah, you read that right. While this race doesn't compliment the Gloom Stalker playstyle in any way, this ability makes it a serious consideration for any solo run. We recommend playing like you don't have a safety net though, as this drastically increases your odds of never having to use it.
Note: This is more for saving you against deaths outside of combat, as in combat it is likely you simply survive one more hit than you normally would and will die anyway, depending on your turn order. It won't help against chasms or falls either. Still, not getting completely bodied and losing your run to a trap is nice.

Halfling
Halflings come with the Lucky & Brave features, which let you re-roll a 1 at will and give you Advantage on Fear checks, respectively. You can also choose between having Advantage on Stealth Checks or Advantage on Saving Throws against Poison and Resistance to Poison Damage. These are fairly handy features, but the drawback is you move 1.5m less than your long-legged counterparts and again, don't get relevant Proficiencies. Gloom Stalker Build Breakdown

We're aiming for Gloom Stalker 5/Assassin 3/War Cleric 1/Fighter 3. Here's our recommendations on getting there:

Lvl 1: Ranger is the obvious starting class. We suggest you pick Bounty Hunter or Keeper of the Veil for your Natural Enemy until you get your first set of Heavy Armour, then you'll wanna grab Ranger Knight until lvl 9 (your level of War Cleric) which is when you can switch back. We recommend Bounty Hunter for the Investigation Proficiency, which helps you get some environment/speech checks easier, or Keeper of the Veil, as Protection from Evil and Good (PfEaG) is so useful, one a day on Long Rest is more than worth it for the trade-off of having Arcana proficiency (ew). You can pick whatever you like though, those just happen to have some consistent utility. You can take Urban Tracker or Resistance: Fire as your Natural Explorer option, but we like the Sleight of Hand Proficiency.

Lvl 2: Ranger is where the magic happens as you gain spells and a fighting style. Pick Archery, along with Longstrider & Enhanced Leap. Every time you wake up from a Long Rest, cast Longstrider on yourself. It's a Ritual spell, so you can cast it for free outside of combat. Same goes for Enhanced Leap; cast before combat to give yourself a "Get Out of Jail Free" card if it goes west.

Lvl 3: Gloom Stalker is an obvious choice as not only is it what the guide is named after, it gives you access to Dread Ambusher; this is an extra attack you can make in the first round of any combat. It is the same as a normal arrow with an additional 1d8 piercing damage that does not cost an action to use. There's also a melee version for when you need to use it. This is effectively the same as having 3 attacks at lvl 5 on round 1 of combat and one does an extra 1d8.

To help you leave combat and re-enter it to get this attack, you also have Dread Ambusher: Hide. This is effectively the Rogue’s Cunning Action: Hide and it’s probably the action you'll use the most. Use it to make almost all enemies pass their turns while one dashes towards you and looks around cluelessly at your ghost. If you're spotted, combat resumes. If you're not, well, it ends... and you can shoot them again, restarting combat and possibly landing a Surprise round. You also regain your Dread Ambusher attack, as this is the first round of a new combat. We won't tell you how to play the game too much, but needless to say, this is an effective strategy... until you can't leave combat. Learn how to play without relying on this strategy overmuch, but as always, you do you.

You now have superior Darkvision and Umbral Shroud, which is free Invisibility for 10 turns per Long Rest. This sounds awesome. It isn’t. The caveat is you must be obscured before you can pop it which directly clashes with equipment we’ll be using in Act 2. Still, its there if you want to use it. You also now have Disguise as an Always-Available Ritual Spell, so those of you without a Mask of the Shapeshifter[bg3.wiki] can join the Drow/Githyanki party.

Finally, you’ll wanna grab Hunters Mark as your third Lvl 1 spell. You may not use it as much as you should but it’s a good Damage Rider for big enemies (that's to say, it adds 1d6 piercing dmg to your attacks made against that enemy). You can also initiate combat with it, which is helpful. The other option would be Speak With Animals so you can consider all the potions of it as sellable, as well as being able to manage the Scratch encounter easier. Hunters' Mark is the superior pick, in our opinion, and is highly useful throughout Act 1 due to it being able to be recast for free.

Lvl 4: Gloom Stalker is a big jump in power as we will be taking Sharpshooter as our feat. Let’s break this down.

ASI at lvl 4 would put you at 18 Dex and we only have room for 1 feat to optimise your chances at defeating certain bosses. We will get 18 Dex anyway with Auntie Ethel and Sharpshooter is a guaranteed 10 dmg to your shots. To put that into perspective, some enemies in Act 1 have 11 hp. That’s a one-shot kill with any bow or xbow you happen to have equipped. To put it into more perspective, lets say you use an Arrow of Humanoid Slaying on... Inquisitor W'wargaz. That's 20 dmg just from Sharpshooter. You decide to switch to Not Gloom Stalker and utilise Flourishes? Same thing. It also allows you to overcome Damage Thresholds on walls, doors and Whispering Scrying Eyes... Sharpshooter crushes. It is a noticeable increase in your damage.

On the other hand, we do hear you. Yes, -5 to hit is big: that's -25% to your hit chance. We offset this with positioning and attacking from stealth until we can reliably manage our hit chance and keep it high with buffs & items. Your main strategy should consist of opening with Sharpshooter turned on and then judge your hit chance; if it's equal to or more than 70% keep it on, if it is less than 70% turn Sharpshooter off. We recommend starting combat with an Acid Arrow as this lowers the targets AC by -2 when they stand in the puddle, which they will do for a minimum of 1 turn if you Surprised them. This will instantly add 10% to your hit chance. We also say 70% as a guideline only. This is a dice game and 70% will statistically miss 3 times out of every ten shots, so if you want to say 80% or 90% then by all means do so. If you aren't taking some risks with Sharpshooter, you may as well grab the ASI but it is not what we recommend at all, as that 10 dmg really does add up when you build around it.

Lvl 5: Gloom Stalker is when you will begin to believe. We have another big jump in power as we receive our second attack, Misty Step and a Lvl 2 spell.

Having a second Attack now means we can start utilising Enhanced Leap, DA: Hide and lines of sight to stay in fights and get used to fighting without ending combat before an enemy has a chance to move. This is crucial to begin to learn as some encounters you won’t be able to run away from without resetting the boss health. You don’t want to drop Ethel to 10HP, go invis from killing one of her clones and have her regen & reset the entire fight. You really don’t. It is important now to learn how to use positioning to force Dashes out of enemies, breaking Line of Sight (LoS) to stop spellcasters getting spells downrange at you and using Hide to draw enemies out.

Misty Step is a run saver. Seriously. You should NEVER go into a big combat without at least a scroll or a slot to cast one so you can run if you need to. There’s a lot of situations you find yourself in that Misty Step can help with. Don’t forget to use it! If in doubt, Misty Step out.

As for your new spell, Fog Cloud is useful for stealing/pickpocketing if you don’t have a scroll, but it’s up to you. You will discover spellcasting with your class spells is fairly limiting, especially when you begin to feel your arrows start to really hurt people. Plus, those Lvl 2 slots are basically for Misty Step unless you wanna rob someone.

Sneak Attack is a reaction that you will want to set as Always Trigger; this is how you get Sneak Attack to work properly! Using the Action treats it as a Damage Rider, but using the reaction treats it as a Damage Rider As A Source.[bg3.wiki] That page explains that stuff better than we can.

Lvl 8: Gloom Stalker 5 Assassin 3 is BIG. Here, you get the Assassin subclass which gives you Advantage on attack rolls against enemies who haven’t taken a turn yet, you refresh your actions in Round 1 of combat and automatically crit Surprised enemies. The Action Refresh also gives you a sneaky bonus when you initiate combat with an attack: because you attacked on a "separate" turn from the First Round of Combat, you get your Extra Attack from your "previous" turn too! To put this in clearer English, you'll get one extra shot than usual when you start combat with an attack as an Assassin. Whether this is intended or not, we don't know, but it's consistent and we're not about to not use an attack, right?
If you thought you were doing big damage before, wait til you start dropping enemies with 100 HP in 2 shots. It may be tempting to go back to abusing stealth to get that first round carnage... Do not give in. Most things will struggle to survive your first round of combat, you will be able to mop up way more consistently now. You must continue to learn how to play the game, as you will be approaching Myrkul at this level and making it past him confidently and consistently separates the Solo'ers from the Camp Casters.

Lvl 9: Gloom Stalker 5 Assassin 3 War Cleric 1 is also big. We grab War Cleric 1 which gives us Guidance, Thaumaturgy and Resistance, as well as Command, Inflict Wounds, Guiding Bolt and Protection from Evil and Good. We also get to respec and pick a different Favoured Enemy if we have Ranger Knight, as War Cleric gives you your Heavy Armour Proficiency, and we have the option of using Bonus Actions to attack in combat.

Please take care not to waste your Bonus Actions by mistake. You can not only waste it on something that didn’t need to be shot this turn, but it also consumes your Bonus Action. This is no exaggeration: that can get you killed.
If you use all of your Actions, only have a Bonus Action and have a War Priest charge remaining, using that Bonus Action for anything other than an attack will cause the game to consume your Bonus Action but behave as if your normal Action requires a Bonus Action to use. This is important to know because if you drink a Speed Potion in this situation hoping to get an extra Action/attack, this bug softlocks you into being unable to do anything until your next turn, wasting the potion and the Bonus Action. We will explain this another way for clarity: do not drink a Speed Potion if you only have your War Priest attack left available to you. Yes, we have almost lost a run over this. Learn from our mistakes!

Lvl 10: Gloom Stalker 5 Assassin 3 War Cleric 1 Fighter 1 is your first Fighter dip. You wanna pick Defence as your fighting style for the +1 AC. You can take Duelling if you want but you shouldn’t really be hitting anything serious with your melee weapon, even with Cloudy G potions.

Lvl 11: Gloom Stalker 5 Assassin 3 War Cleric 1 Fighter 2 is cool as you get your Action Surge. Same principle as the War Cleric attacks: it’s an extra round you can use to seal the deal on bosses or get yourself out of a bad situation. Also refreshes on a Short Rest which is nice. In an ideal situation you now get: your first attack, your extra attack, your Dread Ambusher attack, Action Surge extra action, Action Surge extra attack, your War Priest attack and, if you popped a Speed Pot/Haste Spores, an extra attack from that action too, for a grand total of 7 attacks in the first round. Only certain bosses will live through that kind of punishment and even fewer will remain a threat beyond the third round, dice willing.

Lvl 12: Gloom Stalker 5 Assassin 3 War Cleric 1 Fighter 3 gives you choices. You can choose Champion to lower your Crit Chance threshold or Battle Master for Tripping Attack, Evasive Manoeuvres and Menacing Attack. Champion is a little underwhelming unless you stack Crit Chance, as there is something to be said for consistent Crits outside of the first round, but in your first round of combat Champion is functionally useless. For Battle Master you don’t have to choose those exact abilities but Evasive Manoeuvres is Advantage on Saving Throws that doesn't cost an Action, Menacing Attack can Fear the target which makes you harder to hit + they can't move and Tripping Attack can put targets with Legendary Actions on their backs, which means no Legendary Action (Yes, you read that right). Why War Cleric and not Fighter 2?
A good point was raised in our Discord so we felt it would be prudent to answer it to everyone.

Our guide recommends War Cleric 1 Fighter 1 over Fighter 2 for your lvl 9 & 10 selections. There is good reasoning behind this but in the spirit of giving everyone choices, we will explain a little about why we recommend this approach.

Fighter 2 will give you Action Surge in time for Myrkul; this is two extra attacks in, if you're smart, the first round of combat which will automatically be Critical Hits. Check our Myrkul-specific guide for more info but to summarise: your first shot is against the Mindflayer to start combat, your second is your Extra Attack from that first shot to kill it, throwing Haste Spores is one Action, free Aylin with Scratch so you can do damage, Extra Haste Action is two Attacks and Dread Ambusher is your third. Using War Cleric, you get a fourth shot but using Action Surge, you get a fifth too.

Why wouldn't we advocate for landing five attacks in the first round versus four? This also adds up to a total of 7 guaranteed Crits on Myrkul rather than 6 with Luck of the Far Realms and Killer's Sweetheart in Round 2, so why wouldn't we recommend this?

We will answer our question with two more questions: what happens if we miss and what happens if we have to eat a Gaze of the Dead (GotD) and our Protection from Evil and Good (PfEaG) drops in Round 2 onwards?

The answer is Action Surge does not help us in these situations at all. If you miss a shot in Round 1 you're on the same footing as if you'd picked War Cleric. If you eat a GotD in Round 2 you must decide whether it is worth casting PfEaG again and losing out on an attack or risking continuing without it. Bear in mind, if you have to eat another GotD and you become Feared, your To Hit Chance takes a massive hit.

War Cleric spreads out your additional attacks over the first 3 rounds, allowing you to cover for a miss or eating a GotD and casting PfEaG again without sacrificing the amount of attacks you can land. Yes, you can still miss if you choose War Cleric (and that does suck) but doing the maths, War Cleric is three extra attacks versus Action Surge's two. We choose to go with the higher amount spread out over the first three rounds rather than two in the first, as we cannot stress enough how important PfEaG is to the success of your fight alongside keeping up the DPS on Myrkul before you need to revert to Necromite control. Finger of Death and Myrkul starting to heal is the beginning of the end; if you start getting to Round 5 of combat and Myrkul isn't almost dead, odds are your run is over.

If you want to take Fighter 2 and put all your eggs in one basket, as it is completely possible to 1 or 2-turn Myrkul with that amount of attacks and crits, go for it. If you want to have contingencies in place and love a bit of risk-management, take War Cleric. War Cleric at lvl 9 also offers you a lot more utility than Fighter 1 for taking on Moonrise and Persuading guards you didn't just steal that thing or kill that dude with Guidance.

For those of you asking what if you need to heal and therefore can't use your Bonus Action for a War Cleric shot, this is literally what the Dwarf Meat and Raspberries are for. With the Periapt of Wound Closure, each piece of Dwarf Meat is 16 HP and each Raspberry is 4 HP. One of each is a Greater Healing Potion and if you've saved your Dwarf Meat and been careful with your berries, you should have more than enough to cover your HP losses. Do not be scared to use them all if you need to as this is the last hurdle before the game opens up and you can stack mad amounts of powerful Scrolls and Potions. If you can manage to save some then that's awesome but to reiterate, do not be afraid to use them all if you need to.

As always, we leave the decision of your build up to you.

Arcane Shots: There's Always a Catch
Looking at the Arcane Shots and the Arcane Arrow selection, there's is some good options (on paper). The first snag is fairly obvious though, unfortunately: the Spell Save DC for their effects is based on INT. To clarify, the Save DC is set by your INT but the target will need to roll different Ability Checks based on what arrow you're using, so it's also not going to capitalise off some enemies having poor INT scores themselves as none of the arrows need an INT save.

Of course, the Warped Headband of Intellect exists, but we're not sure we can advocate that being a mandatory pick when so many better helmets exist (Arcane Acuity, Baldurans, Soul Perception, etc). You'd be sacrificing a lot for some frankly mediocre effects in some cases, and it's still not guaranteed; 17 INT would give you a DC13, which is the same as elemental Special Arrows... which you're probably better of using unless you're exclusively using Seeking Arrow. However, theoretically, we could shift some stats around in the later acts to accommodate INT.

We have INT 10 in the Amulet of Greater Health Stat Spread above which is already a decent start and we do have some points in Charisma and Wisdom to play around with to bump that INT up to 12-14, but we're honestly not sure if it's worth it. You could drop Wisdom and get your INT to 16, but this will lock you in to the Boots of Persistence or the Ring of Free Action. Not a massive deal but you're also then nerfing your Perception Checks, Insight checks and Ambush Discovery, as well as your Spellcasting Modifier. This will also render the Diadem of Arcane Synergy useless, as 1 point of damage isn't worth your whole Helmet slot. These are fairly minor points as far as we can see but still points nonetheless.

The biggest flaw with this theory is that you won't get this til Act 3 and you'll probably need to rush the House of Hope in order to benefit from it as soon as possible, which means you'll have to either wait for the Craterflesh Gloves or do it after you grab them. Again, not major but not a smooth ride.

The reality of the situation is if you want the INT for the effects you're only gonna have the stats in Act 3 to make them viable, but then again you'll be hitting lvl 11 and getting the subclass in Act 3 anyway so they kind of cancel out. This isn't to say the effects won't work at all if you don't spec for INT, but the Save DC will be so low you won't get them very often.

It's Not All Negative
Having said all that, you are still getting some extra damage out of these Arcane Arrows that you wouldn't be getting with Battle Master. Having played around with these a little in one of our testing saves, Arcane Arrows that we found to be the "best" were Seeking Arrow, Shadow or Beguiling Arrow and Enfeebling Arrow or Grasping Arrow.
We'll go through every arrow type below and you can decide what you wanna go for.

Banishing Arrow: Banishes your target for 2 turns after failing a Charisma save. No extra damage and again, you're gonna need INT to reliably proc it. This is mid at best, really.

Beguiling Arrow: 2d6 Psychic and Charms the target after failing a Wisdom save. The Charm might come in handy, especially in a 4-man with a Glamour Bard (although why you'd pick this over Swords Bard is a mystery to us) but the 2d6 is really what you want this for since that's 4d6 in the opening round. Tasty.

Bursting Arrow: 2d6 Force in an AoE around the target. The 2d6 doesn't apply to the target itself. We'd give this one a miss, honestly, as you'd be better served using an Arrow of Many Targets or a Smokepowder Arrow, which is essentially the same thing as this but better.

Enfeebling Arrow: 2d6 Necrotic and Enfeebles the target after failing a Constitution save. This is nice against those pesky melee targets, as Enfeebled means they deal half their weapon damage but again, it's the damage you're after.

Grasping Arrow: 2d6 Poison and reduces Movement Speed while causing 2d6 Slashing when the target moves once per turn after failing a Strength save. It's worth noting that the condition that's applied on a failed STR save then turns into Wisdom to remove the condition, or it ends after 10 turns. Basically, this is single target, diet Spike Growth. Also, Poison is resisted often by enemies. We think this is worth a look, but if you chose Enfeebling over this we wouldn't blame you.

Piercing Arrow: Fire an arrow that hits all targets in a straight line in front of you dealing an extra 1d6 Piercing Damage (or Force if you're using Ne'er Misser) on a failed Dexterity save; those that save against it still take half damage. This one is a bit of a let-down to be honest, for three reasons:
The range is awful. Seriously, it's risky trying to get this to hit multiple targets.
It doesn't add Sharpshooter, Arcane Synergy or Hunter's Mark as it doesn't have an Attack Roll.
It requires INT for the extra damage, so you're more often than not going to see that extra damage being saved. Ability Drain (and Encrusted with Frost if you roll with the Slippy Bow Build) will help you in proc'ing this as it is a Dex save, but don't expect miracles. You're basically nerfing your damage hard and putting yourself at risk to use this. We recommend avoiding it for this build.

Seeking Arrow: 1d6 but it never misses and applies Faerie Fire on a failed Dexterity save. This is probably what you'll spend the majority of your Arcane Charges on, as when you really need something to die, this arrow has got your back. This is, in our opinion, a mandatory pick for Arcane Archer, and we genuinely believe this alone is worth the subclass. The Faerie Fire proc is a bonus, but realistically we used this to finish targets more often than not so it was kind of redundant.

Shadow Arrow: 2d6 Psychic and Blinds the target after a failed Wisdom save. Same schtick as Beguiling Arrow, it's just dependent on what flavour of condition you want to attempt to inflict. Blinding something is nice though, especially for Casters and Ranged users, so if it was us we'd probably pick this over Beguiling Arrow.

Final Thoughts
We'd recommend picking this over Battle Master purely based on the Seeking Arrow if you want extra damage options. Having an extra 2d6 Psychic, Necrotic or Poison on your attacks as a backup is always nice to have although we wouldn't attempt to build around the effects proc'ing unless we specifically spec'ced for INT, which would involve a build overhaul or another build entirely, which we just don't see the point in. We'd say choose Battle Master if you want CC + Evasive Manoeuvres over extra damage, as it is still effective. As always it's up to you.

Keep in mind that this summary is based on the fact you get this subclass with this build at lvl 11/12 depending on your choices, which is where these judgements come from. You'll see a lot more usage from the rest of the arrows in the earlier acts.

There is another build that blows all of this completely out of the water, though, which leads us nicely to...
Gloom Stalker: Bonus Build - The Not Gloom Stalker
Another way, you say?
So, you've hit lvl 12 and are feeling rather mighty with your War Priest Charges and your Action Surge. But what if we told you, there is another way? An objectively stronger way?

Once you hit lvl 12, you can respec into Swords Bard 6/Gloom Stalker 3/Assassin 3 and you will be able to play the same as you have played for the whole game, but now you have Flourishes and Spells to play with. Flourishes like Slashing Flourish, which allows you to shoot twice for the cost of one action and your Defensive Flourish, which gives you +4(!) AC for the rest of the turn. Spells like Hold Person, Glyph of Warding and Hypnotic Pattern. Spells like Misty Step but you can cast it more times, Cantrips like Minor Illusion to group enemies together for a sweet Arrow of Many Targets, as well as very high Charisma which makes speech a breeze now.

Couple this with the Helmet of Arcane Acuity and the Ring of the Mystic Scoundrel (more on this in the equipment section) and you now have a Gloom Stalker that can open with an Acid Arrow, a Slashing Flourish, Dread Ambusher and then cast upcasted Hold Person on everyone with a 95% success chance. Pass your turn, then run to the nearest Held target and shoot them point-blank with an Arrow of Many Targets which guarantees critical hits not only on the target but the rest of the consecutive hits the arrow makes as well. We're doing silly damage here; it only gets sillier when you grab the Bhaalist Armour, which makes targets within 3m vulnerable to Piercing damage... You've spent the game keeping your distance and it's time to get up close, as this equates to roughly 4x damage (Vulnerability doubles your damage and then the Crit rerolls all the damage dice again. Yeah. We know. We know, and it is as good in practice as it is on paper.

Str = 8
Dex = 17(19 w/ Mirror)
Con = 14
Int = 8

We're playing fairly risky with the 10 Wisdom, but the logic is offence is your best defence; target big threats in your initial round of combat. There are also items that can protect you against Hold Person & Paralysis (lookin' at you Dolor) but your Perception has taken a hit. Fortunately, most of the Perception checks in Act 3 aren't mandatory; we still have decent Constitution and we're still using Strength elixirs & stacking Dexterity, so not much has changed.

Why Bard 6? This is when you get your Extra Attack, and lvl 5 means you regain Bardic Inspiration on a Short Rest so you get Flourishes back on a Short Rest. That's basically it.

We call it the "Not Gloom Stalker" because you're now a Bard for the majority of your class levels. This is an option you can take but we highly recommend waiting until lvl 12 to switch due to how jarring the playstyle difference is and what you lose vs what you gain from changing over.

Why didn't you tell me this before now?
Well, because it's not a majority Gloom Stalker build, so it's not technically a Gloom Stalker build. We generally consider a Gloom Stalker build as a Bow build, so we've just figured out the most damaging way to utilise bows; the reality of this is that Gloom Stalker 5 isn't the most damaging way. We'll throw you some examples of when you might want to switch to Swords Bard and then some counterpoints to try and convince you it isn't as fun as it sounds until you hit lvl 12.

Switching to Bard 6 instead of Gloom 5/Rogue 1 will give you your extra attack, sure, and you'll have Flourishes. You've also just lost the ability to wear Heavy Armour, use Shields if you didn't pick Half-Elf, you have lost your +4 to Initiative, your Bonus Action Hide, you no longer have your Dread Ambusher attack, you don't have Longstrider or Enhanced Leap and you now need Charisma if you're casting offensive spells. Yeah, not cool.
While we are huge advocates of learning to play the game, you should be in the Grymforge around about now and it is difficult trying to adjust to the new playstyle while not killing your run. Trust us.

Ok, so lets switch to Bard at lvl 9 and we'll take Swords Bard 6/Gloom Stalker 3, we hear you say. Now we have the best of both worlds, right?
Yes, true, but you're also missing out on an entire 3 levels in Rogue and your 1 level in War Cleric. So now you have your Flourishes + Gloom advantages (without your lvl 2 Ranger spells) for the price of the entire Assassin subclass and your War Priest charges, as well as your War Cleric bonuses. You should be just about to assault Moonrise Towers at this stage and there is a reason we strongly recommend having Assassin and War Priest charges. That reason is Myrkul. You will be going into Myrkul without the big first round of combat, extra sneak attack damage and 3 less attacks in total. This will add at least 2-3 turns on to your fight, minimum. You do have Flourishes, but you can't use Special Arrows with a Flourish, and we kind of need Arrows of Undead Slaying as Myrkul is resistant to both physical and magical Piercing damage. We need that double damage to do normal damage. You could offset this by using a certain Hand Crossbow but you need to consider what you will lose taking this route, not just what you'll gain.

Bard 6/Assassin 3 is an option to keep your damage, but you're losing your Initiative, your Archery Passive, Misty Step and your extra attack. You also lose your War Cleric & Fighter levels. True, you will do way more damage from Flourishes and have Spell utility but you're also gonna need to switch up equipment, Dex is now way more valuable and frankly, this is an "akshually" that we just didn't want to get into.

The only smart time, in our opinion, to switch to Bard 6 is at lvl 12, when you will have all your big advantages from your main 2 classes up until now and equipment that makes this all come together. Yes, you don't have Action Surge anymore or your War Priest charges, but your Slashing Flourishes make up for this by allowing you to make 4 extra shots per Short Rest, which adds up to a grand total of 16 (!!!) per Long Rest, because you also get a free Short Rest with your Song of Rest. Action Surge + War Priest charges will give you 2-5 extra attacks per Short Rest and a grand total of 11 per Long Rest, as War Priest charges only refresh on Long Rest. We said "rest" a lot there, we know, but hopefully you can see the maths adds up.
You will also lose your free +1AC from your Fighting Style and obviously Guidance (unless you use Arcane Archer) + Resistance (Thaumaturgy is covered by your proficiencies. You picked Persuasion right?) but your spells are basically way better now, you should have perma-Bless from the Sweet Stone Features and your 'risma will be so high you won't need to worry about Guidance buffs, really. You can experiment with Bard 6 Assassin 3 Fighter 3/Fighter 2 War Cleric 1 but remember our warnings about initiative above, and remember we did the game with the builds we mention so we can verify their effectiveness. Here, we're entering theorycrafting territory so we don't want to go into it more than we already have unless we get enough requests to do so.

As you can see, this is a viable option but you'll only really enjoy it throughout Act 3, so there is absolutely nothing wrong with staying as a traditional Gloom Stalker Assassin for the entire game. You can also start as a Bard while following the instructions above if you're confident you can play without high Initiative and Dread Ambusher. It's a harder version of Gloomstalker until Act 3, if you want the challenge. We present you these paths for consideration only and encourage you to make your own decisions.

Act 2 Helmets
  • Flawed Helldusk Helm[bg3.wiki] - +1 to Con Saving Throws and +2 to Saving Throws against spells. Nice. You get this from Dammon by giving him a piece of Infernal Iron.
  • Helmet of Arcane Acuity[bg3.wiki] - This thing is godlike, especially if you've been stacking scrolls. Gain Arcane Acuity stacks when you make a successful weapon attack. What this means is when you hit things, your spellsave DC goes up by 1 per stack of the condition. This means every stack increases the likelihood of your spells succeeding by 5%. No, that's not a typo. Until you hit Act 3 your combat strat is now "maul something in the first round of combat and avoid getting hit, then cast CC spell/scroll, mop up the battlefield." You can also drink a Haste Potion (or have Haste pre-cast but have a contingency if you lose Concentration) and pull off CC in the first round; Hypnotic Pattern is a really solid option and pairs beautifully with the Ring of the Mystic Scoundrel which we'll grab in Act 3, and those two items pair exceptionally well with the Not Gloom Stalker build. This isn't mandatory per se but it's handy to have as until now, we weren't spec'ced for offensive magic and now we are. You get this from the secret meeting chamber in the Mason's Guild (check the wiki for better directions).

Intermission: The Slippy Bow Build
What?
Ok, so remember how we told you to grab Winter's Clutches? Well, this is the reason. The build and loadout we have provided you will be doing catastrophic damage to 90% of targets in the game, and you're also fairly tanky. We played this build a lot and in our boredom, we tried to figure out a way to make it more interesting and fun, while keeping the combat utility and efficiency.

We found a way to do exactly that. We present to you, The Slippy Bow Build. We will list all of the alternative things you need to make this build work, so forgive us for repeating ourselves a little.
You will need:
  • Winter's Clutches[bg3.wiki] - Inflicts 2 stacks of Encrusted with Frost. EwF gives targets Disdavantage on Dex Saving Throws and 7 stacks will force a DC 12 Con save. If they fail, they take 1d4 Cold Damage and become Frozen. Frozen means the target is Incapacitated and vulnerable to Bludgeoning, Thunder and Force damage. If they take any of those damage types, the condition ends. Fire also removes it instantly. Buy these from Lady Esther on Rosymorn Monastary Trail.
  • Snowburst Ring[bg3.wiki] - Creates a patch of ice surface beneath a target when you deal cold damage to it. You can get it from a hiding spot under the floorboards in a back room at Last Light (check the wiki for more detailed directions).
  • Boots of Stormy Clamour[bg3.wiki] - Inflict Reverberation on a target when you inflict a condition. You buy these from Omeluum after completing his quest.
  • Coldbrim Hat[bg3.wiki] - Inflicts 2 stacks of Encrusted with Frost alongside other spell or cantrip conditions. For our purposes this only works with Winter's Clutches, as the Encrusted with Frost stacks with the Winter's Clutches version and bypasses the spell requirement. This works at the time of writing pre-Patch 8. You get this from a chest hidden behind the bookcase in Balthazar's Room in Moonrise Towers.

The Strategy
We hope you can see where this is going. You enchant your Titanstring with Cold damage by using the Drakethroat Glaive[bg3.wiki] and now you're cooking with... frost? Anyway, you're going to throw water on things before starting combat and then start shooting. They will take 2d4 Cold damage, gain 4 stacks of Encrusted with Frost, 2 stacks of Reverberation and now be standing on an Ice Surface which can prone them if they don't jump out of it, on top of all the damage you normally do. What's that you say? What happens when you use an Arrow of Many Targets with this build? We won't spoil it for you...

This build is about combining damage with CC while also taking advantage of Wet targets being weak to Frost. The CC makes up for having less AC than the vanilla build and the extra Frost damage makes up for your lost damage riders (notably the Flawed Helldusk and Diadem of Arcane Synergy, if you're using it). It doesn't quite match up to the same numbers, but it's enough. Interestingly, the Disadvantage on Dex Throws + Ability Drain should make it harder for targets to make the Prone Saving Throw, but extensive Googling and staring at the combat log haven't proved this for us. Remember, the Coldbrim only applies EwF to the first target you hit that turn, so a Many Targets will inflict 4 stacks on the thing you shoot it at and 2 on the subsequent targets.

Applying your chilly death functions best with Arrows of Many Targets, but it's still very effective against single targets, especially once you start getting Disintegrate scrolls. Not Gloom Stalker will also get Thunder Wave for those unlucky enough to get Frozen but you can grab Thunder Wave, Shatter and Magic Missile scrolls in the meantime.

The downside to this build is in Act 2 a lot of undead are resistant to Cold damage. This doesn't stop you from applying your conditions but the build won't quite match the damage numbers you were putting out; it will always be slightly behind as you don't have the extra dmg riders from Diadem of Arcane Synergy & Flawed Helldusk gloves.
Additionally, we don't recommend this for Myrkul as he is one of the things resistant to Cold damage. You also need to remember you are losing 2AC (Evasive Shoes & Ring of Protection), 1d4 unless things are Wet, Arcane Acuity or Diadem of Arcane Synergy and you must have the Adamantine Shield equipped at all times. Honestly, this is a lot of fun to try out but it feels better suited to party play.

For those of you that want to do 4-man runs, this is the perfect way to compliment your Storm Sorcerer (Frost Sorcerer will want your Frost gear) while still putting serious damage downrange. Add a Fighter to tank & a Cleric to throw water while buffing everyone and you got a yourself a par-tay. The cool thing about that is a Storm Sorcerer will probably want your Boots of Stormy Clamour which allows your Gloom Stalker to take the Evasive Shoes and the same amount of Reverb is still going downrange.

Slayer Form is ok but in our opinion should be better, considering what you sacrifice to get it. Saving Last Light isn't as important for Monks, but obviously having more options in Act 3 for equipment is a benefit we'll want to keep.
Another reason to pick Durge is to have a 1v1 vs Orin in Act 3, making that fight substantially easier. Top tip, Orin's Slayer Form isn't a Wildshape, so when it gets to 0 health she dies; another reason to save Last Light as, if you have the Slayer Form, she doesn't.
Finally, and this is the most important reason to pick Durge: walking around kicking squirrels and carving up Bards is just good fun.

Astarion
The main reason you take Astarion is his "Happy" condition after a bite, which gives you +1 to Attack Rolls, Saving Throws and some Ability Checks. As a Monk, you'll be in melee range the whole game so you will be able to consistently get the buffs, but remember Bite is a Bonus Action and a lot of your powerful Monk abilities are also Bonus Actions; try to grab the buff out of combat if you can.
It's worth mentioning that Astarion gets a +1d10 (sweet) Necrotic bonus added to his Weapon and Unarmed Attacks if he Ascends, but you will only get to enjoy this in Act 3. He is also an Elf, so he'll get Perception Proficiency, Darkvision & the Charmed Advantage & Magic Sleep Immunity.

Gale
Gale is Human, but you don't play him for that. He gets a free lvl 3 Spellslot in Act 2 after wrecking the Thorm siblings, gets the Ring of Evasion (use your Reaction to pass a Dex Saving Throw if you fail it) and can get Anointed in Splendour for free if he chooses to kiss and make up with Mystra. Not bad, but genuinely not that great either. Open Hand Monks won't be slinging spells, so we're not really sure what you'll be using this spellslot for.

Karlach
Playing as Karlach means you can't get the Infernal Robe, so skip her for this one.

Lae'zel
Lae'zel is a Githyanki and comes with all of the bonuses they get. She's also starting out as a Fighter. Realistically, she's the same as Shart: unless you want story flavour, just pick a Tav Githyanki.

Shadowheart
Shadowheart is a High Half-Elf (which, fun fact, is why her Firebolt cantrip sucks: it scales off her INT and not her WIS) so you can have some story flavour if you want to pick Half-Elf, but that is genuinely about it.

Wyll
Wyll gives you access to some nice Warlock gear but other than that, also a Human. Snore. You'd probably be better off picking a Tav and grabbing him as a companion (not in your party, just at camp... and not casting buffs on you) to get the gear, as his Infernal Robe is really useful to us in this build (more on that later).
Monk: Durge/Tav Race
For all Durge and Tav lovers, we'll list all of the races in the order we feel they suit the playstyle best. For Tav specifically, we recommend choosing Guild Artisan as your background, but it's up to you.

Elf
Your optimum selection is Wood Elf, purely based on the Perception Proficiency and the extra movement distance (1.5m) which will help you close the gap as a melee class, which only gets better once you get your Unarmoured Movement & double Improved Unarmoured Movement passives; these increase your Movement Speed by 3m, 4.5m and 6m respectively at lvls 2, 6 & 10 but for Elf you get 4.5m, 6m and 7.5m. Tidy.
You also have Stealth Proficiency and Darkvision, as well as Advantage on Charmed & you can't be magically put to sleep. Perception Proficiency alongside your Wisdom (run ahead to the Stats section for more on this) means you'll have a good chance of finding all those buttons and detecting those Ambushes. You could go High Elf and take Minor Illusion for an easy Grym fight and grouping gribblies up, but we don't believe this is worth it over the Wood Elf bonuses. You'll want high Wisdom as a Monk anyway but Perception is an important check to have good rolls on throughout the majority of the game, so getting +2-+4 on it on top of Wisdom Modifiers means you'll rarely fail one.

Githyanki
Next best would be Githyanki due to their Astral Knowledge, Mage Hand and free Enhanced Leap & Misty Step per Long Rest. The Mage Hand in particular can be used as a source of Wet, Haste Spores or just another target on the field that can throw stuff for you, which is pretty valuable when you only have one character. They don't get that extra movement distance or Proficiencies, though. Not having Darkvision will also suck in the Underdark, Grymforge and Act 2, although this can be offset by the fact you'll be in melee range. Additionally, Gith equipment is dope but you won't be using most of it as it's all Medium Armour or Weapons and the Mask of the Shapeshifter has you covered.

Half-Orc
Half-Orc is a melee race, no doubt about it, but it's Savage Attacks Passive only procs with melee weapon attacks, and we're going to be using Queensberry Rules. It does, however, come with a sweet ability called Relentless Endurance that improves your survivability drastically. When you die as a Half-Orc, once per Long Rest, you are insta-rezzed with 1 HP. Yeah, you read that right.
Note: This is more for saving you against deaths outside of combat, as in combat it is likely you simply survive one more hit than you normally would and will die anyway, depending on your turn order. It won't help against chasms or falls either. Still, not getting completely bodied and losing your run to a trap is nice.

Drow
Basically a Wood Elf without the Stealth Proficiency and Movement Speed. They are also objectively cooler. Weapon Proficiencies don't matter as we be brawlin', and a free Faerie Fire (lvl 3) & Darkness (lvl 5) cast can come in handy but it's never going to be game-changing. You also don't have to Shapeshift in Act 1 at all, which is convenient but again, not game-changing.

Half-Elf
Half-Elf is the same as Elf except it doesn't get the Perception Proficiency and instead gets Shield Proficiency. As above, Weapon Proficiencies don't matter as (we're banging this drum a lot here aren't we?) you'll be using Unarmed attacks. We prefer Elf and Drow over Half-Elf due to this and the fact that you won't get Monk passive bonuses while using a Shield, so this trade-off for lack of Perception Proficiency is moot.

Halfling
Halflings come with the Lucky & Brave features, which let you re-roll a 1 at will and give you Advantage on Fear checks, respectively. You can also choose between having Advantage on Stealth Checks or Advantage on Saving Throws against Poison and Resistance to Poison Damage. We'd go with the second option here. The drawback is you move 1.5m less than your long-legged counterparts and, again, don't get relevant Proficiencies but Unarmoured Movement Passives will help negate this.

Dwarf
Dwarven Resilience, which is Advantage against being Poisoned and Resistance to Poison Damage. You only really wanna go Duergar here for the Invis and Advantage against Illusions, Charm and Paralysis. Honestly, you don't need the Invis and the Advantage on Paralysis Saves is probably a waste; if you ever need to use it, you've made a mistake by allowing whatever it is to attempt to Paralyse you in the first place. The Enlarge cast can be nice on Monk as it gives you Advantage on Strength Checks and Saving Throws, but the extra 1d4 damage only applies to weapon attacks. You also have less Movement Speed than the long-legged races but again, your passives can offset this.

Gnome
Advantage on Wisdom, Charisma and Intelligence Saving Throws. The Sub-Race bonuses are all meh here for Monk too, as doubling your History Proficiency Bonus, Speak with Animals or Advantage on Stealth Checks are either pointless or situational. They have nice saving buffs but like Duergar, if you're utilising them it means you've played too risky or made a mistake, and you're also nerfed in movement but for the third time, passives can cover this for you. There's still better options above, truthfully.

Tiefling
Tieflings are cool but all they're really offering consistently is Resistance to Fire. The 3 subraces all have some spells that you might use but they either have Charisma or Constitution as their modifiers. Mephistopheles Tieflings would probably be our pick for Mage Hand, closely followed by Asmodeus for the Hellish Rebuke with Zariel coming in last with Thaumaturgy. As for their other spells: Produce Flame sucks, Darkness is fine, Flame Blade & Burning Hands most likely won't be used and the smites need weapons to use.

Human
Shield Proficiency and an extra Proficiency, with extra Carry Weight. Meh. Next.

Dragonborn
Oh, this is what's next? Ouch...
Objectively the coolest-looking race and also objectively the worst to play as; you're nerfing yourself for zero reason as these guys literally have nothing going for them, not even Darkvision. Don't pick Dragonborn unless you plan on impressing your enemies to death with your sick horns and tail. Let us know how it goes in the comments.

Our runs were done with Wood Elf & Githyanki, but it's up to you. Remember, we've based these selections on what we found to be consistently useful throughout our runs and comparing what you would lose when compared to our optimal picks. You can have a different opinion to us but we believe Wood Elf and Githyanki to be the "best" races for this build, with Half-Orc being worth a look purely for their passive.

Monk: Builds and Multiclassing
We'll be talking you through our Monk build that we used to complete a run. For those of you that like multiclassing synergies, we have bad news: we did this as a Pure Monk because honestly, it's all you need. If you want to check out some multiclass build recommendations, fly down to the FAQ - Monk section where we talk about what you can take.

For now though, here's what we'll be going for.
Monk Build Breakdown
We're aiming for Open Hand Monk 12. Unlike Gloom Stalker, this is going to be a fairly straightforward process:

Lvl 1: Monk Surprise! Pick Monk as your starting class. Take Athletics and Acrobatics as your Proficiencies as there's not much else to take and if you need to launch things at other things or from the top of cliffs, these will help you do that and defend against it.
You'll get a resource known as Ki Points which are what you'll spend to use your Flurry of Blows actions. There are ways to regain these through equipment and of course, you'll get more as you level up.
You also get some Martial Arts passives, which we'll list for you now:
Martial Arts: Dextrous Attacks allows your attacks with Monk weapons and Unarmed attacks scale with your Dexterity instead of your Strength if your Dexterity is higher. This will help until you get your Hill Giant Elixirs and then it's completely useless, as your Str will always be higher than your Dex.

Martial Arts: Deft Strikes means attacks with Monk Weapons and Unarmed attacks deal 1d4 Bludgeoning damage unless their normal damage is higher. This will increase as your levels do, and it doesn't sound too gnarly until you get Tavern Brawler (more on this in a moment, we're so close to explaining it, we promise!).

Martial Arts: Bonus Unarmed Strike allows you to make another attack with a Monk weapon or your fists as a bonus action. This lets you keep up your fisticuffs when your Ki Points run out.

Unarmoured Defence is one of those aforementioned tasty Wisdom bonuses we get. While not wearing armour, you add your Wisdom Modifier to your Armour Class. This is really good, as when we do the maths on this, your AC is going to be 16 in the Nautiloid. To put that into perspective, the best Armour you can grab until reaching the Emerald Grove is Lae'zel's Githyanki Half-Plate which is 17 AC.

Flurry of Blows is a Bonus Action that allows you to punch twice in quick succession. So you're smacking things with your action then giving a 1-2 combo straight afterwards. Our only complaint is we wish they'd added kicks...

Lvl 2: Monk gives you another Ki Point and some cool abilities. Unarmoured Movement is mentioned earlier but it's giving you an extra 3m of Movement Speed as long as you're not wearing Armour or have a Shield. We can't stress enough how helpful this is, especially if you're a small race. Patient Defence gives enemies Disadvantage on Attack Rolls against you and gives you Advantage on Dex Saves. It's nice for when you can't get out of Dodge and know you're gonna have to eat a few hits.
Step of the Wind: Dash & Step of the Wind: Disengage are how you're going to get around the battlefield while you practice your boxing. These actions are the same as Rogue's Cunning Action: Dash and Cunning Action: Disengage, except you can Jump for free after using them. Yeah. You wanna jump in combat? Use SotW: Dash and double your Movement as a freebie.

Lvl 3: Open Hand Monk is where you start to see why we're genuinely confused as to why Monk is the 3rd least played class. You get another Ki Point and your base damage from Deft Strikes goes from 1d4 to 1d6. You'll also get Deflect Missiles, which allows you to use your Reaction to reduce Ranged Attack damage against you by 1d10+Dex and, if this reduces the damage to 0, you can use a Ki Point to fling it back at whoever shot it. Return to sender, indeed.
Open Hand Monk also gives you better Flurry of Blows Attacks. You'll get Stagger which stops enemies from using reactions, Push which sends them back 5m and Topple, which makes them Prone. Topple is the real meat and taters here, as anything with a Legendary Action that you put onto their backs can't use that Legendary Action until they get back up. Yeah. As long as this lands, most bosses are about to become trivial.

Lvl 4: Open Hand Monk is where you'll hopefully become a convert and also start asking why Monk is the 3rd least played class. You get another Ki Point and Slow Fall, which lets you use a reaction to gain Resistance to Fall Damage. Nice, but also don't rely on this and start dropping off of buildings.
The real star of the show here is Tavern Brawler, which you'll be taking as your first Feat. This means your character is now online and ready to rip. Tavern Brawler adds your Str modifier twice to both your Attack Roll and Damage Roll. Now you see why we're using Strength Elixirs, because that's +10 to your Hit Chance and your damage. Start punching stuff and marvel at how easy the game just became.

Lvl 5: Open Hand Monk is where you'll consider signing yourself up to go hang with those Shaolin dudes irl. You get another Ki Point and Extra Attack like other martial classes, but you'll also get Stunning Strike, which does exactly what it says in the description. You can now open up on things with this, Stunning them, and then follow up with a Topple to put them on their backs and guarantee they ain't doing anything useful. It also means you've got 2 chances to prevent bosses from using their Legendary Actions. It just keeps getting better and better.
Monk: Builds and Multiclassing Cont.
Lvl 6: Open Hand Monk is where this class really becomes a powerhouse. If you thought lvl 5 was spicy get some milk, because this just got dialled up to 11. As usual, another Ki Point and your Unarmoured Movement is upped by another 1.5m.
If you read our Tips section we talk about Diluted Oil of Sharpness. You now have Ki Empowered Strikes, which is basically that on your knuckles, for free, all of the time. Yes, really. Strap in, this isn't even the best bit!
You now have access to Manifestations, which are toggleable abilities that add a 1d4+Wisdom Modifier's worth of damage to your attacks. These manifestations come in the Psychic (Mind), Necrotic (Body) and Radiant (Soul) flavours and there's no penalty for switching between them, you just can't have more than one active at a time. To do the maths, at this stage you'll have 18 Wisdom and therefore you're doing 4+ 1d4's worth of damage in the Manifestation type you pick. For free.
But wait, there's more!
Finally, you have access to Wholeness of Body. This is a Once per Long Rest ability that instantly heals you for 3x your Monk lvl (18 Health from the get go. That's almost a Greater Healing Potion! For free! Madness!) and instantly restores half of your Ki Points rounded down (you're lvl 7 so that means 3 Ki Points). You now have a 3-turn buff that gives you an extra Bonus Action (!!) and restores a Ki Point every turn.
All of this is quite frankly overpowered at lvl 10, nevermind lvl 6. You're now competing for the highest damage output in the game and all you did was level up.

Lvl 7: Open Hand Monk gives you yet another Ki Point and Evasion, which means when you save a Dex Spell Saving Throw instead of taking half damage, you take no damage. Pretty handy but don't rely on it.
Additionally you get Stillness of Mind which sounds good on paper but is a bit of a pain. Whenever you're Feared or Charmed, you spend an Action to rid yourself of the condition. You also don't have a choice with this, as you cannot remove it/turn it off in your Reactions menu. This is like a double-edged sword if the sword also had a blade for a handle: rather than not worrying about Fear or Charm you actually don't want to get hit by it even more, as it will cost you your action. Yes, Bonus Actions are where you have Flurries etc and Speed Potions exist but it's just an inconvenience you don't need nor asked for.

Lvl 8: Open Hand Monk is underwhelming compared to everything before it, frankly. Another Ki Point and choose a Feat. We'd recommend Alert for the Initiative bonus.

Lvl 9: Open Hand Monk gives you another Ki Point, your base damage goes up from 1d6 to 1d8 and you gain Advanced Unarmoured Movement, which allows you to Jump an extra 6m. The real star here is your Ki Resonation strikes and Ki Resonation Blast abilities. You use the strike to Resonate a target for 10 turns (and it stays on corpses) then you can spend a Ki Point, and only a Ki Point, no Actions required, to detonate them for 3d6 Force. This is fairly slept-on in our opinion, as the Resonation Strikes are the same as your normal Action Attack and trigger your Extra Attacks just the same, so there's no downside to using it. We like to hit a few enemies (4 is a nice number), ping a Black Hole then detonate them all together. That's 12d6 on 4 enemies. That's gnarly.

Lvl 10: Open Hand Monk has 11 Ki Points total now, you gain another 1.m to your Movement Speed and you're immune to Poison (damage and the condition) and Disease. Cool, we guess.

Lvl 11: Open Hand Monk is 12 Ki Points total and you gain Tranquillity, which is free Sanctuary when you wake up after a Long Rest. Not game-changing but it's nice to have that initial protection in your first combat.

Lvl 12: Open Hand Monk is the same as lvl 8: underwhelming. Your final Ki Point and a Feat. We recommend an ASI to take your Dex to 18, as this is more AC and another point to your Initiative.

That's pretty much it! The astute among you will notice that Monk peaks at lvl 6 with the bonuses becoming more and more situational as you increase in levels. As always, we believe authenticity is key so this is what we did and we breezed the game, frankly, but if you want more information on how to use multiclassing to get even bigger damage numbers fly down to the FAQ - Monk section.

  • The Deathstalker Mantle[bg3.wiki] - This is a controversial cloak for us, as it is good but it is relied upon far too heavily. Basically, once per turn, this cloak makes you invisible for 2 turns after a kill.

The first hurdle is once you have Cull the Weak the invis won't proc if CtW finishes off a target. The second issue you come across is once you have Flurries, you're going to be wasting them by not using them to keep your invis. You're far too powerful to be needing the invisibility by lvl 4, honestly.
    This cloak is eventually going to drag out combats or put you at more risk than you need to be in while you try and proc the invis when you would be better served learning positioning, planning your fights, your routes to safety and trying to predict how the enemy will move relative to your position. You do not need the mantle to complete a solo and in our opinion it will actually be detrimental to your run past lvl 5. The Dark Urge
The Dark Urge gets the Deathstalker Mantle. We don't believe the Mantle is endgame or Best in Slot (BiS), it just makes fights before lvl 5 easier/less risky. We will dive deeper into the Mantle in the equipment section. To be fair to Paladin, the amount of damage you eventually start putting out will probably work in your favour as you'll be killing a lot of things outright, so you might get more consistent use out of the Mantle than with other classes.
Medicine Proficiency is whatever, Intimidation can come in handy.
Slayer Form is ok but in our opinion should be better, considering what you sacrifice to get it. Saving Last Light is almost mandatory as Dammon sells some of the best gear in the game, but there are options in case the Isobel fight doesn't go your way.
Another reason to pick Durge is to have a 1v1 vs Orin in Act 3, making that fight substantially easier. Top tip, Orin's Slayer Form isn't a Wildshape, so when it gets to 0 health she dies; another reason to save Last Light as, if you have the Slayer Form, she doesn't.
Finally, and this is the most important reason to pick Durge: walking around kicking squirrels and carving up Bards is just good fun.

Astarion
The main reason you take Astarion is his "Happy" condition after a bite, which gives you +1 to Attack Rolls, Saving Throws and some Ability Checks. As a Paladin, you'll be in melee range the whole game so you will be able to consistently get the buffs. As Bite is a Bonus Action, you won't have to worry overmuch about using it in combat or outside, just remember your Great Weapon Master attack (more on that later).
It's worth mentioning that Astarion gets a +1d10 (sweet) Necrotic bonus added to his Weapon and Unarmed Attacks if he Ascends, but you will only get to enjoy this in Act 3. He is also an Elf, so he'll get Perception Proficiency, Darkvision & the Charmed Advantage & Magic Sleep Immunity.

Gale
Gale is Human, but you don't play him for that. He gets a free lvl 3 Spellslot in Act 2 after wrecking the Thorm siblings, gets the Ring of Evasion (use your Reaction to pass a Dex Saving Throw if you fail it) and can get Anointed in Splendour for free if he chooses to kiss and make up with Mystra. Not bad, but genuinely not that great either. He's a decent choice here as you'll get another lvl 3 Smite for free in Act 2, which is always gonna help, but the tradeoff is you have to actually play as Gale.

Karlach
Karlach would, at first glance, be a good pick for a Paladin, but her Soul Coins only really suit her when she stays as a Barbarian. We do NOT want to walk around at under 25% HP for the sake of 1d4 Fire Damage. She is a Zariel Tiefling though, so she'll get Smites of her own and Thaumaturgy to play with. If you've read everything before now, however, you'll know that Branding and Searing Smites use both your Action & Bonus Action which is not efficient, especially when you'll want that action for other things.
Note: It has come to our attention that Karlach's 1d4 Fire Damage is bugged and doesn't require you to be at <25% HP to get, so you do you. Still, playing as Karlach? No thanks.]

Paladin: Durge/Tav Race
For all Durge and Tav lovers, we'll list all of the races in the order we feel they suit the playstyle best. For Tav specifically, we recommend choosing Guild Artisan as your background, but it's up to you.

Elf
With the addition of Patch 8, you can take Booming Blade when you choose High Elf, which is going to make your initial first 4 levels way less difficult. Booming Blade is a melee cantrip that adds 1d8 Thunder Damage when the target moves; this means you can batter something with it in melee and then push them so when they move back into range, you proc that damage. This gets better with more levels, as at lvl 5 you do an additional 1d8 on the cast and the target takes 2d8 when it moves, and this goes up to 2d8 inital and 3d8 movement damage when you hit lvl 11, on top of your normal weapon damage and then you can proc your Smites. Very tasty. This is essentially extra damage like the Half-Orc except you don't need a Crit to gain the benefits, therefore you will gain more consistent damage. Don't choose Wood Elf here, Booming Blade is too good.

Half-Elf
Half-Elf is the same as Elf except it doesn't get the Perception Proficiency and instead gets Shield Proficiency. As above, Weapon Proficiencies don't matter as you'll always have a class that covers them for you in your build. High Half-Elf gets Booming Blade too, so all the stuff above about it applies here as well. Don't go Wood Elf here either.

Half-Orc
Half-Orc is a melee race as it comes with the Savage Attacks Passive (not to be confused with Savage Attacker) which means you roll an extra Damage Dice on Crits. In English, say a normal roll is 1d6. Crit means 2d6. Half-Orc Crit means 3d6. Very tidy. Note we said "normal" damage dice. In Honour Mode, this does not include smites or sneak attacks. We feel like High Elf is now the top contender purely because of Booming Blade and its effectiveness, especially in those early levels. If you spec for Crits, you'll probably get more value out of the Half-Orc passive on the whole but you'll only really be able to spec into Crits heavily in Act 3 and honestly at this stage, you've passed the threshold for "this thing is about to get wrecked" you're just trying to get the "how wrecked is it" numbers higher.
It also comes with a sweet ability called Relentless Endurance that improves your survivability drastically. When you die as a Half-Orc, once per Long Rest, you are insta-rezzed with 1 HP. Yeah, you read that right. We recommend playing like you don't have a safety net though, as this drastically increases your odds of never having to use it.
Note: This is more for saving you against deaths outside of combat, as in combat it is likely you simply survive one more hit than you normally would and will die anyway, depending on your turn order. It won't help against chasms or falls either. Still, not getting completely bodied and losing your run to a trap is nice.

Githyanki
Githyanki get Astral Knowledge, Mage Hand and free Enhanced Leap & Misty Step per Long Rest. The Mage Hand in particular can be used as a source of Wet, Haste Spores or just another target on the field that can throw stuff for you, which is pretty valuable when you only have one character. They don't get that extra movement distance or Proficiencies, though.
Additionally, while there is a lot of equipment that have special effects that only proc when you're a Gith, you can use the Mask of the Shapeshifter[bg3.wiki] or a Disguise Spell/Ability to proc them anyway. Make sure to equip the item after you transform or it won't register your bonus effects properly. The Soulbreaker Greatsword is a nice little weapon to have for a Paladin but genuinely, as MotSS keeps your shapeshifted after you take it off, choosing Half-Orc or Elf is just Gith with extra steps.

Drow
Basically a Wood Elf without the Stealth Proficiency and Movement Speed. They are also objectively cooler. Weapon Proficiencies don't matter as we'll be covering all of that with our classes, and a free Faerie Fire (lvl 3) & Darkness (lvl 5) cast can come in handy but it's never going to be game-changing. You also don't have to Shapeshift in Act 1 at all, which is convenient but again, not game-changing.

Halfling
Halflings come with the Lucky & Brave features, which let you re-roll a 1 at will and give you Advantage on Fear checks, respectively. You can also choose between having Advantage on Stealth Checks or Advantage on Saving Throws against Poison and Resistance to Poison Damage. These are fairly handy features, but the drawback is you move 1.5m less than your long-legged counterparts and again, don't get relevant Proficiencies.

Dwarf
Dwarven Resilience, which is Advantage against being Poisoned and Resistance to Poison Damage. Gold Dwarf is a good shout for extra HP, especially in the early acts, and Duergar for the Invis and Advantage against Illusions, Charm and Paralysis is also good. Honestly, you don't need the Invis and the Advantage on Paralysis Saves is probably a waste; if you ever need to use it, you've made a mistake by allowing whatever it is to attempt to Paralyse you in the first place. The Enlarge cast is also good but situational and it only lasts for 10 turns. You also have less Movement Speed than the long-legged races.

Tiefling
Tieflings are cool but all they're really offering consistently is Resistance to Fire. The 3 subraces all have some spells that you might use but they either have Charisma or Constitution as their modifiers. Mephistopheles Tieflings would probably be our pick for Mage Hand, closely followed by Asmodeus for the Hellish Rebuke with Zariel coming in last with Thaumaturgy. As for their other spells: Produce Flame sucks, Darkness is fine, Flame Blade & Burning Hands most likely won't be used and Searing Smite is... well... you should know our opinions on this from our info on Karlach. We don't think it's worth it, to be honest.

Gnome
Advantage on Wisdom, Charisma and Intelligence Saving Throws. The Sub-Race bonuses are all meh here for Paladin too, as doubling your History Proficiency Bonus, Speak with Animals or Advantage on Stealth Checks are either pointless or situational. They have nice saving buffs but like Duergar, if you're utilising them it means you've played too risky or made a mistake, and you're also nerfed in movement. There's still better options above, truthfully.

Human
Shield Proficiency and an extra Proficiency, with extra Carry Weight. Meh. Next.

Dragonborn
Oh, this is what's next? Ouch...
Objectively the coolest-looking race and also objectively the worst to play as; you're nerfing yourself for zero reason as these guys literally have nothing going for them, not even Darkvision. Don't pick Dragonborn unless you plan on impressing your enemies to death with your sick horns and tail. Let us know how it goes in the comments.

We feel like High Elf is a serious contender for top spot with melee classes now, but our runs were done with Half-Orc as they were in Patch 7. As always, it's up to you. Remember, we've based these selections on what we found to be consistently useful throughout our runs and comparing what you would lose when compared to our optimal picks. You can have a different opinion to us but we believe Half-Orc to be the optimum and therefore "Best" pick for pretty much any melee-heavy character.
Paladin: Stats
Here are the stat spreads you want for your Paladin. We have broken them down with optimum switcheroos in mind, but it is completely up to you how much of our advice you take.

Character Creation Stat Spread
Str = 8
Dex = 14
Con = 16
Int = 8
Wis = 12
Cha = 16

This is your spread at character creation. You can play with Wis and Cha for a bit, for example going 14 apiece for a more even spread but remember, Paladins' Spellcasting Modifier is 'risma and they get bonuses (mainly Aura of Protection at lvl 6) based off of it. We'll tell you here and then probably remind you a few times later on, but your spellcasting modifier does not affect the strength of your Smites, only the level of spellslot you used. You may only have 2 skills that go to 16; later on we will be respec-ing to get 18 Cha. That means your Wis will have to take a hit but for now, that 17 is a complete waste of a point that you can utilise elsewhere.

Strength 8 is because we're going to dump Strength, as you will be using Hill Giant Elixirs and, when you unlock them, Cloud Giant Elixirs.
Dex 14 is as high as you'll want it to be as we'll be using Medium Armour and then Heavy later on down the line; you can only gain a maximum AC modifier of +2 when wearing Medium Armour.
Con 16 gives you respectable health and a tasty +3 to Con saves.
Int 8 will make Arcana checks suck but it contributes nothing to the build and you’ll grab the Warped Headband of Intellect anyway if you really want some brainpower.

Paladin: Builds and Multiclassing
Paladin builds are many but the premise behind all of them is Big Smites. You want to hit things and hit them hard while having as many spellslots as possible. Inevitably, as Paladins are half-casters, this means a multiclass. There are more options than what we are presenting here but as always, this is the build we used on a successful run so here it is, level by level, choice by choice.

Paladin Build Breakdown

We went for the Paladin 6 Abjuration Wizard 4 Fighter 2 approach as we found this gave us a tasty balance between defence and spellslots. For those that don't know, Abjuration Wizards gain something called Arcane Ward which blocks damage equal to its intensity then decreases intensity by 1. You gain intensity by casting Abjuration spells; the ones you want will be listed below.
Without further ado:

Lvl 1: Oath of Vengeance Paladin Paladin is your first level (shock). Take Oath of Vengeance as when you get Vow of Enmity at Lvl 3 (literally after leaving the Grove, which is madness) you cast it on yourself to gain Advantage on all enemies for 10 turns. Yes. Really. We think it's a bug but it's whatever at this stage, it ain't getting fixed. At least it isn't "Cast Darkness + Devil's Sight" or "cheese Raph for the Armour" levels of trash... If you don't wanna use this for whatever reason, Oathbreaker is your next best bet due to Aura of Hate at Lvl 6 but beyond that, we'll be focusing on Vengeance as this is what we actually used.

We recommend Athletics and Insight as your Proficiencies as you will have high 'risma; you can take Persuasion or Intimidation if you want instead of Athletics. As always it's up to you.

Lvl 2: Oath of Vengeance Paladin The entire reason you take Paladin has arrived! Divine Smite. Divine. Smite. DIVINE SMITE! Let it roll off the tongue. After you connect with an attack, your weapon deals an extra 2d8 Radiant Damage, 3d8 against Fiends and Undead and you can use it as many times as you like as long as you have a spellslot available. It's also worth mentioning that each level of spell you use adds another 1d8 but this caps out at 4th Level Spells; any higher and you don't get more than 5d8 or 6d8 against Fiends and Undead. Delicious. As of now, you have 2 Lvl 1 Spellslots to use and whatever you use them on at this stage in the game usually won't live through it. We recommend you acquaint the Bugbear and Za'krug in the first Grove Fight with them.

Last thing on Divine Smite: go into your Reactions menu and set it to Ask on Hit and Ask on Crit; you must do this every time you get a higher level spellslot as it counts as an entirely different reaction. This way, you can decide on every attack that hits what will and will not survive that turn. You don't want it on Always On because if something has next to no health, you waste the spellslot; you don't need to add extra damage dice when something will die to your normal weapon damage right? Do not be afraid to burn all your slots in a difficult fight as Camp Supplies are plentiful and you should be looting everything. it's not uncommon in a Paladin Solo to be Resting after every other fight. Momma's big strong boy needs his beauty sleep, a-yes he does!

Take Great Weapon Fighting as your Fighting Style to reroll 1s & 2s on your damage dice (including your Smites!) and for your spells grab Shield of Faith, Command and Protection from Evil and Good. Grab whatever else you feel like but between your upcoming Vow of Enmity and wanting to save spellslots for Smites, you won't use anything else; Shield of Faith is a nice +2 AC bonus in the beginning of the game and Command is nice to keep particularly spicy things calm while it waits its turn to be Smote to bits. PfEaG will be mandatory in Act 2. Additionally Wrathful, Searing and Thunderous Smites are worse versions of Divine, don't use these; they cost your Bonus Action + a spellslot and do less damage. They're just not worth it.

Lvl 3: Oath of Vengeance Paladin Here we go... Vow of Enmity is here, along with Abjure Enemy, which is whatever. You also get Bane and Hunter's Mark, both of which are pointless when you think about casting them or think about having another Smite. Unfortunately this is going to be a pattern until you get yourself Wizard levels and the Helm of Arcane Acuity (see the gear section) and even then you'll mostly want to use scrolls as a Smite is just the better option for your spellslots. Speaking of, you now have an extra lvl 1 to use. Tidy.

Lvl 4: Oath of Vengeance Paladin is where we get our first really spicy upgrade. Take Great Weapon Master as your Feat, which is Sharpshooter for Melee. You also gain the ability to make another Melee Attack using your Bonus Action when you kill something or land a Crit; you have Etsy Extra Attack 1 level before everyone else gets an Extra Attack... and you have Smites. Quick little mathematical intermission: assuming you're drinking Hill Giant Elixirs (which you definitely should be) you're now doing your chosen weapon's damage + 5 + 10 + 2d8 Radiant, which when it crits is all doubled, and then you get to do it again with your Bonus Action. At level 4. 4. As for your -5 chance to hit for that +10, Vow of Enmity has you covered with Advantage, as mathematically Advantage is equivalent to +3-5 on your rolls and you're gonna be using a weapon that gives you a 1d4 to your chance to hit, so that's 5-20% extra Hit Chance right there. You can also turn off All In and still get the Bonus Action attack on crits. It's just broken, in all honesty. No spellslot this level though. Boo.

Lvl 5: Oath of Vengeance Paladin is where things just get silly. Extra Attack is here, so now you get all of the above guaranteed twice per turn + your Bonus Action if you crit. You also get Hold Person and Misty Step for free, which is nice, but you're still better off using Scrolls and saving your spellslots. You should take Magic Weapon as your spell this level too, as you now get to choose between Shield of Faith or Magic Weapon as your Concentration Spell for the remainder of Act 1 (you really want PfEaG in Act 2). Do you want +2AC or +1 to Attack Rolls and Damage? Choose depending on your fight, is our advice. Keeping track of your Smites, you now have four lvl 1 slots and two lvl 2 slots. Use them liberally. Remember, your lvl 2s add an extra 1d8 to your Radiant Damage, so use those on things you want to delete.
If you picked High Elf as your Race, Booming Blade also gets a buff now that applies 1d8 Thunder to your damage roll and 2d8 Thunder if the enemy moves. Start pushing things, knife-ear!
Paladin: Builds and Multiclassing cont.
Lvl 6: Oath of Vengeance Paladin is our final level of Paladin and where all this 'risma finally becomes useful. You get Aura of Protection which adds your 'risma to your Saving Throws. All of them. You now have +4 to all of your Saving Throws without Bless. Yeah, seriously. It only disappears if you get knocked Unconscious but if that happens you're probably dead anyway. You can pick another spell too but once again, it really doesn't matter what you pick because you probably (definitely) won't use it. No extra spellslots here either. Don't worry, that's gonna change next level...

Lvl 7: Oath of Vengeance Paladin 6 Wizard 1 is where we start to build on the foundations of Smites and Aura of Protection.
You also get Arcane Recovery which is another lvl 1 spellslot with extra steps, as you can replenish spellslots outside of combat.

You can pick 3 Cantrips here and we recommend Booming Blade if you didn't pick High Elf. For those that didn't, you now basically want to use Booming Blade instead of attacking normally, as it also triggers Divine Smite, inflicts 1d8 extra Thunder Damage (as we're past lvl 5) and inflicts 2d8 Thunder damage when the target moves that turn. Yes, really. Well done if you chose Athletics over a dialogue Proficiency, as now you can shove stuff to trigger extra damage with your Bonus Action if you have it to spare and you have a better chance at landing those shoves. Your other two cantrips can be anything you like as Booming Blade is the real star of the show here, although there's something to be said for Minor Illusion being able to group things for CC or lure stragglers away to be Smote.

As for spells, Shield is mandatory to improve your survivability and as it is also an Abjuration spell, it will contribute to your Arcane Ward next level. Shield will drastically increase your defence but remember, it will eat into your Smites. The saving grace is if you're dead you can't Smite at all, so don't be scared to use Shield liberally, as it gets stronger next level.
Also remember you can memorise up to lvl 3 spell scrolls now (as you're technically a lvl 5 caster) so it really doesn't matter what spells you take vs what scrolls you use, so from here on out we'll recommend good spells for utility and CC but you don't have to take them or prioritise them other than Shield as you still want spell slots for Smites.

Lvl 8: Oath of Vengeance Paladin 6 Abjuration Wizard 2 choose the School of Abjuration: every time you really don't wanna take a hit, ping Shield as your Reaction and you get +5AC for the rest of the turn, automatically block all Magic Missile missiles (yep, try it!) and increase your Arcane Ward intensity by 1 which means 1 less damage next time you don't use Shield. It also procs when you cast Protection from Evil and Good, which you should be doing as you'll be in Act 2 by now. This might not seem like a lot but with our equipment guide, this will be adding to your inherent equipment bonuses that also nullify damage. Remember, you can also cast Shield multiple times in a fight to stack that bonus.

Lvl 9: Oath of Vengeance Paladin 6 Abjuration Wizard 3 is where we slowly but steadily begin to climb the steps of greatness.
You can take Shadow Blade but remember this is a one-handed weapon and we're specc'd for two-handers, plus if you're gonna do that respec and go full Bladesinger. Seriously. You don't need smites if you take Shadow Blade and we'll explain why in our Bladesinger section.

Lvl 10: Oath of Vengeance Paladin 6 Abjuration Wizard 4 is where our spellslots are finalised. You get another Cantrip and another spell here but same old same old, we don't really care about any of that. We want Booming Blade + Smites + Shield with Arcane Ward. That's all we care about.
As for your Feat, Savage Attacker is what you want here; coupled with your Great Weapon Fighting your damage dice are now being re-rolled if you get a 1 or a 2 and then Savage Attacker rolls again and picks the highest result. It does this not only for your weapon damage but your Smites too, so the odds of you getting low damage rolls have now become very very low. Your Savage Attacker rerolls aren't what you're stuck with either: if you roll a 6 and a 3 on your first rolls and then a 3 and an 8 on your second ones, SA picks the highest so you end up with 6+8. You basically have Advantage on your damage dice, with the ability to reroll 1s and 2s, which in flat numbers adds on average 30% more damage due to getting better rolls. We're just breaking the game at this stage. You can also get an ASI and stick your 'risma up to 20 which gives you +5 on your Aura of Protection but Savage Attacker is, in our opinion, way more useful.

Lvl 11: Oath of Vengeance Paladin 6 Abjuration Wizard 4 Fighter 1 has a choice integrated into it. We made our decision with Fighter as we want Action Surge but you could keep going with Wizard if you wanted to get those dope lvl 3 slots but realistically, Action Surge is going to be putting out more DPS for those big opening turns where stuff just needs to be focused down (we're looking at you Ansur, Sarevok and Orin). If you do choose to go for Wizard 6, Counterspell is mandatory (and is also an Abjuration spell). Protection from Energy is nice for Ansur if you don't have The Sparkswall or don't want to wear it for whatever reason but you can also get that in scroll form. There's also Elixirs of Universal Resistance at this point too.
Anyway, if you do choose to follow us and go Fighter, pick Defence as your second style for that +1 AC.

Lvl 12: Oath of Vengeance Paladin 6 Abjuration Wizard 4 Fighter 2 ends this build with a bit of a whimper rather than a bang, to be honest. Action Surge is here. Take another turn for free every short rest. You've earned it.

That's pretty much it! As we said, all of this is your choice and you can choose to take other things but this is what we did. It's worth noting we didn't have Booming Blade when we completed our run with this build, so if anything it should be easier for anyone attempting a Paladin Solo these days. We will also reiterate if you want to take Shadow Blade, respec and go full Bladesinger or go Bladesinger 10 Paladin 2, which is technically a Bladesinger build, which we'll cover further into the guide.

You get this by forging it in the Adamantine Forge (read the wiki for how to do this).

Bladesinger: Durge/Tav Race
For all Durge and Tav lovers, we'll list all of the races in the order we feel they suit the playstyle best. For Tav specifically, we recommend choosing Guild Artisan as your background, but it's up to you.

Half-Orc
Half-Orc is a melee race as it comes with the Savage Attacks Passive (not to be confused with Savage Attacker) which means you roll an extra Damage Dice on Crits. In English, say a normal roll is 1d6. Crit means 2d6. Half-Orc Crit means 3d6. Very tidy. Note we said "normal" damage dice. In Honour Mode, this does not include smites or sneak attacks. A Half-Orc wizard is not only hilariously going against the norm, it's also probably the best version of Bladesinger you can get since you get Booming Blade as one of your starting cantrips, nullifying the need to go High Elf. We did play as High Elf though cos you know, we like moving around a lot.
It also comes with a sweet ability called Relentless Endurance that improves your survivability drastically. When you die as a Half-Orc, once per Long Rest, you are insta-rezzed with 1 HP. Yeah, you read that right. We recommend playing like you don't have a safety net though, as this drastically increases your odds of never having to use it.
Note: This is more for saving you against deaths outside of combat, as in combat it is likely you simply survive one more hit than you normally would and will die anyway, depending on your turn order. It won't help against chasms or falls either. Still, not getting completely bodied and losing your run to a trap is nice.

Elf
Unless you're using a non-melee class or a class that doesn't get Booming Blade at lvl 1, go High Elf. Booming Blade is too good, as you will discover later. Fey Ancestry, Darkvision and Perception Proficiency are way more valuable in a Solo than in a party too, they just don't get Savage Attacks which is really good.

Half-Elf
Half-Elf is the same as Elf except it doesn't get the Perception Proficiency and instead gets Shield Proficiency. Your issue here is the same as full Elf in which the main reason this Race is now Top-Tier is nullified by the fact we're taking Wizard as a class.

Githyanki
Githyanki get Astral Knowledge, Mage Hand and free Enhanced Leap & Misty Step per Long Rest. The Mage Hand in particular can be used as a source of Wet, Haste Spores or just another target on the field that can throw stuff for you, which is pretty valuable when you only have one character. They don't get that extra movement distance or Proficiencies, though.
Additionally, while there is a lot of equipment that have special effects that only proc when you're a Gith, you can use the Mask of the Shapeshifter[bg3.wiki] or a Disguise Spell/Ability to proc them anyway. Make sure to equip the item after you transform or it won't register your bonus effects properly. The Soulbreaker Greatsword is a nice little weapon to have for a Paladin but genuinely, as MotSS keeps your shapeshifted after you take it off, choosing Half-Orc or Elf is just Gith with extra steps.

Drow
Basically a Wood Elf without the Stealth Proficiency and Movement Speed. They are also objectively cooler. Weapon Proficiencies don't matter as we'll be covering all of that with our class, and a free Faerie Fire (lvl 3) & Darkness (lvl 5) cast can come in handy but it's never going to be game-changing. You also don't have to Shapeshift in Act 1 at all, which is convenient but again, not game-changing.

Halfling
Halflings come with the Lucky & Brave features, which let you re-roll a 1 at will and give you Advantage on Fear checks, respectively. You can also choose between having Advantage on Stealth Checks or Advantage on Saving Throws against Poison and Resistance to Poison Damage. These are fairly handy features, but the drawback is you move 1.5m less than your long-legged counterparts and again, don't get relevant Proficiencies.

Dwarf
Dwarven Resilience, which is Advantage against being Poisoned and Resistance to Poison Damage. Gold Dwarf is a good shout for extra HP, especially in the early acts, and Duergar for the Invis and Advantage against Illusions, Charm and Paralysis is also good. Honestly, you don't need the Invis and the Advantage on Paralysis Saves is probably a waste; if you ever need to use it, you've made a mistake by allowing whatever it is to attempt to Paralyse you in the first place. The Enlarge cast is also good but situational and it only lasts for 10 turns. You also have less Movement Speed than the long-legged races.

Tiefling
Tieflings are cool but all they're really offering consistently is Resistance to Fire. The 3 subraces all have some spells that you might use but they either have Charisma or Constitution as their modifiers. Mephistopheles Tieflings would probably be our pick for Mage Hand, closely followed by Asmodeus for the Hellish Rebuke with Zariel coming in last with Thaumaturgy. As for their other spells: Produce Flame sucks, Darkness is fine, Flame Blade & Burning Hands most likely won't be used and Searing Smite is... well... you should know our opinions on this from our info on Karlach. We don't think it's worth it, to be honest.

Gnome
Advantage on Wisdom, Charisma and Intelligence Saving Throws. The Sub-Race bonuses are all meh here for Paladin too, as doubling your History Proficiency Bonus, Speak with Animals or Advantage on Stealth Checks are either pointless or situational. They have nice saving buffs but like Duergar, if you're utilising them it means you've played too risky or made a mistake, and you're also nerfed in movement. There's still better options above, truthfully.

Human
Shield Proficiency and an extra Proficiency, with extra Carry Weight. Meh. Next.

Dragonborn
Oh, this is what's next? Ouch...
Objectively the coolest-looking race and also objectively the worst to play as; you're nerfing yourself for zero reason as these guys literally have nothing going for them, not even Darkvision. Don't pick Dragonborn unless you plan on impressing your enemies to death with your sick horns and tail. Let us know how it goes in the comments.

Our run was done with a High Elf but as we have a class that offsets the Elven advantage of having Booming Blade from lvl 1, gives you the extra Booming Blade damage at Lvl 5 and the extra crit die, we believe Half-Orc to be the optimum and therefore "Best" pick for pretty much any melee-heavy character.

Bladesinger: Builds and Multiclassing
It's here and it's clear: if you don't like Bladesinger or don't find it to be laughably broken, you're doing it wrong. This is one of those times where we're not gonna budge on our viewpoint and we don't mean it as a bad thing, it's just a fact. Up there in the guide we say something along the lines of "Gloom Stalker isn't an instant win button" but Bladesinger actually is. Well, more accurately, there are three buttons you press to win but we'll tell you what those buttons are. Read on to unlock the full potential of the class and enjoy easy Solo runs.

Bladesinger Build Breakdown

We'll be going for Bladesinger 12 as we did a pure Bladesinger run as part of a challenge and in the process discovered it's broken. It has literally everything you need and more. Check the FAQ for more build exploration but it basically boils down to Bladesinger 10 Paladin 2 and you pretty much don't even need Paladin 2.

Lvl 1: Bladesinger Wizard Take Wizard as your starting class and grab Booming Blade, Minor Illusion and Toll the Dead.
As mentioned above, Booming Blade is a melee cantrip that causes opponents to take 1d8 Thunder Damage when they move. Kinda whatever. Just wait though, like Eldritch Blast, it gets better per character level, not class level. Still, you should always use this instead of your regular attack, as it is especially useful against Gobbo Archers and their tendency to Disengage and run away to shoot you as well as any enemy you can manage to Shove away from you after hitting it.
Minor Illusion is for Grym easy-mode and grouping things up or pulling away stragglers. Toll the Dead is a very good new cantrip in Patch 8 which is going to be your main source of Ranged damage without costing you a spellslot; it is a Wisdom Saving Throw that causes 1d8 Necrotic or 1d12 if the opponent has health missing.

As for spells, you wanna take Mage Armour, Shield, Magic Missile, Longstrider, Enhanced Leap, Feather Fall, Thunder Wave and Chromatic Orb. You wanna have Mage Armour, Shield, Longstrider and either Magic Missile or Thunder Wave for the Nautiloid. Get Mage Armour cast immediately which will put you at a respectable 16 AC at Lvl 1, then get yourself buffed with Longstrider.

You also get an Arcane Recovery Charge which means you get to restore a spell slot outside of combat; this effectively makes your Mage Armour a free cast when you compare other casters as you can use it after that to get your slot back and cast two offensive spells. Nice.

Choose Insight and Investigation as your Proficiencies, or whatever you like.

Lvl 2: Bladesinger Wizard is where you get to take two more spells and memorise another one. Grab whatever spells you want and then get Enhanced Leap added to your memorised list. Use this before combat to get around.
You also get to pick your subclass and we will be going for Bladesinger (shock). Pick your weapon: it doesn't matter as you now have Proficiency with all of them and next level you're gonna be using something way better anyway but you should be dual-wielding a combo of shortswords and scimitars at this stage. You also get Performance Proficiency which is cool. Kinda. Whatever. Moving on...

Bladesingers whole thing is activating Bladesong in fights. You start with 2 charges that increase as you level up. It's free to activate in combat but make sure you activate it at the start of combats you want to use it in because Bladesong lasts for 10 turns and while it's active, every time you cast a Spell you get a Bladesong Damage Charge and every time you make a melee attack you get a Bladesong Healing Charge. You can use Bladesong Climax as a Bonus Action which is a Jump (so Enhanced Leap increases the range of it!) which deals 1d6 Force damage to enemies in the radius and heals you (and allies but we're solo so redundant here) for 1d6. Bladesong Climax also cashes in all of your charges and for each charge the amount of Force damage you deal and the amount you are healed goes up by 1d6. You can, therefore, passively accumulate a Bonus Action heal that deals damage to enemies while you wail on them with melee weapons OR sling spells at stuff til you want to drop in with Force Damage AoE then start swingin'.
While Bladesong is active, you also get (are you ready?) +2 AC, +3m to Movement Speed, Advantage in Acrobatics and +2 to Constitution Saving Throws. It's such a powerful ability and it only gets stronger as you level up. In really spicy fights this early, popping Bladesong and a Haste Potion gives you 20 AC. 20. Utter Madness.

Lvl 3: Bladesinger Wizard brings us to the crux of the class and you're like, half online with this build at this point. Pick Magic Weapon and Shadow Blade as your two new Level 2 spells and remove your offensive spell from your bar which should be Magic Missile or Thunder Wave; your bar should now be Mage Armour, Shield, Longstrider, Enhanced Leap, Magic Weapon and Shadow Blade. You may also take Cloud of Daggers instead of anything else if you want to use it in combination with the many Grease scrolls and bottles you'll get in Act 1 as this combo is OP but whatever you choose, Shadowblade is mandatory because...

Shadow Blade is disgusting. You conjure a blade made of shadow (no way) into your hand that deals 2d8 Psychic Damage and has Advantage against Lightly or Heavily Obscured Targets. Outside and target is in the shade? Lightly Obscured, Advantage. The entire Grymforge (mostly)? Advantage. Act 2? Advantage. You're also using Strength Elixirs so you're operating at 21 Strength, which means you're already walking around with a good chance of hitting stuff on top of the Advantage.
Lets put Shadow Blade damage into perspective for you too: right now your most damaging melee options are Two-Handed and we'll use the ones we recommend for Paladin as examples. Sword of Justice/Svartlebees both do 2d6 +1 + Str so they're doing 8-18 Slashing damage. Everburn Blade is doing 2d6 + Str + 1d4 Fire, which is doing 8-17 Slashing + 1d4 Fire, so 8-21 total. Shadow Blade is doing 2d8 + Str so it's doing 7 - 21 Psychic Damage as a one-handed weapon. You can have a whole other weapon in your other hand while dealing more/equal damage than the three best melee weapons available to you right now.
Coupled with Booming Blade and your correct proc'cing of its effect, you're doing 3d8 + 5 to targets, of damage types that are rarely resisted throughout the entire game. 3d8 + 5. At Level 3. Do you understand how much damage that is? Yes you can of course use Booming Blade (and should be) as a Paladin wielding those Two-Handed weapons but remember, Shadow Blade is a One-Handed weapon doing the same damage. Dis-gust-ing. And this is level 3. It gets better as you level up! Speaking of levelling up...

Lvl 4: Bladesinger Wizard gives us our Feat, another level 2 spellslot and another cantrip. For Feats we took Alert for the bonus Initiative to make sure we we're going first in combat. We took Featherfall but Thunder Wave to get our Booming Blade procs ia a good shout if you have potions. For your cantrip grab Firebolt for the utility and another option of damage type vs Toll the Dead but it's really up to you. One more level to go and you come online properly...

Lvl 5: Bladesinger Wizard is where arrogance can creep in, because you get a Level 3 spellslot, another Bladesong charge and +3 to your AC + Con while using it. Your level 3 spellslot means upcasted Shadow Blade which is now doing 3d8 Psychic Damage. Your Booming Blade is now also doing 1d8 Thunder on hit and 2d8 Thunder if the target moves. It's getting silly already, because you're now doing up to 6d8 dmg to stuff when you proc Booming Blade properly, not including whatever you land on your offhand attack. Also worth remembering, this is without equipment. The Underdark should be coming up now too. Advantage anyone?
Bladesinger: Builds and Multiclassing cont.
Lvl 6: Bladesinger Wizard is where you catch up with the melee classes and gain your Extra Attack. On average, with Cull the Weak, stuff with =<40HP is dead in a turn before your Booming Blade movement damage procs. You also get yourself some more spellslots and some more spells. Counterspell is mandatory then you can grab Fireball, Hypnotic Pattern, Haste (if you're confident) Glyph of Warding, Lightning Bolt or Slow.
A note about Booming Blade here: you can only use it once per Action per round, so you can't use it with your Extra Attack if you used it on the first swing but you can if you opened up with a normal swing. Additionally, if you pop a Haste Potion (or cast it) you can use it, then normal attack, then use it again on your second Action. You can bypass this restriction in the first round of combat due to how combat works; as you make the first Booming Blade strike outside of combat, when combat starts it's a separate "round" so you can hit them with it twice. You should by now know to prioritise threats but definitely do this on scary things. Unfortunately, the Booming Blade condition is overridden and doesn't proc twice but still, you're gaining an extra 1d8 and later on 2d8 per opening round on some targets by remembering this and planning accordingly.

Lvl 7: Bladesinger Wizard means more spells and a 4th Level spell slot alongside another Bladesong charge. Don't use this to cast Shadow Blade as it has no effect on your damage. We recommend Wall of Fire or Ice Storm.

Lvl 8: Bladesinger Wizard contains your next Feat and more spellslots. For your Feat take Savage Attacker to get those rerolls on your damage; this effectively reduces the range of your damage with Shadowblade on average; while this is gnarly and could be taken earlier, Alert first means you don't have to worry about going first which is way more important. You could also take War Caster or an ASI to up your Int or your Dex, depending on whether you want more AC or more spells memorised. Worth mentioning at this point you'll be in Act 2 which means you'll have access to the Helm of Arcane Acuity and the Gloves of Battlemage's Power which will both give you Arcane Acuity, so your Int being high is now no longer a concern unless you want more memorised spells.

Lvl 9: Bladesinger Wizard is yet another jump in power as we get access to a 5th level spellslot which means Shadow Blade is now doing 4d8 and our Bladesong gives us +4 AC + Con. You also get to pick a spicy spell; Hold Monster, Cone of Cold and Cloudkill are our recommendations, with Cloudkill being the most efficient use of your spellslot but perhaps not your actions... up to you. You also get another Bladesong Power charge for a total of four casts per Long Rest.

Lvl 10: Bladesinger Wizard grants you another lvl 5 spellslot, an additional cantrip and Song of Defence, which allows you to spend spellslots to reduce incoming damage by 5 dmg per spellslot spent. Handy to have but not majorly game-breaking. Pick whatever cantrip you want and whatever spells you want.

Lvl 11: Bladesinger Wizard ups your Booming Blade to 2d8 Thunder on hit and 3d8 when the target moves and gives you more spell slots and your first lvl 6 spell.
You're now landing 9d8 when you properly proc Booming Blade. That's one less damage dice than Chain Lightning, one more and +2 better damage dice than Circle of Death, 3d8 more than Sunbeam and 12 damage more than Otiluke's Freezing Sphere from a one-handed attack. Let that sink in for a moment... 9d8. Absolutely rank levels of damage.
It's at this point where you can choose to grab Paladin for your final two levels but if you wanna go for your lvl 6 spell slots you absolutely can; it's really a matter of preference.

Lvl 12: Bladesinger Wizard if your final level and Feat. We took another ASI to put our Int to 20 to have another spell memorised but you can take whatever you'd like. War Caster, Spell Sniper or Lucky could be good calls but don't take Dual Wielder as you should, once you read our equipment section, already have or be going to get a certain weapon that makes this kind of redundant unless you want to wear armour and really want the +1 AC or don't want to use your Bonus Action for actually fighting with.

That's it! As is the case with all classes, stuff starts to taper off towards the back end of levels for most classes but extra d8's on your stuff is always nice. The equipment section will refine the raw power of this build into a flexible killing machine.

Bladesinger: Special Equipment
Reso-what?
We felt the need to write a whole section on this one thing that is the final piece of the Bladesinger puzzle. For those who know, yes you guessed it and for those that don't, we are about to change your Bladesinger experience.

If you thought you were doing good damage and controlling the battlefield with your movement and CC spells, we present to you the Resonance Stone.[bg3.wiki]

This thing gives everyone within 9m except Undead and Constructs the Steeped in Bliss condition, which gives the target Advantage on Strength, Constitution and Dexterity Ability Checks, Disadvantage on Intelligence, Wisdom and Charisma Saving Throws and Vulnerability to Psychic Damage. Here it is. The final piece of the Bladesinger puzzle.

With the Resonance Stone in your Inventory, everything within 9m of you is Vulnerable to Psychic Damage, which means they take double damage from your Shadow Blade. Quadruple on a crit, yes, correct, well done for remembering us explaining Bhaal Armour earlier, cos this thing is basically Bhaalist Armour for your Shadow Blade. Killer's Sweetheart just got so much more valuable to you right?

You'll also gain Advantage on things like Lockpicking, which is quite nice.

Where is it?
You find this most holy of artefacts in the Oubliette/Mindflayer Colony just before you fight Myrkul. It's near the Mind-Archive Interface (check the wiki link for better directions).

What's the catch?
Unfortunately, there are some downsides. Yes, you do give everything Advantage on Physical Ability Checks but whatever. The real, serious downside to this is you are also Vulnerable to Psychic damage, so Mindflayers just got extra scary to fight. Undead and Constructs are Immune to this altogether but hey, that's what your spells are for.

Also, we don't know what causes it but sometimes this thing just runs out of battery, loses signal, dies or otherwise ceases to function when you leave Act 2. If this happens it's not the end of the run but it's gonna add time to your fights and you'll probably want to overstock on spell scrolls to compensate. It's nothing a few Disintegrate scrolls can't remedy!

So... is it worth it?
You have to be extra-careful around anything that deals Psychic damage and you also need to remember you're not as powerful as you usually are against Undead and Constructs in melee if you don't send the stone back to camp when you're fighting against them (which we recommend you do). Those are your two biggest traps to fall into.

Once you get into Act 3 and Baldurs Gate proper, you can buy Elixirs of Universal Resistance from Vicar Humbletoes and a few other vendors which gives you Resistance to everything except Psychic as your vulnerability is cancelled out to regular damage numbers. They're also not a true Elixir so it won't replace your Cloud Giant Strength but remember they only last for 10 Turns. Other than Disadvantage on Mental Savings throws, it's now no longer scary taking the stone into the Brain but please be ultra-careful against Mindflayers until you have a stash of them and keep an eye on your remaining turns! Also, definitely don't do the Mind-Mapping from Viconia without using one of those Elixirs or you can get one-shotted and we aren't even exaggerating a little bit. If you do the House of Hope just walk in blasting rather than sitting on the bench because at this stage it isn't worth losing 35+ hours to an insistence on roleplaying.

We recommend sending it back to camp when you need to pass mental Saving Throws (the second half of the Necromancy of Thay comes to mind, for example) but this isn't the end of the world if you forget.

Our personal recommendation is to take this back to your chest in camp at least as you don't need to carry it with you all of the time. We guarantee there will be fights you can use this for free without worrying about the drawbacks; Ansur, Ethel 2, Raphael and more can now be pummelled into submission within 1 or 2 turns depending on your rolls and equipment. It is entirely up to you whether you utilise this and you can complete the game without it. We personally took it for pretty much the rest of the game but you make your own choice.

FAQ - General
What makes you guys qualified to tell me what to do?
Nothing, but we have almost 2k hours (9/5/25 - nearly 3K now; we do have lives, honest) between us and 90% of that time has been doing Honour Mode, and probably 70% of it has been doing solo runs. We don't know everything, but we're pretty clued in, if we do say so ourselves!

I keep missing my attacks, what's going on?
This is a dice game. Sometimes you can do everything right, stack the odds in your favour and still get quadruple 1s. That's just how it be.

Why are you saying it's up to me to choose things?
We want to give you the data, the reasoning and the strategies to then allow you to form your own way, to avoid being stuck in a rigid framework that has no variation. For some reason, lots of Solo runs look the same, with no fights until lvl 4 and running ahead to the Grymforge, etc. We hope that we can help you create new routes by showing you how to become powerful without cheese and encouraging you to play the game, so you're more confident to try new things. Plus, we don't like being told what to do so we won't tell you what to do. If it sounds like we are, well, maybe there's less wiggle-room in that decision but you can always choose not to take our advice and that's cool too. If you die because you didn't listen, though, that's on you.

Do you guys have any other guides?
Yes we do! We have a Solo Myrkul Guide (WIP) that covers Gloom Stalker, Monk, Paladin and Bladesinger! We also have the Solo Orin Guide (WIP) for the 4 classes too! We call this guide the Definitive one but we will most likely write the Boss Fight guides separately and link them here to avoid a monumental wall of text getting even more words added to it. Seriously, thank you for reading this far if you read the whole thing, we appreciate you.

You guys have a lot more info for Gloomstalker, why is that?
Gloomstalker is by far the most beginner-friendly Solo class which is why we paid a little more attention to it than the other classes. It's also widely circulated in YouTube Shorts and Twitch streams so we thought we'd give our version some spotlight, as most people think it's an instant-win class when it really isn't. When played correctly it crushes but there are some areas that you need to be ready for due to inherent weaknesses to the build, namely Myrkul and the Steel Watcher Foundry due to the Piercing Resistance and lack of other weapon options to overcome it.

Can I use these builds in a 4-man party?
Absolutely. These builds can solo the game so by having 4 of them in a party you're basically guaranteeing your win as long as you don't do anything stupid. Following our guides and building a 4-man party with all of the classes above means most things won't live through the first round of combat. FAQ - Gloom Stalker
For Gloom Stalker, why the Titanstring Bow? Isn't that just a +1 from Act 1?
It is the best bow in the game for this build at putting out raw damage, because it is one of the few things in Honour Mode that has Damage Riders As A Source. We invite you to check out this page on DRs[bg3.wiki] as there is already a lot of info available there that is explained better than we can explain it.

To sum it up, on average the Titanstring is doing +8 damage more than every other bow per shot, which adds up when you use Special Arrows as this +8 is applied to Acid and Fire Arrow elemental damage. Using those types of Special Arrows you're on an extra 16 damage per shot. Chunky.

You also get it as early as lvl 3 in Act 1 so even if you want to switch, it's still the most damaging option available to you with literally nothing that is situationally better until Act 2.

Are there any other bows I can use for Gloom Stalker?
Yes, as a matter of fact there are.

The Ne'er Misser[bg3.wiki] is a hand crossbow you buy from Roah Moonglow in Moonrise Towers. The reason you'd want to use it is the Force Damage due to it barely being resisted in the game and the fact it can be dual-wielded. The main argument we can see for the Ne'er Misser is for fighting the Shadow-Cursed Shambling Mound, Thisobald Thorm, Myrkul and the entirety of the Steel Foundry. Unfortunately, the main argument against using this is that the damage outside of your guaranteed crits is utter garbage. We had better luck using Scrolls + Arcane Acuity for the Shadowcursed Mound and Thisobald; we also had better damage brute-forcing the Titanstring with Arrows of Undead Slaying and Construct Slaying for Myrkul and the Steel Foundry. Give it a try by all means but this handy-C is not the miracle weapon the community sometimes makes it out to be.

Check out this guide for how we did Myrkul despite Titanstring/Piercing Damage being resisted.

You'll also encounter The Dead Shot[bg3.wiki] and Gontr Mael[bg3.wiki] in Act 3. They won't be putting out the same damage but they're worth a look. Anything else is unfortunately not worth swapping out the Titanstring over. Seriously, we spent a lot of time with a lot of bows and the Titanstring, for raw damage, wins every time.

Are there any other builds I can use for Gloom Stalker?
If you want to experiment with Gloom Stalker, you can switch Assassin to Thief to get two Bonus Actions and then run Dual Hand Crossbows. We recommend the Ne'er Misser above and the Hellfire Hand Crossbow[bg3.wiki], although any old +2 hand crossbow will do. Unfortunately this is less damage than Assassin.

We also mention the Not Gloom Stalker above which is Swords Bard 6 Assassin 3 Gloom 3 and that genuinely crushes. You could possibly swap out Gloom 3 for Fighter 2 War Cleric 1, Fighter 3 or go Rogue 4 Fighter 2 and grab +2 Dex, it's up to you. If anyone wants us to elaborate further on the differences between your last 3 level selections, let us know in the comments.

So what is the BEST build and the BEST weapon?
TLDR:
Titanstring is the best flat damage output when you utilise Strength Elixirs.
Dual Hand Crossbow builds will consistently out-damage Titanstring builds in Act 3 when you consistently land the extra Off-Hand Attack, otherwise they fluctuate. We prefer the Bonus Action being left to utility.
From most to least damage output overall, you have Bard/Assassin>Gloom/Assassin>Bard/Thief>Gloom/Thief. We prefer Bard/Assassin & Gloom/Assassin.

In our opinion Titanstring is the "best" bow for a Solo Run due to damage and utility, and the "best" progression path is the one we have laid out that switches to Swords Bard 6 Assassin 3 then either Gloom 3, Fighter 3 or War Cleric 1 Fighter 2, depending on how you want to play. In our run, we went Gloom 3 so can verify first-hand how much that build shreds.

Personally for us, the BEST is what we feel allows you to complete a Solo run with the most odds in your favour. The BEST is subjective, which ties in to us not outright saying "this is the best" unless it's objectively the best, when we say the best it's more "this is what we've found works, because we've done it before, multiple times."
We mention this earlier but predictability and familiarity are the cornerstones of a successful run. You have to put the odds in your favour everywhere, not just in the damage department.

After crunching numbers, Titanstring is the best bow in the game for damage. It is only surpassed in Act 3 by Dual Hand Crossbows if you take the Craterflesh Gloves and Dolor Amarus; the reason it overtakes is the extra off-hand attacks with those two items adding guaranteed damage on Critical Hits (2d6 + 7 or 14). This also assumes the numbers are perfect, which they aren't in practice; you won't get that Bonus Action shot every turn and that's where you overtake Titan Weapon. You kind of need to dual-wield them to overtake Titanstring too, as if you use a single hand crossbow you do less damage outright and don't have the Titan Weapon modifier. You could argue that you can use a Bloodlust Elixir to proc more attacks on average and that's fine, but we've already accounted for that in our theoretical numbers and the theory probably favours more Bloodlust procs than you'll realistically get. This is not to say it isn't better damage or bad in any way, but often we notice people specc'ing for damage and misunderstanding how a Solo works. If you rely on Bonus Actions to get damage out, you're limiting your utility for this build.

As for builds & classes, Swords Bard 6 Assassin 3 functions way better than Gloom 5 Assassin 3 because of Flourishes. We do switch to this in Act 3 but up there, we still give you the choice. Both builds will complete the game, at that point it's just a matter of preference/how easily you can adjust to Bard 6 Assassin 3 before Myrkul, if you choose to switch there. We also discovered that X/Assassin is way better than X/Thief for Dual Crossbows, as the damage you put out in the first round with the guaranteed crits is outplaying the extra bonus action. True, there is a point where your crit damage is outdone by your additional bonus action but it's usually a case of either "you probably wouldn't have needed those rounds if you opened with guaranteed crits" or "everything is dead by turn 4-5 anyway" so for us, we're always going for Assassin.

Also, Pure Monk rips face, so there's that.

Are there any other Monk builds?
Of course, but (yeah, banging this drum again) we are going for authenticity over theory-crafting and pure Monk was what we used.

TLDR:
Open Hand Monk 6 Rogue 4 Fighter 2
Open Hand Monk 8/9 Rogue 4/3
Shadow Monk 6 Rogue 4 Fighter 2
Shadow Monk 8/9 Rogue 4/3

Open Hand Monk is by far the gnarliest subclass in terms of damage due to the Flurry of Blows they get. You can, however, go for Shadow Monk and teleport around the battlefield battering things. Either way, both of these classes benefit greatly from a Rogue dip at either lvl 6 or lvl8/9 depending on whether you want your extra Feat or extra Ki Points.

You really wanna get to lvl 6 with both Monk subclasses because they both get Ki-Empowered Strikes, which means your fists are now magic jazz hands that deal Magical damage. As for individual subclass bonuses, OH gets Wholeness of Body (WoB) + a Manifestation and SM gets Shadow Step which is kind of the whole point of the subclass. Shadow Step is a cool teleport ability that gives you Advantage on your next melee attack after using it. The catch is that you must start and end your teleport in shadows. In game terms, this means you have to be Lightly or Heavily Obscured to use it. Apart from that this is basically Misty Step (with a caveat) that is a Class Action. That's really strong. The Advantage is nice but honestly, you're taking Tavern Brawler and using Strength Elixirs which means your chances to hit are gonna be majorly high anyway.

Your Rogue dip will be either Thief or Assassin, depending on what you want. Both subclasses will benefit from an additional Bonus Action but OH Wholeness of Body gives you a taster of this for free every Long Rest. Knowing this, Assassin might be your best bet for OH; you will vaporise most enemies in the first 3 turns of combat. Truthfully though, Pure Monk is just as deadly so this is now "how big can we get the number" rather than "this is mandatory to bring the build together".

Your Fighter dip will give you +1AC as Defence is the only Fighting Style relevant to you because you should be punching stuff and of course you'll get Action Surge, which is just objectively always good to have. You do need to pass off the Wisdom bonuses as you'll need to wear Armour to trigger Defence unless you're rocking Helldusk and we haven't tested that, so can't confirm if it still works (we think it will but no promises). Is +1AC worth losing all your Wisdom bonuses? We don't think so but you do you.

If you choose to stay in your Monk subclass until lvl 8 you're obviously losing your Action Surge and +1AC but you get Evasion and Stillness of Mind as well as more Ki points. Lvl 9 improves your Unarmed damage from 1d6 to 1d8 and gives you Advanced Unarmoured Movement.
Whether all this is as valuable as a Fighter dip or a Feat is up to you, but the principle above for Rogue remain the same. If you wanted to go these routes, we'd recommend dipping into Rogue at lvl 6 then returning to Monk once you get your subclass.

Once again, we'll remind you that this is the realm of theory-crafting as we haven't actually used these builds for a run, we just know they exist. There may well be a build we have missed so by all means let us know in the comments!
FAQ - Paladin
Are there any other builds I could use?
Yes. A few, actually.
TLDR:
Paladin 2/Swords Bard 10
Paladin 2/Sorcerer 10
Paladin 5 or 7/Warlock 5 or 7/Sorcerer 5 or 7
Paladin 12

We believe Swords Bard 10 Paladin 2 is the strongest iteration of Paladin because of how gnarly you are on the whole. You're basically a Bard that can Smite, which isn't really a Paladin build anymore however that doesn't mean it isn't really really good. You'd use Defensive Flourish most for that naughty +4 AC but there is something to be said for a Slashing Flourish followed by Smites.
As both classes share a Spellcasting Modifier and there are weapons that allow you to use it instead of Strength or Dex, you can get some nice Spell Save DC and melee damage downrange if you set yourself up right. Saying that, the reason you take Bard is for the spellslots to be used as Smites but a cheeky Hold Person or Glyph of Warding here or there wouldn't be the worst thing.

Sorcerer 10 Paladin 2 is more spellslots; a lot more when you consider you only need lvl 1s to Smite and you can use Sorcery Points to create them. The only downside to this one is you won't get an Extra Attack, but Haste exists and your Smites are basically an extra attack anyway. We'd recommend Draconic for an Elemental Resistance or Storm for that sick Bonus Action fly. Wild Magic is genuinely asking for trouble, in our experience.

Paladin 7/5 Warlock 5/7 used to be for the aforementioned Charisma synergy and Spellcasting Modifier/Pact Weapons, as there was a bug with Pact of the Blade giving you 2 Extra Attacks. This has been patched but you still get some spellslots that recharge on Short Rest, some Eldritch Invocations and Eldritch Blast. You can switch up between 5 or 7 levels of Warlock, depending on what you want, but you really want Paladin 5/Warlock 5 as a minimum to get your Extra Attack. We don't condone this but if you bind a Pact Weapon as a Warlock then switch to, say, Paladin 2 Bard 10, your Pact Weapon will still use your 'risma and will still be bound to you, so you can technically bypass Strength and Dex as your main damage stats for any Paladin build. This feels cheap to us, frankly, so we don't bother with it but it is an option.

Sorcerer instead of Warlock is more Spellslots with an Elemental Resistance or Call Lightning and movement after casting but let's be honest, you're doing it for the Spellslots. You don't get Hunger of Hadar, Eldritch Invocations or Short Rest Spellslots but Sorcery Points kind of make up for this, so it depends what flavour you want.

You could switch up your Wizard flavour too; we haven't tested Bladesinger 4 but we are willing to bet Shadow Blade is going to take your damage from "that's gnarly" to "how high can I get these numbers to go" and you wouldn't be sacrificing any spellslots, which is kinda the whole reason you want Wizard 4 in the first place. Our other recommendations would be Divination for re-rolls or Transmutation for the utility of more potions and poisons but honestly, it feels like it's a case of "why would you do that when Bladesinger or Abjuration exist". You'll probably want to follow our Bladesinger guide more than our Paladin one if you decide to go that route and you'd most likely end up going with more Bladesinger levels since Shadow Blade is unbelievably broken.

These builds all basically capitalise on Spellslots from other classes for More Smites. That's pretty much the gist of all Paladin builds. Pure Paladin is also a flavour but as we just mentioned, multiclassing for More Smites is usually the better option. Honestly, it really feels like unless you're gonna go for pure Paladin, roleplaying (which we don't care about around these parts) or try for Warlock synergy, you're better off just accepting that Paladin 2 on any melee build that can have it with lots of spellslots is the optimum way to use the class.

All that aside, the most "powerful" Paladin builds we've listed give you a choice of either forgoing your Extra Attack until lvl 8 or not using Smites until lvl 8. We would recommend the first option as your Smite can be considered your Extra Attack, so start as Paladin then at lvl 3 switch to your chosen multiclass option; you'll also gain Heavy Armour Proficiency this way too. For the Warlock builds, we recommend going Paladin first to get your Extra Attacks then you can switch up your levelling order as you please from there.
FAQ - Bladesinger
Are there any other builds I could use?
Yes but we've done some experimenting and frankly, full Bladesinger is perfectly fine. It's so broken it's actually not fun to do runs with because there's no real challenge. You have everything and with our equipment setup, you're unstoppable unless you really drop a ball and go into something woefully unprepared. Arrogance is the only thing that will kill you as a Bladesinger.

To get 5th Level spells you need to get to lvl 9 which doesn't leave much room for anything else and definitely locks you out of a Feat. The only contenders we feel are worth looking at would be Assassin, Fighter or Paladin. It's worth remembering that you won't get anything really valuable out of them until Act 3 and as always, for the last time, by then it's just adding stuff on top of your already concrete formula. The TL;DR is basically go Paladin, as having Smites to spend your lvl 1 & 2 spellslots on is nice. Assassin is probably next best, followed by Fighter. here's a more detailed breakdown of each:

You could stay Bladesinger 9 and grab Assassin, as Sneak Attacks would be a nice addition to your damage alongside Cunning Action: Dash for more movement but this will conflict with your Bladesong Climax and your Off-Hand attack. Minor, we know, however it's still something to consider. Guaranteed Crits, especially if you picked Half-Orc, is going to elevate your damage to foolish levels but as always, at the point you get it in the game it's just a numbers game and not necessary for finishing the game with.

Fighter is probably the "weakest" of the three options as it doesn't do anything to up your actual damage, just gives you more attacks with Action Surge. You can also choose to take the Defence Fighting Style (+1AC) or Two-Weapon Fighting. Defence only works if you wear a piece of Light Armour which will also nullify your Mage Armour but if you really want it to work, Boots of Persistence, Legacy of the Masters, Helm of Arcane Acuity, Helmet of Grit and the Elegant Studded Leather are all good options to make sure you get that extra AC. We'd recommend going for the Elegant Studded Leather though to counter the lack of your now-unusable Mage Armour. You can also use the Elven Chain but that starts to get a little messy with the bugs that allow Defence to be applied twice, Bracers of Defence working with them etc so we personally don't go for it. TWF is viable until you get Belm then it's redundant. Champion could be a nice subclass, again especially if you're Half-Orc and Battlemaster Manoeuvres can be nice additions to your bag of tricks but as always, at this point in the game you're probably stocked up on scrolls and gnarly spells so you may well end up not using them. We'd recommend Evasive Footwork, Menacing Attack and Riposte as your Manoeuvres, as always it's up to you.

We feel like Paladin is probably the go-to here and the "best" multiclass for Bladesinger since you'll get the Defence Fighting Style and Smites. Since you get your Divine Smite at Paladin 2, realistically you might as well go to Bladesinger 10 and get another lvl 5 slot alongside your Song of Defence, which allows you to spend spellslots to reduce incoming damage, to round out what is in our opinion, the most efficient Bladesinger build. Remember that respeccing and picking Paladin first for Heavy Armour Proficiencies here is pointless as you can't Sword Opera in Medium or Heavy Armour and the Elven Chain isn't classed as Armour so sidesteps this Profiency requirement anyway. As a final note, your Paladin subclass absolutely does not matter since what you get at lvls 1 and 2 are probably never going to be used.

If you insist on multiclassing we will leave you with a final point. Every level of multiclassing delays your upcasting of Shadow Blade, so you only really want to multiclass at lvl 9. Don't start as a Fighter, Paladin, or Rogue and try to game the system because that means you don't get your upcasted Shadow Blade until Act 3, whereas pure Bladesinger gets it just in time for Myrkul. Seriously, just go Bladesinger from the start.

Is Shadowblade good on other Classes?
Yes, but with caveats.

Warlocks with Pact of the Blade/Hexblades can use Shadow Blade but there's three problems.
The first one is Hexblades want to Bind their weapon for Hexblade's Curse and their ghosty friends when they hit lvl 6. This means that Strength from Elixirs is pointless as a Shadowbound weapon uses their spellcasting modifier. We highly recommend using other Elixirs and putting up with less damage overall if you're going to go this route. Bloodlust or Viciousness would be our picks.

A Warlock in a Solo run wants a Charisma ASI and Alert, which means you don't get Savage Attacker to reduce your damage range on Shadow Blade until lvl 12. You can manage your Initiative with some items but it's still not a guarantee, so realistically you want those items and Alert. You also may want an Assassin, Fighter or Paladin dip at lvls 6-8 to then continue your Warlock levels so you have an edge for Myrkul. Assassin was our pick but up to you; you won't have Savage Attacker by then with pure Warlock anyway.

There's also the issue of having one of your Warlock Spellslots being taken after every Long Rest and you're gonna want Armour of Agathys so that's an immediate Short Rest if you want to cast Spells in combat. Spoiler: You definitely do. It's not game-breaking and camp supplies are plentiful but it's a ball-ache. We have done this so we know first-hand; if you forget this and go into combat with no spellslots, you're usually going to have a bad time unless you're packing scrolls.

Sorcerer gets Shadow Blade too and doesn't need to Bind anything so Strength Elixirs are on the table again, but this pretty much locks you into Githyanki or Shield Dwarf as your Race so you're not relying on Light Armour; Sorcerers, alongside Wizards, get the least amount of HP per Con point + level ups. You don't want to be caught with low AC and those health pools, even with the Shield Spell. You're in danger of being one-shot by any crit for probably the entirety of the game unless you can get the Adamantine Armour and we don't want to piss feats up the wall when we're probably only gonna get two. There's also the threat of AoE spells.
It's a glass-cannon build for sure, plus honestly, if you're gonna do this, Bladesinger or Hexblades just do it better.

Eldritch Knights get Shadow Blade at lvl 8. It really doesn't seem worth it going for EK for SB, as the whole point of SB is you get it at lvl 3 and it's busted. Again, just go Bladesinger or Hexblade. Let's be real too, you choose EK to cross with Barbarian and throw stuff, not to get SB at lvl 8.

Arcane Trickster can also get SB at lvl 7 with their Spell Replacement but that means actually playing as an Arcane Trickster which is just such a bad idea on a Solo. Do so at your own risk.

Don't use the Shadow Blade Ring as it's locked to lvl 2 and therefore only does 2d8 damage. One of the great things about Shadow Blade is that when you get to lvl 5 and get lvl 3 Spellslots, it gets another d8 and the same when you reach level 9 and get lvl 5 Spellslots. Plus, by the time you get the ring, there's better melee options.

Do I have to use the Resonance Stone?
No but it's basically Bhaal Armour for free when you use Shadow Blade so why wouldn't you use it? Shadow Blade is still strong without it but nowhere near as strong, so it really depends on how powerful you want to be. Bear in mind most illithid abilities deal Pyschic Damage too, so all of those powers within melee range now also do double damage... which is countered by using Elixirs of Universal Resistance (our good friend Vicar Humbletoes is our go-to Resistance dealer but other NPCs can stock them). It feels like a no-brainer but if you really don't want to use it, by all means don't use it.
