This article is about the in-game attributes. You may be looking for Actions.

Abilities are representations of a creature's physical and mental attributes in Baldur's Gate 3. There are six abilities in Baldur's Gate 3, and every creature has a set of six ability scores – numerical values which represent how well they perform at each ability.

Each ability score translates directly into an ability score modifier – modifiers which function as bonuses or penalties that are added to most dice rolls in the game.

Overview[edit section | visual editor]

There are six abilities each of them represents an aspect of a creature's capabilities:

  • Strength
    physical power and might
  • Dexterity
    agility, coordination and balance
  • Constitution
    health and endurance
  • Intelligence
    rational reasoning and memory
  • Wisdom
    awareness and insight
  • Charisma
    influence and force of character

Ability scores are bought with points during character creation. The minimum ability score during character creation is 8, and the maximum is 15, before any of the ability score increases are applied.

Ability score modifiers[edit section | visual editor]

Each ability has an ability score modifier, derived from its ability score. Ability score modifiers are added to nearly all ability checks, saving throws, and attacks a creature makes associated with that ability, as well as to the Difficulty Class (DC) of any spells they cast.

Creatures also add an ability score modifier to some damage rolls they make. Some features allow creatures to add additional or alternative ability score modifiers to specific rolls. Creatures add their dexterity modifier to initiative rolls

An ability's modifier is decreased by 1 at every odd score below 10, and is increased by 1 at every even score above 10. Alternatively, it can be calculated by subtracting 10 from the ability score, then dividing by 2 and rounding down.[1]

Ability checks[edit section | visual editor]

This section is an excerpt from Dice rolls § Ability checks.[edit]

Ability checks are dice rolls made to determine whether a creature succeeds or fails at a task. They are rolled against the task's DC. The DC of these checks are generally predetermined by the game. Each ability check is made using one of the six abilities in the game, and creatures add an ability's corresponding ability score modifier to the results of ability checks they make.

Skills[edit section]

Ability checks are usually made using a specified skill. Skills are specific areas of expertise, each associated with an ability that characters can be proficient in.

Characters add their proficiency bonus to any ability checks they make using skills they are proficient in.[2]

Custom characters gain proficiency in two skills based on their chosen background during character creation, with origin characters having predetermined backgrounds. All characters can choose 2 more skills to be proficient in from a list of skills determined by their class (bards and rangers choose 3, while rogues choose 4).

Additionally, some races, subclasses, and feats also grant proficiency in specific skills, and bards receive the class feature  Jack of All Trades at level 2, allowing them to add half their proficiency bonus (rounded down) to ability checks they make using skills they are not proficient in.

Proficiency does not stack – there is no benefit to having multiple sources of proficiency for a skill.

Expertise[edit section]

Characters can also have expertise in a skill, which allows them to add double their proficiency bonus when making a corresponding ability check. While it is possible to have both proficiency and expertise in a skill, they do not stack. However, some sources of expertise do require the character to already be proficient in a skill.

Sources of expertise that require prior proficiency in the respective skill include:

  • Bards gain expertise in any two skills they are proficient in at both levels 3 and 10.
  • Rogues gain expertise in any two skills they are proficient in at both levels 1 and 6.

Sources of expertise that do not require prior proficiency in the respective skill include:

  • A Rock gnome's expertise in History
  • A Knowledge Domain Cleric's selection of expertise in two of the following skills at level 1: Arcana, History, Nature, or Religion
  • Expertise in Deception and Performance from the  Actor feat
  • Expertise in Deception, Intimidation, and Persuasion from the  Illithid Expertise feature

Types of ability checks[edit section]

Automatic rolls
Some ability checks are automatic. For example, when a creature approaches a trap, a Perception check is rolled to determine whether they notice it. Perception is a Wisdom skill, so the creature adds their Wisdom modifier and, if proficient in Perception, their proficiency bonus to the ability check.
During dialogue
Ability checks are also common during dialogue, where some responses require an ability check to determine the outcome. Examples include using Charisma skills like Persuasion, Deception, or Intimidation to influence others, or Intelligence skills like Arcana, History, or Religion to determine or remember facts. Before these checks, a button labelled "Add Bonus" appears along the bottom of the screen,[3] allowing the player character and other party members to use certain spells and / or items to grant bonuses to the check. This is also possible when making ability checks while interacting with objects, such as when disarming traps or picking locks.
Contests
A contest is a situation in which two creatures both make a roll, and the one with the better roll wins. One could also say that one creature makes a roll to determine the Difficulty Class for the other creature's roll. An example is attacking while hidden, in which case the target rolls Perception and the attacker rolls Stealth to contest it.
This may also involve a passive check instead of an active roll. For example, a hiding player character in the line of sight of a non-allied creature must make a Stealth check contesting the other creature's passive Perception score. Another example is attempting to  Shove or  Throw a creature, in which case the creature making the attempt rolls an Athletics check, which contests the other creature's passive Athletics or Acrobatics score (whichever is higher).

Frequency of checks[edit section]

Perception is among the most commonly rolled skills in the game, as it automatically triggers around traps, hidden objects, and ambushes.

Of the 3 skills that only appear in dialogue, Persuasion, Deception, and Intimidation, Persuasion is the most common (appearing in 293 dialogs), followed by Intimidation (appearing in 209 dialogs), and Deception (147 dialogs).[verify]

Of the two skills that are used in melee combat, Athletics and Acrobatics, Athletics is generally more useful, as Athletics is checked both when shoving, and being shoved – Acrobatics is only checked on being shoved. Athletics also has more checks outside of combat than Acrobatics (which only has 2 non-combats checks in the whole game).[verify]

Arcana is among the most common lore checks, and often enables options for using magical devices. Animal Handling, Medicine, and Performance rolls are less common, and Animal Handling does not appear as an option if  Speak with Animals is available.

Saving throws[edit section | visual editor]

This section is an excerpt from Dice rolls § Saving throws.[edit]

Saving throws represent a creature's attempt to "save" themself from harm. Spells and actions taken by other creatures frequently allow their targets to attempt a save, as do hazards like traps and surfaces. Each save has an associated ability and a save DC that creatures attempting to 'save' roll against. When attempting a save, a creature adds the ability score modifier of that save's associated ability; if they are proficient in saves made using that ability, they add their proficiency bonus as well.

While the result of an attempted saving throw is always binary – either a success or a failure – the exact outcome of a successful save depends on the effect in question. Typically, the damage or conditions inflicted by the associated effect will be reduced in severity, or sometimes negated entirely.

As stated above, saving throws do not automatically fail or succeed on natural 1s and natural 20s, except when made to maintain concentration or during dialogue.

A number of features affect saving throws, and some races have advantage on certain types of saves.

Save proficiency[edit section]

Each class grants proficiency in saving throws with two abilities. Though when multiclassing, only the first class taken grants saving throw proficiencies (unless they come from a separate feature, like  Iron Mind). An additional save proficiency can be gained by taking the Resilient feat.

Save DCs[edit section]

The Difficulty Class rolled against when attempting to save is called the save DC. A successful save can mean completely avoiding negative effects, reducing the damage received (usually by half), or both. For example, successfully saving against a spike trap could mean that a creature takes no damage because it successfully evades the spikes. On the other hand, if caught in the area of effect of a  Fireball spell, a successful save merely halves the damage. Saving against a  Thunderwave spell both halves the damage taken and prevents a creature from being pushed by the spell.

Different mechanics calculate save DC differently:

Spell save DC
The Difficulty Class of a spell that can be saved against is determined through the following formula:
8 + proficiency bonus + spellcasting ability modifier
Each class, except for fighter and rogue, has a spellcasting ability which is used for its class features (features that appear under a class's tab in the spellbook) and spells that call for a spellcasting ability modifier. Some non-class features also use this spellcasting ability, such as spells cast through items (including scrolls), free recasts of recastable spells (regardless of source), and illithid powers. A multiclassed character uses the spellcasting ability of their newest class (the class in which they most recently reached level 1) for non-class features. There are some class features which are bugged and will incorrectly use the newest class's spellcasting ability, as noted on those features' pages.
Fighter and rogue do not have a spellcasting ability properly defined, and so use the caster's default spellcasting ability. The default spellcasting ability is Intelligence for custom characters, as well as the Origin characters  Astarion,  The Dark Urge,  Gale,  Karlach, and  Lae'zel; Wisdom is used by  Halsin,  Jaheira,  Minsc,  Minthara, and  Shadowheart; and finally,  Wyll uses Charisma. For a monoclassed character, this works as expected, but if they are multiclassed and their most recently taken class was fighter or rogue, then the next most recent class's spellcasting ability will be used instead.
The Arcane Trickster and Eldritch Knight subclasses, however, do properly define Intelligence as their spellcasting ability, and so both of those subclasses will behave as expected on a multiclassed character. Even though the spellcasting ability for those subclasses is only defined after level 3, the newest class is still the most recent to reach level 1.

Certain conditions and equipment worn by the caster can also affect their spell save DC.
Spellcasting classes can check their spell save DC in the spellbook (shortcut "K"). A target's saving throws can be checked by examining them (shortcut "T").
Weapon action DC
Most weapons allow proficient users to perform special "weapon actions", which are typically limited to once per short rest (e.g.,  Backbreaker). These actions often include the chance to inflict a condition on the target, and these conditions require the target to attempt a save to avoid them. Each weapon action can grant its own inherent bonus to DC that is not listed anywhere, but is frequently +2. The Difficulty Class of saves against weapon actions is calculated as follows:
Weapon Action DC = 8 + proficiency bonus + Strength or Dexterity modifier (whichever is higher) + inherent weapon action bonus DC
Equipment other than weapons may have features that use weapon action DC. Some class features also use weapon action DC, notably most manoeuvres of a  Battle Master, and many  Monk abilities, such as  Stunning Strike.
Some unique weapons actions instead use spell save DC.
Hybrid save DC
Certain weapon actions, notably  Concussive Smash, instead allow the acting creature to either use their spell save DC or weapon action DC with a +2 bonus, whichever is higher.
Fixed Save DC
In the case of threats which do not originate from a spellcaster, such as a trap or poisonous apple, the game sets the DC based on how serious the threat is intended to be. For example, a slightly spoiled tart could impose a DC 5 Constitution save when eaten, whereas a potent snake venom could impose a DC 15 Constitution save on the victim. Many consumable items, such as elemental arrows, throwables, and coatings, use fixed save DCs.

Death saving throws[edit section]

Death saving throws are a special type of saving throw made by playable characters after they have been  Downed. Death saves are made once per turn while the character remains Downed. Death saves are always DC 10. If a Downed character receives damage from any source that is not a critical hit, they automatically fail one death saving throw. A critical hit against a Downed character results in 2 failed saves. Melee attacks against a Downed target are always classified as critical hits.

Playable characters die when three failures are accumulated or stabilise when three successes are accumulated, whichever happens first.

Death saving throws are not associated with an ability score and so do not get any ability score modifiers, nor do they benefit from a proficiency bonus. They only benefit from bonuses which apply to all saving throws (such as  Bless) or specifically to death saves (such as from the  Family Ring).

Interactions with classes[edit section | visual editor]

Each class has designated primary and secondary abilities. While these designations have no particular mechanical significance, they are mentioned in character creation, and serve as recommendations for players unfamiliar with the rules of the game. Classes do, however, frequently have features that rely on these abilities.

Overview of abilities and skills[edit section | visual editor]

Strength[edit section | visual editor]

Main article: Strength
“
Muscles and physical power. Affects your effectiveness with melee weapons. Also determines how far you can jump and how much you can carry.
„
— In-game ability description

Strength represents a creature's ability to exert physical force, and is the game's recommended primary ability for barbarians and fighters.

Attack and damage rolls
Creatures add their Strength modifier to the results of attack and damage rolls for most melee weapons.
Jump distance
The distance which a creature can jump is determined by their Strength score.
Carrying capacity
Carrying capacity uses a creature's Strength score to determine how much weight it can carry before becoming  Encumbered.
Throws
The maximum weight a creature can  Throw is determined by their Strength score.
Strength checks
Strength checks are made when physical might is required, such as when attempting to push over a heavy object or when attempting to force open locked doors or containers during dialogue.
Athletics
Athletics is used during attempts to  Shove other creatures, as well as when attempting to resist being shoved.

Strength saves[edit section | visual editor]

Strength saves are rare, and primarily called for when attempting to resist being knocked prone.

Dexterity[edit section | visual editor]

Main article: Dexterity
“
Agility, reflexes, and balance. Affects your effectiveness with ranged and Finesse weapons. Also affects your Initiative and Armour Class.
„
— In-game ability description

Dexterity represents a creature's agility and reflexes, and is the game's recommended primary ability for monks, rangers, and rogues.

Attack and damage rolls
Creatures add their Dexterity modifier to the results of attack and damage rolls of ranged weapons and, when the creature's Dexterity score is higher than their Strength score, melee weapons with the Finesse property.
Armour Class
Creatures also add their Dexterity modifier to their Armour Class (AC) – making them more difficult to hit. This bonus goes up to a maximum of +2 when wearing medium armour (excluding armour with the  Exotic Material feature), and is ignored when wearing heavy armour.
Initiative
Creatures add their Dexterity modifier when rolling for initiative, which determines the turn order during combat.
Dexterity checks
Dexterity checks are made when attempting to be nimble.
Acrobatics
Acrobatics is used to resist attempts at being shoved.
Sleight of Hand
Sleight of Hand is used when attempting to pick locks, disarm traps, pickpocket NPCs, or steal items without being noticed.
Stealth
Stealth is used to  Hide from other characters and when attempting to remain undetected with  Greater Invisibility.

Dexterity saves[edit section | visual editor]

Dexterity saves are commonly called for by spells and traps that deal area of effect damage, usually halving the damage of these effects.

Constitution[edit section | visual editor]

Main article: Constitution
“
Stamina and physical endurance. Affects your hit point maximum.
„
— In-game ability description

Constitution represents a creature's health and endurance.

Hit points
Creatures add their Constitution modifier to the amount of hit points they gain on level up. This increase is applied retroactively.
Constitution checks
Constitution checks are made when a creature's resilience is tested, but more commonly a Constitution save is attempted instead. Constitution checks are rare and have no associated skills.

Constitution saves[edit section | visual editor]

Constitution saves are made to maintain concentration on certain spells, and are used when saving against poisons or spells that test a target's endurance or resilience.

Intelligence[edit section | visual editor]

Main article: Intelligence
“
Memory and mental power. Improves spellcasting for wizards.
„
— In-game ability description

Intelligence represents a creature's recall, as well as their ability to reason and think quickly. It is the game's recommended primary ability for wizards, and serves as the spellcasting ability of wizards, Eldritch Knights, and Arcane Tricksters.

Intelligence checks
Intelligence checks are used when a creature attempts to apply logic or reason, or when attempting to remember something specific.
Arcana
Arcana is used when attempting to remember facts about the Weave, the source of magic and spells.
History
History is used when attempting to remember specific historical dates or events.
Investigation
Investigation is used by creatures to discover unusual things in their surroundings, and is used during attempts at deduction during dialogue.
Nature
Nature is used when attempting to remember facts about nature.
Religion
Religion is used when attempting to remember facts about deities and the planes of existence.

Intelligence saves[edit section | visual editor]

Intelligence saves are used against psionic spells and features, such as those used by illithids.

Wisdom[edit section | visual editor]

Main article: Wisdom
“
Senses and intuition. Improves spellcasting for clerics, druids, and rangers.
„
— In-game ability description

Wisdom represents a creature's intuition and awareness of their surroundings. It is the game's recommended primary ability for clerics and druids, and serves as the spellcasting ability of clerics, druids, monks, and rangers. Several of a monk's class features benefit from Wisdom.

Wisdom checks
Wisdom checks are attempts to discern situations and read between the lines.
Animal Handling
Animal Handling is used when interacting with animals.
Insight
Insight is used to read situations and when attempting to get a greater understanding of someone's intentions.
Medicine
Medicine is used during dialogue to heal and aid others, as well as by Transmutation School wizards to craft extra materials with the  Experimental Alchemy class feature.
Perception
Perception is used when a creature attempts to discover hidden or hard to find things in their surroundings, such as hidden containers, mechanisms, or traps, as well as when attempting to spot hidden creatures. During dialogue, it is used to notice hard to spot details.
Survival
Survival is used when attempting to spot hidden treasure, as well as during dialogues when dealing with anything related to experience with animals or the wilderness.

Wisdom saves[edit section | visual editor]

Wisdom saves are some of the most common in the game, frequently made to resist spells that test a creature's willpower, composure, or self-control.

Charisma[edit section | visual editor]

Main article: Charisma
“
Force of personality. Improves spellcasting for bards, paladins, sorcerers, and warlocks. Influences traders' prices.
„
— In-game ability description

Charisma represents a creature's ability to exert their will when interacting with others, and is the game's recommended primary ability for bards, sorcerers, warlocks and paladins. It is the spellcasting ability of barbarians, bards, paladins, sorcerers, and warlocks.

Charisma checks
Charisma checks are attempts at influencing others during dialogue.
Deception
Deception is used when attempting to deceive or trick others.
Intimidation
Intimidation is used when attempting to frighten or coerce others.
Performance
Proficiency in Performance allows characters to play instruments, and the skill is used when attempting to entertain others.
Persuasion
Persuasion is used when attempting to negotiate with or convince others. It affects the price that traders give a character when buying or selling items.

Charisma saves[edit section | visual editor]

Charisma saves are rare, generally found only on effects which inflict the  Banished condition or given as class-based alternatives to sorcerers, warlocks, or bards on Wisdom or Intelligence saves found in dialogue, such as when reading the  Necromancy of Thay.

Improving or reducing ability scores[edit section | visual editor]

There are multiple ways of improving or reducing ability scores, either permanently or temporarily.

Permanently[edit section | visual editor]

Ability scores can be permanently improved with the Ability Improvement feat, though this cannot raise any score above 20.

There are also several ways of permanently improving ability scores through quests and interactions, which can raise ability scores over 20.[6]

Temporarily[edit section | visual editor]

Ability scores may be temporarily increased above 20 through various enhancements, such as magical spells, elixirs, or special equipment.[6]

Ability score reduction[edit section | visual editor]

Some supernatural or magical effects (like Devour Intellect) can temporarily drain abilities, reducing one or more of the target's ability scores, potentially to 0. If any score is reduced to 0, the target dies instantly.

Priority[edit section | visual editor]

Ability score bonuses are applied in a three-tier hierarchical order, which can occasionally lead to unexpected interactions between competing modifiers.

The first tier of bonuses includes the Ability Improvement feat, which provides 2 ability points, and specific feats:  Athlete,  Lightly Armoured,  Moderately Armoured,  Performer,  Tavern Brawler, and  Weapon Master, which provide 1 ability point. Because these are internally categorised as Ability Improvements, they are identified in the ability score tooltip as bonuses "from Class."

These primary bonuses are subject to a hard cap of 20. When selecting one of these feats, the user interface accounts for all other feats selected during levelling, but excludes external modifiers from equipment or permanent world bonuses; if all relevant scores have already reached 20 through other feats, the game prevents the selection of these specific "Ability Improvement" feats.

Following Ability Improvements, the system adds Uncapped Bonuses. This second tier of modifiers applies regardless of the current ability score. They have no maximum value indicated in their description or tooltip, and are added to the base score before capped bonuses are considered. The only bonuses in the game which seem to act this way are:

  •  Actor
  •  Devilfoil Masks ( Infernal Legion)
  •  Nimblefinger Gloves ( Nimblefinger)
  •  Duke Ravengard's Longsword
  •  Auntie Ethel's Hair
  •  Potion of Everlasting Vigour
  •  Broken Promises (temporary)
  •  Dark Lady's Awareness (temporary)
  •  Dark Lady's Erudition (temporary)
  •  Dark Lady's Grace (temporary)
  •  Spirit of the Land

Because second-tier values (and first-tier feats) are calculated early in the stack, they provide a foundational increase that later capped bonuses must account for.

The third tier comprises Capped Bonuses, which represent the majority of modifiers in the game. It includes all other feats, such as  Resilient: Intelligence and  Heavily Armoured, and items like  The Mighty Cloth and  Khalid's Gift. These bonuses only apply if the current total remains below the specific cap assigned to the bonus – typically 20, though  Birthright is an exception with its cap of 22. If a bonus provides more than a +1 increase, it may partially apply until the score hits the cap. These are applied in ascending order of their caps to maximise the final value, though existing uncapped bonuses can sometimes result in a capped bonus being ignored if the threshold has already been crossed.

The bonuses granted by the Mirror of Loss (including the +1 Charisma from Patriar's Memory) possess the highest functional cap in the game at 24, and are therefore calculated after all other modifiers. This hierarchy establishes 24 as the standard maximum for most ability scores unless a fixed-value modifier, such as the  Elixir of Cloud Giant Strength, temporarily overrides the calculation.

However, it is possible for some ability scores to exceed 24 if the final total is derived exclusively from uncapped bonuses without the interference of capped modifiers. Strength can reach 26 early into Act Two with the Potion of Everlasting Vigour (or 28 temporarily with Broken Promises), and it can even reach 29 in the High Hall with Spirit of the Land. Charisma can reach 25 in High Hall, but it can actually reach 27 for one long rest in Act Two via Dark Lady's Grace, which can be obtained in the Sharran Sanctuary. Similarly, Intelligence and Wisdom may temporarily hit 26 using the buffs from the Sharran Sanctuary.
